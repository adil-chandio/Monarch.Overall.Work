# 3. Revenue model — use Studio numbers, not fantasy

YouTube RPM is revenue per 1,000 views after revenue share and includes all views, not only views with ads. Long-form and Shorts economics must be measured separately.

## Formula

```text
Long-form revenue = long-form views ÷ 1,000 × actual long-form RPM
Shorts revenue    = Shorts engaged views ÷ 1,000 × actual Shorts RPM
Total revenue     = long-form + Shorts + other YouTube revenue sources
```

## Illustration only — not a Monarch Cam forecast

If a channel's actual long-form RPM were **$2**:

| New long-form views | Arithmetic revenue |
|---:|---:|
| 25,000 | $50 |
| 100,000 | $200 |
| 250,000 | $500 |
| 1,000,000 | $2,000 |

Monarch Cam's actual RPM may be lower or higher. It must come from **Studio → Analytics → Revenue**, not from an internet niche average.

## Required-views formula for a monthly target

```text
Required long-form views = target revenue ÷ actual long-form RPM × 1,000
```

Example only: a $300 target at a measured $2 RPM requires 150,000 views. This is arithmetic, not a prediction that those views will arrive.

## First 48-hour revenue checks

For the last 28 days and previous month, capture:

- estimated revenue;
- long-form RPM;
- Shorts RPM;
- estimated monetized playbacks;
- revenue by video;
- revenue by geography;
- limited/no-ads icons;
- active copyright claims and revenue-sharing restrictions;
- original bear video's current revenue and RPM.

Only then set a dollar target for December.

- camouflage;
- visual puzzles;
- unusual adaptations;

Do not run funny pets, instant karma, trains, aviation, horror and ocean fear between these uploads.

## The viral-quality chain

```text
High-demand concept
  → instantly readable real thumbnail
  → accurate standalone title
  → proof in the first 3 seconds
  → payoff by 0:25
  → fresh evidence/explanation throughout
  → satisfying final answer
  → highly related next video
```

Hashtags cannot compensate for a weak link in this chain.

# 6. Long 1 — detailed production blueprint

## Title candidates for native YouTube A/B testing

1. `This Bear Learned to Open Car Doors — Here’s Why`
2. `The Bear Didn’t Break In — He Used the Handle`
3. `Why Bears Keep Opening Car Doors`

All footage and claims must support the selected title.

## Thumbnail

- Real, cleared frame of a bear at a car handle.
- Paw + handle + face readable at small size.
- Optional two-word text: `HE LEARNED`.
- No fake biting/attack, no multi-panel collage, no tiny labels.
- Do not use a realistic synthetic bear event as though it actually happened.

## Target runtime

**8–9 minutes only if four or five real examples and the explanation justify it.** Otherwise ship the strongest shorter cut. Do not pad to reach an ad threshold.

## Opening script architecture

### 0:00–0:03 — visual proof

> “Watch his paw. This bear isn’t forcing the door—he’s using the handle.”

Show the action before any branding or explanation.

### 0:03–0:10 — exact question

> “How does a wild bear learn that this small handle can lead to food?”

### 0:10–0:25 — first sourced payoff

> “Colorado Parks and Wildlife says bears that learn cars and homes can contain food may repeat the behaviour—even when no food is currently inside.”

Verify the final wording against the original source and footage.

### 0:25–0:35 — episode map without filler

> “These three cameras show how that lesson starts, why it repeats, and what drivers accidentally teach bears next.”

Only promise three examples if the final edit contains three cleared examples.

## Body

1. **Incident:** what the camera proves and what remains unknown.
2. **Mechanism:** food conditioning and learned routines.
3. **Second example:** a different piece of evidence, not the same point repeated.
4. **Why repetition continues:** scent, memory, reduced fear and human behaviour, each sourced.
5. **Safety/context:** secure food, lock vehicles/homes and keep distance; use wildlife-authority guidance.
6. **Final payoff:** return to the opening animal and answer the title question clearly.
7. **Next-video handoff:**

> “The next camera shows what happens when the same lesson brings a bear back to a house.”

The next line must match the actual next episode and its content.

## Editing standard

- 16:9 long-form master.
- Real footage remains the hero.
- Natural raw camera/reaction audio where useful.
- Large TV-safe typography; no cluttered arrows or scorecards.
- Use maps, simple diagrams or labels only when they add information.
- New visual or information beat roughly every 20–40 seconds, without mechanical zoom spam.
- Natural, mature English narration; no “silent death,” “pure chaos energy” or “stay locked in.”
- Original commentary throughout—not merely an intro/outro over someone else's clips.

# 10. Monetisation without killing trust or reach

## Before every upload

- Ads and YouTube Premium monetisation checked where eligible.
- No active unknown copyright/reused-content issue.
- Thumbnail/title and footage are non-graphic and accurately framed.
- No animal-harm spectacle or collision compilation.
- Realistic altered/synthetic content disclosed when required.
- Music and stock licences recorded.
- Mid-roll settings checked for videos **8 minutes or longer**.

For eligible long videos, use automatic mid-roll slots and/or natural manual breakpoints. Do not insert an ad in the middle of the title payoff or a sentence. Do not stretch a weak video to eight minutes just for ads.

Official mid-roll guidance: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6175006?hl=en

## Advertiser-friendly editorial direction

Prefer:

- animal learning;
- close encounters without graphic injury;
- behaviour and food conditioning;
- visual discovery/camouflage;
- prevention and safety context.

Avoid:

- graphic collisions;
- repeated animal suffering;
- fake attacks;
- gore/shock thumbnails;
- sensational framing that the footage does not support.

Official advertiser-friendly guidance: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en

## AI rule

AI may assist with outlines, research organisation, non-realistic diagrams, captions and production. It must not fabricate a wildlife event and present it as real evidence. Realistic altered/generated material requires disclosure where applicable.

Official AI-disclosure guidance: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en

# 11. Rights and originality gate

**Owner production preference recorded 5 November 2026:** do not run a direct permission-request outreach workflow. The default footage pool is therefore material that already permits the intended use—verified public-domain or suitably licensed Creative Commons/stock material—plus Monarch Cam's original graphics, narration and other production. Arbitrary copyrighted clips are not treated as cleared merely because they have been edited. Fair use is a fact-specific legal doctrine, not a blanket production licence or a guaranteed Content ID result.

For the first bear episode, use the verified NPS public-domain source pack in `MONARCH_CAM_PERMISSION_FREE_BEAR_FOOTAGE_PLAN_2026-11.md`. The CPW clip is a factual research lead, not the default commercial-edit footage source: CPW's published media licence is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 and expressly restricts commercial use and modification.

Every new episode needs a clip ledger before it enters the edit:

| Field | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Clip ID | Unique internal identifier |
| Original URL | Original source, preferably not a re-upload |
| Rights basis | Public domain, CC licence, stock licence or original asset |
| Usage evidence | Exact public-domain declaration or licence terms covering commercial use and editing |
| Date verified | Actual verification date |
| Credit | Exact required wording, even when attribution is optional but recommended |
| Editorial addition | Narration, explanation, graphics and story role |
| Backup | Source file plus archived rights/declaration evidence |

**Credit alone does not clear an arbitrary copyrighted clip. “Section 107” alone is not blanket permission. Even a legally usable clip still has to satisfy YouTube's separate reused-content/originality requirement. Crop, mirror, speed, filters and disclaimers do not create rights or guarantee monetisation.**

Each long video must add:

- original narration;
- a clear editorial question;
- sourced behavioural context;
- substantive story structure;
- meaningful visual explanation;
- a distinct Monarch Cam point of view.

Official channel monetisation policy: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en

# 12. Production rhythm for every six-day cycle

## Day 1 — concept, demand and proof

- Validate the title's central question using `yt_intel` research and direct source review.
- Collect the strongest real footage.
- Start rights verification before scripting around the clips.
- Draft three honest title/thumbnail concepts.

## Day 2 — research and script

- Build a source sheet for every factual claim.
- Write the cold open last, around the strongest cleared proof.
- Remove generic hype and repeated transitions.
- Read narration aloud for natural English.

## Day 3 — voice and rough assembly

- Record natural, mature narration.
- Preserve useful raw camera audio.
- Assemble story order before visual polish.
- Check that the title payoff is not underweighted.

## Day 4 — edit, sound and QC

- Finish visual explanation and pacing.
- Run audio QC: loudness, true peak, clipping, clicks, mono compatibility and dynamics.
- Review phone and TV readability.
- Review every factual and rights receipt.

## Day 5 — packaging and release

- Final title/thumbnail selection or native A/B test.
- Description with sources and a related next destination.
- Monetisation, ad-suitability, AI-disclosure and copyright checks.
- End-screen video + playlist configured.

## Day 6 — Shorts and learning

- Publish/schedule the two connected Shorts.
- Update old-winner and related-video bridges where appropriate.
- Record initial metrics without declaring success/failure in the first minutes.

# 13. Analytics decision rules

Review at **24 hours, 72 hours and 7 days**, and compare equivalent windows—not a new upload's first week against a two-year-old lifetime total.

| Signal | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Low impressions + strong retention | Distribution/audience-fit test did not scale | Check restrictions, strengthen old-winner bridge, publish the next adjacent episode |
| High impressions + weak CTR | Package not earning the click | Test a clearer title/thumbnail; change one variable at a time |
| Good CTR + sharp 0:30 loss | Opening or title promise mismatch | Rebuild the next opening; move proof/payoff earlier |
| Good retention + weak end-screen clicks | Weak next-story bridge | Improve verbal handoff, end-screen fit and playlist order |
| High Shorts feed impressions + high swipes | First frame/first seconds failing | Rework the opening action and context |
| Views + revenue but weak RPM | Revenue/geography/monetized-playback issue, not automatically a content failure | Inspect Revenue tab, geographies, ad suitability and format mix |
| Views but no returning-viewer growth | Isolated topic, weak channel promise | Strengthen series consistency and next-video value |

## Internal quality targets

The old audited benchmarks reached approximately:

- **60–67% at 0:30**;
- **28–38.5% average viewed**.

Aim to beat the channel's own historical **67% at 0:30** and **38.5% average viewed** over time. These are internal improvement targets, not YouTube algorithm guarantees.

For Shorts, establish the current viewed-vs-swiped and average-percentage-viewed baseline before assigning a numeric target.
