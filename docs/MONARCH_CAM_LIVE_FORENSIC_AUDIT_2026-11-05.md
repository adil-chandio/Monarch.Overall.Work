# 1. Executive verdict

## Monarch Cam is not completely dead. Its new-upload system is stalled and incoherent.

The strongest evidence is:

1. The original 2024 bear video is still growing: **952,975** in the supplied 3 October report to **959,920** live on 5 November — **+6,945 public views in 33 days**, roughly 210/day across that interval.
2. The newest long video has only **25 views and 0 public likes after 39 days**.

5. The public brand is contradictory: the channel is called **Monarch Cam**, the About page says **KARMA CAM**, and the January 2026 bear video calls itself **Daily Dose of Smiles**.
6. The latest long video abandons the channel’s strongest live promise—real wildlife caught too close—and instead offers a generic, AI-labelled bear ranking with inconsistent criteria, delayed payoff and multiple credibility red flags.

### The correct response

**Do not delete the channel. Do not mass-delete old videos. Do not keep re-uploading the latest video. Do not start another random niche experiment.**

Rebuild around two organised wildlife promises:

- **Primary:** `Too Close: Wildlife in Human Space`
- **Secondary:** `Nature’s Hidden Tricks`

Publish a coherent run of adjacent, standalone long videos and connect the still-active bear asset to that new run.

# 6. Microscope: latest long video

## Metadata

- **Title:** `I Was WRONG About Bears — #1 Will TERRIFY You`
- **Published:** 27 September 2026
- **Runtime:** 9:40 / 580 seconds
- **Public performance at audit:** 25 views, 0 likes
- **Category:** Pets & Animals
- **Public production label:** `Made with AI` — sounds or visuals were altered or fully generated

The AI label itself is **not proof of reduced distribution**. The creative quality, originality, factual reliability and viewer response must be evaluated separately.

## 6.1 Title/audience mismatch

### What the historical winner promises

A real person, a real bear and an immediate close encounter.

### What the new upload promises

An unidentified narrator’s personal correction plus a delayed ranking reveal:

- `I Was WRONG`
- `#1 Will TERRIFY You`

Problems:

1. There is no established on-camera personality whose previous belief creates stakes for the audience.
2. “Bears” is broad; the title gives no real place, camera, encounter or behavioural question.
3. “#1 Will TERRIFY You” is interchangeable click language rather than a specific Monarch Cam promise.
4. The content is an opinion-led ranking, not the proven `wildlife too close to humans` format.
5. Grizzly as #1 is not inherently a surprising payoff without a rigorous, clearly defined ranking system.

This is a **format and promise departure**, not simply a bad title.

## 6.2 Opening failure

The transcript begins with generic countdown language:

- “Eight bears enter one brutal ranking tonight.”
- “Weakest goes first, strongest saved for last.”
- “Science says number one is not polar.”
- “Stay locked in, the countdown starts now.”

The first species starts at approximately **0:28**.

Compared with the blueprint’s proof-first standard, this opening spends its most valuable seconds explaining the countdown and advertising intensity rather than supplying a remarkable visual/factual payoff.

## 6.3 The edit underweights its own title promise

Using the published chapters:

| Segment | Approximate time | Share of total runtime |
|---|---:|---:|
| #8 Panda | 0:28–2:16 = **108 seconds** | **18.6%** |
| #1 Grizzly | 8:45–9:20 = **35 seconds** | **6.0%** |

The title sells `#1 Will TERRIFY You`, but:

- the viewer must wait **8:45** for #1;
- #1 receives only about **35 seconds** before the closing section;
- #8 receives roughly three times as much runtime as #1.

That is a concrete structural payoff problem. Even a viewer who clicks for the title’s main promise reaches it late and receives a short treatment.

## 6.4 The ranking has no stable scientific criterion

The narration says “science says” and “weakest to strongest,” but the basis changes:

- Panda is penalised for diet and supposed lack of weapons.
- Spectacled bear is penalised for lacking “apex status.”
- Sloth bear rises on aggression/tiger anecdotes.
- Black bear is praised for adaptability rather than raw dominance.
- Polar bear loses #1 partly because melting ice shrinks its habitat.
- Polar bear also loses because grizzlies may displace it at carcasses and walking away is called “not alpha behaviour.”
- Grizzly wins through generalism, speed, claws, smell and attitude.

**Strength, danger to humans, adaptability, conservation status and one-on-one dominance are different rankings.** Mixing them makes the conclusion editorial rather than scientific.

## 6.5 Factual and credibility red flags

These examples require correction or reliable sourcing; this report does not treat every unsourced number as automatically false.

| Script/description claim | Forensic issue |
|---|---|
| Panda has “no real weapons” / is “built for cuddles only” | Misleading and unsafe minimisation. Smithsonian’s National Zoo describes powerful jaws/strong teeth and says giant pandas can be as dangerous as any other bear. |
| “Science says number one is not polar” | No scientific source or stable ranking definition is supplied. |
| Grizzly has a “2-ton bite” | Ambiguous force unit and no source. It is also presented alongside polar-bear PSI, which is not a like-for-like comparison. |
| Sloth bear “fights tigers and wins” | Overgeneralised from encounters that can have different contexts and outcomes. |
| Polar bear is not #1 because walking away is “not alpha behaviour” | “Alpha behaviour” is not a valid cross-species strength/scientific ranking criterion here. |
| Black bear is “800,000 strong and still growing” | Needs geography, date and a reliable population source. |
| “18 mile nose,” “20 mi through ice,” “flips 1,000-lb rocks” | Each needs a precise, credible source and careful wording rather than being stacked as hype. |

### Why this matters for growth

For an older, TV-heavy wildlife audience, trustworthy observation is a better long-term differentiator than escalating adjectives. Unsupported claims weaken returning-viewer trust and make the channel feel interchangeable with generic automated listicles.

## 6.6 Tone is the opposite of the intended premium brand

The transcript repeatedly uses phrases such as:

- “pure chaos energy”
- “nightmare attitude”
- “sloth bear war zone”
- “fights like demons”
- “white silent death”
- “no mercy”
- “pure wild attitude”
- “monsters built for war”

The 3 October blueprint—published six days *after* this video—later warned against exactly this style. The video is therefore a useful example of the older problem, not evidence that the newer blueprint has already been properly tested.

## 6.7 Description issues

- Repeats the same hype and unsourced numerical claims.
- Contains no meaningful scientific source list.
- Promises a next-week Lion vs Tiger upload that did not materialise.
- Uses broad hashtags that cannot repair a weak premise or opening.

## Latest-long conclusion

The 25-view result should not be “fixed” by simply adding more hashtags or re-uploading. The concept, title promise, opening, ranking logic, factual standard and runtime allocation all need work. A title/thumbnail test cannot repair the script’s underlying payoff and credibility problems.

## What is strategically better

[At 3 AM, He Opens The Door Anyway](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/l7N1U4wWyn8) is much closer to the correct direction than the latest long:

- one specific bear event;
- a concrete action: opening a door;

- a behavioural explanation about learned food conditioning;
- relevance to `Too Close: Wildlife in Human Space`.

The [original CPW thread](https://x.com/CPW_SE/status/1665762810909847552) says bears can learn that cars and homes hold food rewards, learn to open doors/windows and repeat the behaviour even when no food is currently inside.

## Remaining problems

1. **Only 3 public views / 0 likes at audit.** This is a real distribution/performance failure, but public data cannot say whether the Short was barely shown or was shown and rejected.
2. **The title’s “He” and “door” are ambiguous.** It could suggest a person at a house rather than a bear opening a car door. A clearer title would be `This Bear Knows How Car Doors Work`.
3. **The 3 AM detail needs verification.** It is not stated in the text of the linked CPW thread. It may be visible in the footage, but should not be asserted without checking the source video.
4. **“They never forget” is too absolute.** The source supports learned/repeated behaviour; the narration should stay close to that evidence.
5. **No related long-video destination appears in the description.** The CTA is to subscribe, not to watch a connected full story.
6. **Credit is not rights clearance.** `Used with credit for commentary/education` is not the same as a licence or written permission.
7. **First-frame and swipe performance remain unreviewed.** These require the video frame and Studio’s Shorts metrics, not guesses from the title.

## Do not abandon this direction after one Short

The topic and behavioural angle are suitable. The correct next step is to verify rights/facts, improve the first-frame promise, connect it to a relevant long video and evaluate a small coherent run—not to revert to random karma or pet clips.

# 14. Recommended first-video packaging and opening

## Title options for an official A/B test

1. `This Bear Learned to Open Car Doors — Here’s Why`
2. `Why Bears Keep Opening Car Doors`
3. `The Bear Didn’t Break In — He Used the Handle`

## Thumbnail brief

- Use a real, cleared frame of a bear at the car handle.
- Make paw + handle + bear face immediately readable.
- One event, no collage, no fake attack, no tiny labels.
- Optional two-word text: `HE LEARNED`.
- Check readability at phone size and at TV distance.

## First 30 seconds

**0:00–0:03 — proof**  
Show the actual handle/door action.

> “Watch his paw. This bear isn’t forcing the door—he’s using the handle.”

**0:03–0:10 — sourced explanation**  

> “Colorado Parks and Wildlife says bears that learn cars can contain food may repeat this behaviour—even when no food is inside.”

**0:10–0:25 — second proof / expanded stakes**  
Show another cleared example or a simple explanation of learned food conditioning.

**By 0:30 — the real editorial question**

> “The important question isn’t only how he opened it. It’s how people accidentally teach bears to keep checking our cars and homes.”

No logo animation, “stay locked in,” or generic countdown preamble.

# 15. Long-form and Shorts connection system

## Every long video

- 7–9 minutes only when the story supports it.
- Strongest real proof in the first three seconds.
- A small payoff by 0:25, not only a promise for the end.
- Natural, mature English narration.
- Specific observation and sourced context instead of maximum-intensity adjectives.
- One relevant next video verbally introduced at the end.
- One matching end-screen video and one matching playlist.

## Every Short

- One moment/question, not an unrelated five-clip dump.
- First frame makes the animal and action readable immediately.
- First line of description: `Full story: [related long-video URL]` once that episode exists.
- Pinned comment links the same related story/playlist.
- Final-second cue: `Full story on Monarch Cam`.
- No unsupported time, location, species or behaviour claim.

A Short should be an entry point into the same story world, not an isolated view source.

| Strong early retention + drop before delayed payoff | Structure is withholding value too long | Move the title payoff earlier; rebalance chapter time |
| High Shorts feed impressions + low stayed-to-watch | First frame/first seconds are losing viewers | Rework the opening frame and initial action |
| Low Shorts feed impressions + strong swipe/retention metrics | Creative may not be the main constraint | Do not judge from views alone; test the next coherent Short |
| Views but almost no next-video movement | Weak content bridge/end destination | Strengthen series adjacency, verbal handoff and end screens |

---

# 17. Testing rules for the next four long videos

YouTube’s current official A/B tool can test up to three titles/thumbnails or combinations and chooses results using **watch-time share**, not CTR alone. Tests can take up to two weeks and may be inconclusive when impressions are insufficient.

Therefore:

- Do not expect a meaningful A/B result from 25 views alone.
- Test materially different, accurate packages—not three versions of the same hype.
- Change one strategic variable at a time outside the native experiment.
- Compare uploads over equivalent 24-hour, 7-day and 28-day windows.
- Do not compare a 39-day-old upload’s lifetime count with a two-year-old winner’s lifetime count.

### Internal creative goals, not algorithm guarantees

The supplied historical benchmarks showed:

- 0:30 retention around **60–67%**;
- average viewed around **28–38.5%** on the audited winners.

A reasonable production aim is to beat the channel’s own historical **67% at 0:30** and **38.5% average viewed** benchmarks. These are internal improvement targets, not universal YouTube thresholds.

Judge the reset only after **four coherent adjacent wildlife long videos**, not after one ranking upload or one 3-view Short.

2. Do not delete the still-active original bear video.

# 19. Final diagnosis in one paragraph

Monarch Cam still owns a valuable, actively viewed bear asset and several proven wildlife-curiosity concepts. The current collapse is concentrated in the **new-release system**: extreme long-form inactivity, mixed audience promises, contradictory branding, generic and structurally weak latest-long packaging, unsupported factual hype, uncertain footage rights and missing Shorts-to-long-form bridges. The public evidence supports a focused wildlife rebuild on the same channel. It does **not** support declaring the channel permanently dead or inventing a shadowban explanation. Studio screenshots for the latest long and three recent Shorts are the next evidence needed to determine whether the immediate bottleneck is impressions, CTR, first-30-second retention or Shorts swipe behaviour.
