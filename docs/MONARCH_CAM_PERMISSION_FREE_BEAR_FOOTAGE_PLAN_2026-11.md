## 1. The rule for this production route

No permission emails does **not** require random copyrighted downloading. Use assets whose existing terms already allow the intended use:

1. Public-domain footage with an asset-specific declaration.
2. Creative Commons/stock footage whose actual licence permits commercial use and the intended edits.
3. Original narration, diagrams, maps, typography, animation and safely filmed demonstration material.

Do not treat crop, mirror, zoom, speed changes, filters, credit or a disclaimer as copyright clearance. YouTube's reused-content review and copyright enforcement are separate. A strong original edit is required for originality, but editing alone does not clear an arbitrary copyrighted source.

Official references:

- YouTube fair use: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148?hl=en
- YouTube copyright myths: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797449?hl=en
- YouTube channel monetisation/reused content: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en

Do **not** make CPW's video the default commercial edited-footage source under this no-permission workflow. CPW publishes its media under **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**, states that commercial use requires express written permission, and does not permit modification apart from resizing under that licence:

- https://cpw.state.co.us/news-and-media/media-kits

This is why the NPS public-domain pack is the cleaner default for a monetised, edited episode.

These visuals must clarify the real event, not fabricate extra wildlife action.

## 6. Originality standard for every sourced clip

The episode should contain:

- a new editorial question and complete narrative structure;
- original natural-English narration throughout;
- source-based behavioural explanation;
- deliberate shot selection, sequencing and removals;
- meaningful annotations/diagrams where they add understanding;
- clear distinction between observation, sourced interpretation and uncertainty;
- original thumbnail composition and sound mix using audio we are entitled to use.

Avoid:

- long unaltered source segments joined by a few narration lines;
- replaying the same clip without adding new analysis;
- random crop/mirror/speed/filter changes presented as transformation;
- `no copyright intended`, `all rights to owner` or `fair use` boilerplate as a clearance substitute;
- a video whose main appeal is simply watching someone else's original incident again.
