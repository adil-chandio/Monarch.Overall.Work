# MONARCHs Edit — celebrity-life POV: deep channel study & operating plan

## Executive decision

**Recommendation: develop and test the concept; do not yet commit the whole channel to it.** The promise is: “Experience a day in your favourite famous person's life, from your point of view.” This is a *lifestyle experience*, not the stadium-crisis story suggested earlier. Audience should feel the attractions **and** trade-offs of that life. English-language, globally intelligible, genuinely animated 2.5D; no stock footage or A/B-roll.

A new lead strengthens the adjacent evidence: a recently created football POV channel has several multi-million-view public videos. But its match/football POV is **not proof** of demand for an animated celebrity's full day, or proof of its audience geography. Exact celebrity-day stickman examples in our sample remain weak. That combination is an untested hypothesis, not a confirmed open market.

**The requested forensic video review remains incomplete.** We have not watched the competitor videos or analyzed their actual frames/seconds, camera, animation, sound, or workflow. On an earlier 12-video public probe, unauthenticated playback/download attempts all received YouTube's verification challenge. We did not bypass it. YouTube Data API metadata and comments are not footage, retention or production data. This report distinguishes observations from proposals.

### Comment observations, not causal explanations

- On the Neymar POV, sampled comments discuss football stars and at least one references the thumbnail; on the manager POV some explicitly mention AI and realism. This suggests engagement with familiar identity and visual plausibility, but comments cannot reveal actual retention or validate authorship. The majority of audience did not necessarily comment.
- On the Ronaldo POV, a sampled comment asks what the viewer would do; another mentions a luxury car. This is compatible with testing experiential choices, but seven comments cannot validate overall demand.
- On the LV Animation video, sampled comments appeared in multiple languages. **Do not infer audience proportions or Tier-1 viewers from this.**
- On Mr Stickify, selected commenters discuss whether the depicted workplace situation feels accurate. This is a clue to test recognizable life moments, not a reason the video went viral.
- No cross-video conclusion about pacing, actual animation, soundtrack, hook retention, watch time or a creator's production process can be derived from these comments.

## Strategy: a coherent channel, not interchangeable celebrity templates

**Positioning:** “Step into the life of the world's most recognizable people—one animated day at a time.” A viewer-focused English series with personality-specific storytelling, original character animation and a blend of aspiration, work, fame and real-life trade-offs.

**Four content pillars to test:**
1. **Elite athlete:** celebrity, training, travel, match-day pressure and limited privacy, as supported by public sources.
2. **Entrepreneur/creator:** work, decisions and public exposure, not invented wealth claims or fake dialogue.
3. **Musician/actor:** rehearsal, performance and public-facing moments where independently documented.
4. **Counterfactual rich/famous:** explicitly fictional “what if” life when factual information is insufficient; do not present it as a named person's true day.

Each episode needs its own specific premise, sourced details, distinct props/locations and satisfying ending. Celebrity labels and decorative mansions are not enough. Titles promise an experience and the opening should deliver it immediately. Make no claims of insider access. The aspirational tone should be fun and premium, not gossip, humiliation, clickbait or moral lecturing. Use English narration and globally understandable cues; Tier-1 is a measured goal, not an assumption.

**Ronaldo example — a proposed imagined day, not a verified itinerary:** wake up in a luxurious setting → life gets focused around athlete-level training → fans recognize you as you go about your day → football/work and recovery → quiet family-time imagery → ask the viewer whether they would trade privacy for that life. Show a mix of POV hands/eyes and an expressive third-person stickman; strictly stylized, no cloned voice or real footage. The 2019 [Evening Standard report](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/world/ronaldo-reveals-price-of-fame-in-emotional-interview-i-can-t-take-kids-to-park-i-want-to-be-free-a4239681.html) describes a historical interview about loss of privacy; do not present it as his precise current daily experience. [Goal's training interview/report](https://www.goal.com/en/news/cristiano-ronaldo-diet-workout-fitness-regime-real-madrid/1ez74ip78o20715bzr1947rwhi) supports broad training/discipline themes; it also includes disputed exact sleep claims, which should not be used. No unsupported alarm time, car ownership, private address, salary or family dialogue. Mark storytelling as illustrative.

## Recommended validation plan

**Phase 0 — source & quality gate (before production):** complete forensic viewing using authorized recordings; make a claim ledger for the first personality; source public statements and clearly separate illustrative scenes. Develop a short fully animated rig/action test *after user approval* to verify feasibility; do not present a static slideshow as animation.

**Phase 1 — 3–5 pilot Shorts, not yet a permanent pivot:**
- Start with one Ronaldo life-experience pilot because the user's example is clear. Based on user's requested format, plan roughly one minute; do not pad if the story is stronger shorter. The 7–20s competitors above are not proof that 60s fails—test own retention.
- Follow with two or more subjects with distinctly different work/lives, subject to sufficient reliable sourcing. Keep premise, craft and publishing conditions reasonably comparable; do not pick people just for reported wealth.
- Track tests as exploratory, not statistically conclusive: different celebrities bring different fan bases, time, event cycles and distribution; avoid attributing results solely to style or duration.
- Repeat successful *principles* only after inspecting multiple episodes, not copying other creators' animation or a repetitive template.

**Phase 2 — measurement after upload:** At 24h, 7d and 28d record views, engaged views, stayed-to-watch/swiped-away, average view duration and percentage, key retention dips where available, traffic source, subscriber change, geography, and cost/hours per usable minute. Use [YouTube Studio's Shorts metric definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid). Compare like-length uploads with same-channel baseline where possible; do not compare raw numbers at different ages as velocity. Determine numeric goals with actual recent Studio data rather than invented benchmarks.

**Decision gates:** Continue if the pilot batch demonstrates improving initial hold, completion and genuine comments *and* a material share of target-country viewers, relative to own channel baseline, at a sustainable production cost. Refine if hold is weak but specific moments retain viewers. Pause/pivot if several well-executed pilots fail against comparable channel results or quality cannot be delivered economically. Samples of 3–5 are directional, not definitive.

**Monetization reality:** Shorts RPM is calculated per 1,000 engaged views; actual returns depend on geography, monetization eligibility and other factors, not merely English narration. [YouTube RPM](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357?hl=en), [Shorts monetization](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en). No views, RPM or revenue guarantee. Use original scripts, animation and rights-cleared audio; highly repetitive or mass-produced templates risk monetization eligibility ([YouTube monetization policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). Channel identity must not imply celebrity affiliation or impersonation ([YouTube impersonation policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801947?hl=en)). Check realistic AI/altered media disclosure for the actual finished content ([disclosure guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)); stylized animation is not automatically required to be disclosed, and labels do not override other policies.

## Production design — genuinely animated, no stock

- Art direction: rigged stick figure with character-specific but original non-branded cues, layered 2.5D locations, controlled lighting and legible vertical composition.
- Animation: head, torso, upper/lower limbs and planted feet; action keys, in-betweens, anticipation, follow-through and object interactions. Use POV hands where helpful, not only detached character cutouts. Independent depth layers, props and a moving virtual camera for parallax.
- Editorial: clear first-second promise, situations with causal continuity, motivated transitions, sound design, licensed/original music and English narration, with captions inside safe areas. No fake voice, actual third-party footage, still-image slideshow or A/B-roll.
- Workflow: source ledger → script → boards and animatic → character rigs → blocking → polish and sound → visual QA → factual/rights check → publish only with user approval. Rendered motion must be inspected frame-by-frame for feet sliding, limb intersections and abrupt camera jumps; include a mobile-size pass and audio mastering check. This is an implementation plan, not a claim we have achieved finished quality.
