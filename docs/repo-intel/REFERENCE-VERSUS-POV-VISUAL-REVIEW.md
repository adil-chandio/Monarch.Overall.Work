## Scope of the new result

`deliverables/monarch-cinematic-reference-pov.mp4` is an original 60-second, 960×540, 24 fps 2D test. It uses a fictional celebrity-day subject, prior synthetic voice clips and a new procedural score. It does not reuse the user's video frames, characters, audio, brand or source set designs, and does not claim to depict the real person's private routine. All visual frames are generated from shapes, gradients and effects; sets may have deliberate holds and repeated elements.

QC: FFmpeg fully decoded 1,440 video frames and audio across 60 seconds. The earlier build was visually checked on 18 sampled stills and on three 3-second/4-fps contact sheets (36 sampled output frames); final revision changed a close-up expression and small idle bob, then was fully decoded and four selected frame pairs tested for reproducible change. No human audio listening or full output frame inspection. Mean/max from volumedetect were -17.5/-1.4 dBFS, **not** integrated LUFS or true peak. Final file SHA-256 `eaa26d445f929b5310a752404025622794a79f52adb2e337af01b1cd2297514a`.

This is closer in overall lighting and composition than the prior portrait attempt, but it is **not a reference match or publication-ready premium film**. Weak points: simplistic torso and limb design, roughly timed ball contact, limited physical staging and expression, repeated backgrounds, abrupt/limited camera moves, limited scene-specific sound, and no independent audio QC. The previous mistake was presenting a surface-level stickman/color approximation as reference-inspired without reflecting these other qualities; the comparison above makes that gap explicit.
