# Stickman motion: direct visual forensic study — partial, not a viral-competitor audit

## Scope and evidence

This is a close visual analysis of **two accessible animation files**, not of the YouTube competitors identified in our market research. Neither sample has been verified as viral, recent, 2.5D, celebrity POV, or suitable as the quality target for MONARCHs Edit. They are two works by the same animator, so observations cannot be generalized to an entire market.

1. **Coconut Mayhem** — turkey3, [source and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Coconut_Mayhem.webm), [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/). 594×446, encoded 30 fps, duration 12.073 s, 362 decoded frames. I inspected all 362 decoded frames at small contact-sheet size, laid out chronologically as [13 timestamped sheets](open-animation-motion/coconut/allframes/). At this size fine line/art changes can be missed; multiple consecutive encoded frames can depict the same pose. This is **not a claim that the creator animated 30 unique drawings per second**. Audio was not audited, so there are no sound-design conclusions.
2. **Snowball Fight** — turkey3, [source and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Snowball_Fight.webm), [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/). 594×446, encoded 30 fps, duration 38.023 s. Visual examination: 1 fps sheets across duration and 5 fps sheets for the first 8 seconds ([sheets](open-animation-motion/snowball/)). This is sampled, **not** a complete frame-by-frame review. Audio not audited. Commons notes the source recording contains lag, so measured screen motion may reflect the recording rather than original timing.

The displayed contact-sheet timestamps correspond to encoded sample presentation times. The original screen begins approximately 0.014 seconds into the decoded stream. The times below are rounded and describe the visible frames; they are not audience-retention measurements. The contact-sheet derivatives have been scaled and annotated; attribution and same-license terms are retained. Do not reuse these examples in a future monetized video without checking licenses and any third-party rights.

### Close motion mechanics

- **Staging:** uninterrupted wide framing with palm at center, left/right figures separated and a bright high-contrast sky. The eye tracks the brown object against a largely stable backdrop. This is screen-space 2D imagery; the frames do **not** prove a layered 2.5D scene or camera parallax.
- **Silhouette and gesture:** the bending pickup, raised arms, straight-to-horizontal rotation and question mark carry intent without detailed facial animation. Hand/foot lines are extremely small. Actual joint rigging or keyframe software cannot be inferred merely from rendered pixels; Commons describes it as frame-by-frame Scratch animation.
- **Action continuity:** the object changes screen position across frames rather than being replaced by a simple zoom on a still. Some poses visibly persist across consecutive encoded frames. The throw/reversal/fall are clear macro beats, but the exact contact moment and individual fingertip movements cannot be confidently verified from the thumbnails.
- **Timing:** the pickup occupies much of the opening second; the ball's travel is followed by a reaction, a return and a long aftermath. The signal is cause → reaction → consequence. No competitor-retention or sound timing conclusion can be drawn from a frame sheet.
- **Limitations as a premium benchmark:** small characters, locked camera, simple flat background, minimal body detail and long holds are very different from the requested premium lifestyle film. Treat it as a narrow motion-mechanics reference, not the finished aesthetic or quality bar.

## B. Snowball Fight: sampled visual timeline

**Sampling qualification:** the first 8 seconds have 0.2-second contact-sheet samples; from there the timeline uses approximately one sample per second. Fast contacts and between-frame animation may be missed; the following describes visible sampled states rather than claiming complete events.

- **0–2 s:** Snowy wide scene with two stick figures and a central tree. One figure is seen low/airborne on the left, another figure at the right; a small circular prop appears between them. The changing body angle makes the action readable against a static background.
- **2–4 s:** One figure lies toward the right, while the other remains upright and the circular prop changes location. By roughly 4 s the prop is seen high against the tree; a surviving pose on the right appears with arms wide. The sparse samples do not prove every causal step.
- **4–8 s:** The right-side character bends, stands and changes arm/leg pose; two small circles are visible near ground at points. At around 7–8 s figure spacing and arm poses shift. The 5 fps sheets reveal more transition than the 1 fps overview, though they still skip five out of six encoded frames.
- **8–14 s:** Sampled prop positions appear above/near tree branches and then figures return to both sides. A dark curved line appears near the upper trunk/branches around 10–11 s. Its exact function is unclear from these samples.
- **14–21 s:** Figures exchange position and airborne/prone poses; around 16–17 s a figure is high against the tree and the other appears grounded, then one is prone near the right. Circular objects continue to change placement.
- **21–29 s:** Multiple poses show one figure moving through the center, another at the right, occasional low/inverted positions, and circles on the ground. At 24 s one figure appears inverted. Frames alone do not establish the precise collision technique.
- **29–38 s:** Two figures appear closer near center-right; toward the ending a much larger white ball appears at the far right and grows/persists while figures remain nearby. This is an escalation in *visible scale*, not evidence of 3D depth or camera motion.

**Transferable but unvalidated hypothesis:** keep character/object/space relationships instantly readable, use anticipation → movement → visible reaction, and stage interaction rather than merely panning stills. For our celebrity lifestyle scenes these are principles to test, not a formula copied from the clip. A POV hand reaching for a breakfast glass should show shoulder/elbow/wrist movement, hand-object contact, glass travel and settle; a training shot should show a weight shift and grounded feet. Add a designed 2.5D environment, camera and sound separately because these two samples do not establish those qualities.
