# MONARCHs Edit: Stickman celebrity POV — research & pilot recommendation

## Executive verdict

**Recommendation: test the concept as a pilot, not a full channel pivot.** There is strong evidence for adjacent stickman animation, football and engaging character stories, but this search did not establish strong demand, competition levels, RPM, or Tier-1 viewership for their exact intersection. A celebrity name alone is not a story; a concrete challenge and surprising payoff are the proposed differentiator.

**Important: the requested frame-by-frame and second-by-second competitor audit is NOT complete.** We inspected metadata and public comments, not the videos. ## What the comments can and cannot tell us

Among the requested top comments, the jumping comparison drew discussion of height and framing; the football-trivia video included skepticism about sourcing. The stickman/gaming example drew discussion of game-lag scenarios, and the motivation film drew personal reflections about discipline. These are directional clues that **a specific question, recognizable scenario or credible detail can generate conversation**. They do not demonstrate why a video succeeded, establish that viewers are from Tier-1 countries, or quantify demand. No comment-derived claims about editing rhythm or motion are justified.

## Proposed creative position — a hypothesis, not copied competitor analysis

**Format:** 55–60 seconds, English, original animated stickman in an authored 2.5D world. Celebrity POV gives immediate context, but each episode revolves around a specific dilemma rather than an unsupported luxury itinerary. Potential title: **“POV: You’re Ronaldo — But the Stadium Goes Silent”**. Alternate hypothesis: **“POV: You Live Like Ronaldo for One Day”** with a clear shift from luxury to training. Both should be tested for appeal; neither has been validated as a viral winner.

**Proposed beat sheet**, subject to sourcing and creative approval (not a reconstruction of any competitor):
- **0–3s — hook:** a stickman in an iconic number-7-inspired, original non-branded kit faces an enormous stadium; the crowd abruptly cuts out. Text: “POV: You're Ronaldo. One shot. Everyone's watching.” Frame as illustrative fiction, not footage of a real match.
- **3–10s — scale:** tunnel walk, shifting shadows and stadium reveal; a camera push creates depth, with the character's feet visibly stepping and arms counter-swinging.
- **10–21s — tension:** stylized warm-up and training; one failed attempt, reaction and reset. Do not fabricate his real daily schedule or medical habits.
- **21–37s — decision:** goal frame, goalkeeper silhouette, close-up of planted foot and ball; anticipation, kick, follow-through. Camera and sound punctuate the action, not replace it.
- **37–50s — payoff:** ball travels in genuine animated perspective; crowd erupts or a deliberate surprise occurs. Treat as fictional illustration, not a claim about a particular real event.
- **50–60s — close:** quick replay/callback and an honest, non-bait question, e.g. “Whose POV should we animate next?”

This intentionally tests **pressure + performance**, not an unverified private day-in-the-life. The exact script and claims need approval and sourcing before rendering. Secondary reporting quotes Ronaldo discussing training and discipline, but should not be used to fabricate an exact routine: [Goal](https://www.goal.com/en/news/cristiano-ronaldo-diet-workout-fitness-regime-real-madrid/1ez74ip78o20715bzr1947rwhi).

## Production spec: actual animation, not a still-image edit

- **Character:** articulated head, torso, pelvis, upper/lower arms and legs; visible foot plants, weight shifts, anticipation, follow-through and reactions. Build rig with hierarchical transforms and editable keyframes; rigging and in-between motion are required. No repeated static stickman cutouts pretending to act.
- **World:** separate background, midground, foreground, floor and lighting/shadow layers; 2.5D perspective and depth-based parallax. Build a virtual camera and animate true relative layer movement rather than zooming a flattened image. Original stadium/props; no stock footage or A/B-roll.
- **Action:** staged poses first, then animation curves, controlled timing, overlapping limbs and contact shadows; independently animated ball and goalkeeper. Prioritize clear silhouettes at phone size. Sound-led timing is a design choice, not a claim derived from unviewed competitor frames.
- **Camera/edit:** motivated shot changes, tracking, push-ins, close-ups, restrained motion blur, no rapid editing solely to disguise weak animation. Captions large, short and within safe areas, with an unobstructed character.
- **Sound:** English narrator or concise on-screen storytelling, original/appropriately licensed sound and music; kick impact, footsteps, ambience and controlled silence. No cloned celebrity voice. Mix and rights review prior to publication.
- **Feasibility gate before 60s render:** after approval, first rig and complete a short moving-action proof, verify actual limb motion/contact/depth, then storyboard and render the full minute. Review motion continuity, title/claim sourcing, sound mix and mobile legibility. No stock footage.

## Experiment and success criteria

Start with a **small pilot**, then decide whether to expand. Ideally compare 3–6 episodes across a few weeks, varying one primary element at a time: sports-person POV versus generic rich-person POV versus non-celebrity high-pressure POV; balance editing effort and duration. A tiny set cannot establish causation. Avoid optimizing for views alone or changing several variables between tests. Reuse actual character rigs and scene components, not scripts or generic template spam.

For each published Short record first 24h, 7d and 28d: displayed views, engaged views, stayed-to-watch vs swiped-away, average view duration and percentage viewed, retention graph where available, country/region share, subscriber change and production time/cost. Compare with the channel's own recent comparable videos and monitor distribution rather than guaranteeing a threshold. Set numeric targets only after checking current Studio baselines. [YouTube engagement metrics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en-GB&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid). In Shorts, RPM is based on engaged views; geography affects advertising economics, and searches with `regionCode=US` are no substitute for actual analytics. [YouTube RPM](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357?hl=en), [Shorts monetization](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en). Do not promise millions or a specific RPM.

## Trust, rights and platform safety

- Mark fictional POV as an **illustrative imagined scenario**; distinguish sourced public facts from creative interpretation. No fake dialogue, endorsement, private routine, defamatory event, face/voice clone or exact likeness implying official affiliation.
- Follow YouTube's [synthetic content disclosure rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop) where applicable; stylized non-realistic animation may not require disclosure, but realistic synthetic depictions can. Assess the actual finished content, including AI audio/music, at upload.
- Use original or properly licensed assets and sounds; fictionalization does not remove copyright, trademark, publicity or other legal risks. Avoid copying competitors' shots or soundtrack.
