# EXTINCT LIFE — English-market research and next-direction decision

**Research date:** 5 November 2026. **Brief:** market-first, English content, intended US/UK/Canada/Australia viewers. Past Extinct Life formats are not a constraint. This is a decision memo, not a virality or RPM guarantee.

## Executive decision

Do not become another interchangeable “AI monster facts” account. Test **story-led ancient-world science**: a clear mystery or survival question, an evidence trail, original visual explanation, and a satisfying answer.

Recommended positioning to test:

> **Extinct Life — Earth's lost worlds, investigated.**

Two initial lanes:
1. **Ancient case files:** a surprising fossil, experiment or reconstruction leads to an explanation. Primary differentiation: what the evidence actually shows versus what it cannot show.
2. **Survival under real constraints:** place the viewer in one ancient setting, then examine climate, water, ecology and predators. Present hypothetical outcomes as conditional, not precise scientific death timers.

Use long-form as the main narrative product, with original short-form companion stories. This is a production/positioning recommendation supported by the sampled long-form results, NOT evidence that long-form will produce a particular RPM or that Shorts viewers will convert.

Do not fully rebrand or abandon existing uploads before two pilots provide channel-specific evidence. No channel settings, public titles or published videos have been changed.

### Relevance deep dive
- Ten manually shortlisted channels; up to first 50 uploads per channel.
- **297 catalogue records**, with **649 unique video IDs across discovery plus these catalogue samples**. The 297 overlap some discovery records: do not add 374+297 as unique.
- Top two public-view videos per shortlisted channel sampled for up to 50 top-level comments each: **857 top-level records retrieved**. Selected relevant excerpts were inspected; this is not exhaustive sentiment coding of all 857.
- Ten top-video public thumbnails downloaded and visually inspected. Thumbnails are credited research references, not production assets to reuse.

### Coverage limits that matter

This is a bounded discovery sample, not the whole of YouTube, a random sample, or proof of an unsaturated niche. Search result ranking, query vocabulary and recency filters bias discovery. English relevance settings still returned Hindi, Spanish, memes, gaming, celebrity and other irrelevant records. Those records remain in the raw evidence but are excluded from the strategic comparators below. No Hindi/desi market strategy is recommended.

Duration <=180 seconds is only a short-length proxy; aspect ratio/placement was not verified for every result. Search popularity does not establish US/UK/CA/AU audience composition. Country settings, English titles, subscriber ratios and hashtags such as #usa do not establish RPM.

Public metadata and thumbnails were retrieved, not complete video playback or private competitor retention/CTR/geography/revenue. Title structures, description chapters and visible thumbnail choices are observations; explanations of why they work are hypotheses. Creation dates establish account age, not the date an editorial business started. Subscriber growth needs repeated snapshots; no “growing fastest” ranking is claimed here.

## 3. What people are packaging successfully

These are recurring **observed promises**, not experimentally isolated viral causes.

### A. Make deep time personal

“How long would YOU last?” and modern-animal/ancient-environment questions give the viewer a familiar reference point. Fossil and BeyondTheBlue show multiple large results around this family. Description chapter lists provide discrete eras or matchups, suggesting an episodic progression rather than one unbroken creature list.

**Hypothesis:** an easily understood question plus successive decisions offers reasons to keep watching. No competitor retention curve was accessed, so this mechanism remains an inference.

**Our adaptation:** one bounded place/time, explicit assumptions and changing survival constraints. Do not copy their exact script, timer or sequence. Avoid pretending a fictional encounter has a scientifically exact survival duration.

### B. A real scientific action opens a mystery

BlackWater's alligator title names what scientists did, an unusual setting and an unresolved consequence. Its description lists research references and chapters for the three animals. Mark Watts pairs a large geological consequence with an explanation and source links.

**Hypothesis:** “action → surprising observation → investigation” is a more concrete promise than “10 terrifying creatures.” This has both repeat-channel evidence and a distinct visual grammar.

**Our adaptation:** investigate a different ancient-life question with an actual paper, a timeline and a clear answer. Research-led does not mean pretending we performed the experiment ourselves.

### C. Familiar belief, defensible correction

Discovery Lab's Megalodon title and Fossil Stories' birds/dinosaurs premise challenge a known idea. Their outcomes vary widely. Do not use “scientists were wrong” as a generic truth claim or manufacture a new discovery.

**Our adaptation:** name the old reconstruction, show the new evidence, state the remaining uncertainty. Explain changing models as science working, not experts lying.

### D. Humor creates a voice, not just a topic

Fossil Stories' retrieved comment samples repeatedly quote a humorous dinosaur/bird line. Fossil's leading comments include self-deprecating survival jokes. These support the presence of a memorable audience-facing voice; comments are not a transcript or proof that a joke caused views.

**Our adaptation:** restrained conversational humor after the premise is clear, not stock “you won't believe” narration.

### E. Speculative evolution and crossover biology are adjacent, not a default pivot

Vividen and Axtro show notable views around fictional/real organism crossovers and growth sequences. Their thumbnails and descriptions explicitly involve invented organisms or named third-party creations. The audience and rights context differ from factual extinct-life explanation.

**Decision:** keep as a later, clearly labeled original-fiction experiment if we can fund original worldbuilding. Do not mix invented biology into factual episodes without labeling it.

## 4. Thumbnail observations: evidence, not a universal formula

Ten actual public thumbnail JPEGs were inspected, stored under `assets/extinct-market/`.

- **Fossil:** dark scene, one imposing animal, small human reference and “12 Minutes” in red/white. Strong visual statement of personal danger; the number is packaging, not a validated scientific survival estimate.
- **BeyondTheBlue:** large monochrome sea creature, tiny diver, red arrow and “9 MINUTES.” Similar personal-scale/time promise.
- **BlackWater:** one alligator suspended above the seafloor, minimal “The Bait” text and arrow. It shows the setup, while the title asks for the result.
- **Mark Watts:** a dramatic rift landscape and “BIRTH OF A NEW OCEAN,” translating a geological process into an immediately readable consequence. A thumbnail alone cannot establish whether this is a photograph, simulation or generated reconstruction.
- **Fossil Stories:** portrait subject, illustrated face/eyes and large captions: playful educational packaging rather than prestige CGI.
- **Discovery Lab:** portrait diver above a huge shark silhouette with caption text: recognizable scale and threat.
- **Axtro:** four age stages/color panels with invented creatures. An obvious progression, but inappropriate as unmarked factual natural history.
- **Vividen:** illustrated fictional leviathan confronting marine life; strong genre signal.
- **ExtinctTracks:** month/fate text over a dinosaur, reinforcing interactive identification.
- **TimeSeed:** Hindi text visibly contradicts treating it as a clean English-market reference.

**Production rule to test:** one question, one dominant image, one useful scale/causal cue. Design title and thumbnail as complementary promises. Test alternatives; do not copy specific compositions or claim a CTR lift without our own impressions.

## 5. What comments add—and what they do not

Selected public comment excerpts suggest:
- Some viewers value a natural narrator: a Fossil comment praising not hearing an AI voice had **2,933 likes** at the snapshot. This is not proof of production provenance or a blanket ban on synthetic voices; it is a warning against monotonous delivery.
- Viewers challenge ecological assumptions: a BeyondTheBlue comment questions the premise that every part of an ancient ocean is densely populated by predators.
- Viewers notice reconstruction uncertainty: comments discuss revised Dunkleosteus size and contested shark reconstructions. Viewer claims are not scientific sources and require independent verification.
- BlackWater viewers discuss camera-light effects and request better observational methods. These are useful questions to investigate, not automatically correct facts about light or the study.
- Fictional-growth viewers request more fictional organisms; those requests belong to that audience, not proof that Extinct Life should pivot to the same intellectual property.

**Opportunity hypothesis:** combine a strong mystery with visible assumptions and honest uncertainty. Be more trustworthy without making the story feel like a lecture. This is differentiation to test, not an established empty market.

## 7. Proposed first 30 days — tests, not a permanent niche lock

**Deliverables target:** 2 original 10–14-minute pilots + 8 standalone/companion Shorts. These runtimes and cadence are working production choices, not observed universal optimum. If source/visual quality cannot fit the available hours, reduce output instead of filling with generic clips. No paid subscriptions, equipment purchases or production budget are authorized by this plan.

### Pilot A: Ancient case file

**Working title:** *What Happened After a Sea Monster Died?*

**Promise:** investigate the organisms and evidence around ancient marine carcasses rather than another “which monster wins” list.

**Why test it:** adjacent demand from BlackWater's experiment narrative, direct ancient-ocean demand from Fossil/BeyondTheBlue, and a research-backed bridge between modern processes and prehistoric life. This exact topic was not separately demand-validated, so it remains a proposal.

**Proposed thumbnail:** a reconstructed marine reptile skeleton fading into the seafloor; a small close-up trace/evidence cue; optional “WHAT ATE IT?” No competitor artwork or simulated fossil presented as a real specimen.

**Research foundation already located:** the 2019 alligator-food-fall paper and 2015 work on bone-eating worm traces in Mesozoic reptile bones. The alligator paper is a real modern experiment, not a newly performed 2026 discovery. Directly retrieved abstract: [Alligators in the abyss](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6924670/). The 2015 study and reported fossil borings are described here: [2](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/04/150414212447.htm). Read the original 2015 paper before locking detailed claims.

**Narrative draft:**
- 0:00–0:25: one giant dies; why might evidence of it disappear?
- 0:25–1:15: state the question and show the evidence boundaries.
- 1:15–3:30: explain modern carcass-fall observations with source/date labels.
- 3:30–6:00: follow the documented ancient bone traces.
- 6:00–9:00: how preservation changes what we can infer; no claim that one organism explains the whole missing fossil record.
- Ending: answer the opening question and identify one unresolved question.

These are planned beats, not a measured reconstruction of a competitor's edit.

### Pilot B: Survival with real constraints

**Working title:** *Could You Survive a Day in Ice Age Britain?*

**Promise:** one researched environment, not a death timer across all of Earth's history. Water, shelter, weather and energy matter alongside predators.

**Why test it:** direct repeated survival-format results. A familiar English-speaking-market location is a creative relevance choice, not proof of Tier-1 distribution.

**Research gate:** select a specific time slice/locality before scripting; verify the actual fauna, climate, coastline and human context for that slice. Do not combine animals from different continents/eras or claim a mathematically exact survival time.

**Thumbnail proposal:** a lone human-scale silhouette, one clearly reconstructed habitat/animal and a simple shelter/survival cue. No copied “9/12 MINUTES” gimmick or unverified extreme scale.

## 8. How production should differ from generic automation

1. **Research dossier:** one question, at least two credible sources for major factual claims where available, source date, competing interpretations and licensing notes. A competitor description is a lead, not proof.
2. **Claims ledger:** line-by-line fact / inference / reconstruction / fiction labels. Mark numeric ranges, geography, evolutionary relationships and uncertain behaviors.
3. **Packaging before expensive production:** title plus thumbnail sketch plus the first 20–30 seconds. If the opening cannot fulfill the promise, rewrite before generating scenes.
4. **English editorial pass:** natural language, correct names, clear units (metric plus imperial when useful), no forced slang/accent or falsely claimed location. Actual intelligibility matters more than performing a “premium” accent.
5. **Narration:** audition a short passage for pacing, pronunciation and personality. Human or licensed synthetic narration must be directed and reviewed; do not infer that an AI voice automatically fails or a human voice guarantees success. No voice registration or audio generation was performed in this research task.
6. **Visual grammar:** original maps/timelines/scale diagrams, rights-cleared evidence images and controlled reconstructions. Use cinematic imagery to clarify, not replace explanation. Keep anatomy, environment and scale consistent across scenes.
7. **Edit around information:** each beat advances the question. Do not enforce an arbitrary cut every two seconds or pad to a revenue-oriented length. Keep music below narration and label reconstructions.
8. **Rights and truth check:** track licenses and required attributions, verify disclosure requirements, avoid implying AI reconstructions are actual discovered footage. No downloaded competitor thumbnails may be reused in production.
9. **Prepublish QC:** factual review, pronounced species names, captions, phone/TV readability, audio intelligibility and title/payoff match. Stop publication if a central claim or right is unresolved.
10. **Human approval:** automation can help collect evidence, track tasks and prepare assets; it should not auto-publish unchecked scientific claims.

## 9. Tier-1 intent and monetization: what can actually be controlled

Choose English subject framing, understandable narration, accessible captions, credible sourcing and relevant viewer questions. Then measure actual country mix in our own Studio once there is enough data. US channel settings, VPNs, #usa, tagging and English search filters do not establish premium audience delivery.

Do not forecast an RPM or income from competitor views. Watch Page ads and Shorts Feed ads are separate monetization mechanisms: [2](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/94522?hl=en&ref_topic=9257989). This is why a deliberate long-form test is useful for an eventual narrative/revenue strategy, not proof it will earn more for this channel.

YouTube's monetization guidance explicitly addresses repetitive/mass-produced/inauthentic content and original value: [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en). That supports building original investigations rather than scaling a low-variation template. AI use itself is not established here as an automatic disqualification; rights, originality and applicable disclosure/policy requirements still matter.

No monetization eligibility, actual revenue, advertiser suitability approval, audience-country composition of competitors, or sponsorship availability was verified.

## 10. Measurement and decision rules

### Long-form
At 24h, 72h, 7d and 28d: impressions, CTR by major traffic source, first-30-second retention, average view duration, average percentage viewed, watch time, subscriptions, returning viewers and actual geography. Distinguish Browse/Suggested/Search and compare similar upload ages. Public competitor views are not a retention benchmark.

### Shorts
Track engaged views, stayed/swiped, average duration/percentage viewed, source mix, actual countries where available, returning behavior and related-video traffic if available. Shorts reach alone does not demonstrate long-form conversion.

### Diagnostic action, not arbitrary universal thresholds
- Low impressions: insufficient distribution evidence; do not immediately blame the thumbnail or delete the video.
- Meaningful impressions but weak source-matched CTR: revisit packaging and promise clarity.
- Good click response but sharp opening loss: check whether the opening fulfills the promise before adding more effects.
- Good consumption but wrong intended audience mix: reassess topic/framing/distribution; English does not automatically equal Tier-1.
- One large winner and several weak videos: investigate before scaling. Discovery Lab is the cautionary comparator.
- Repeated same-lane wins with manageable production cost: allocate the next batch toward that lane.

Two pilots are exploratory. A permanent pivot needs more comparable uploads and enough observations; no statistical significance or exact optimal posting hour is claimed. Maintain a second market snapshot after 7–14 days to measure changes in views/subscribers instead of calling one snapshot “growth.”

## 11. Suggested decision now

**Approve research-led pilot development, not a full irreversible niche pivot.** Keep English as fixed. Use Ancient Case Files as the first production hypothesis and Real-Constraint Survival as a competing second hypothesis. Let evidence decide whether to expand ancient oceans, Ice Age survival, reconstruction science or another adjacent lane.

The advance over the old channel is not “more AI” or “more uploads.” It is a clearer question, better evidence, an original voice and a repeatable way to learn which audience actually responds.
