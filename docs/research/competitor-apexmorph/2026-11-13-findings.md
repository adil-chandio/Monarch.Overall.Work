(`15bNSSAxGbC9diW8TpuBHjL6NUP3uD-4h → Channel 1`). Three files were in it: two
screenshots of the channel page, and one full video.

**Read this before the conclusions.** The video was measured on the sandbox: container,
duration, bitrate, loudness, frame samples and shot changes, all real numbers. The **script
was not transcribed** — every speech-to-text model is unreachable from here

→ 404 at their old paths). So nothing below claims what the narrator *said*. Where I reason
about the writing, it is from on-screen text that is legible in the frames, and it says so.

millions". It is: **this channel found one vein and hit it twice.** The 15K man-eaters video
proves the format alone does not carry a video; the *subject's tension* does.

## What the picture is actually made of

I sampled 30 frames across the whole runtime into a contact sheet
(`contact-sheet-orcas-video.jpg`, next to this file) and pulled five full-size ones.

**It is not 3D. It is 2D vector motion graphics**, plus some real underwater plate footage
underneath at least one sequence (the bait-ball opening).

The frame inventory, in order of screen time:

| Element | What it looks like | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Solid-colour animal silhouettes** | Flat black whale/orca shapes, no texture, no shading | An untextured silhouette is cheap to make, reads instantly at phone size, and cannot look "wrong" the way a bad 3D model does |
| **Dotted eye + single white patch** | The orca's eye is a *dot*; the white eye-patch is one flat shape | One carefully placed dot is the whole face. This is a deliberate cheat that saves days of modelling |
| **Labelled anatomy diagrams** | `MELON`, `RAKE MARKS`, `MOTHER / CALF / ESCORT`, `FLUKES`, `→ 50 TONNES` | Turns narration into evidence. The viewer is being *taught*, which is why 10 minutes is acceptable |
| **Full-screen text cards** | `WHAT SCARES AN ORCA?`, `THE ORCA PLAYBOOK`, `FLIGHT SPECIES`, `HUMPBACK MUTUAL` | Each card is a chapter marker. Four cards ≈ four acts ≈ a reason to keep watching |
| **Big yellow numbers** | `115 ENCOUNTERS`, `2049` (an animated year counter), `12 BREAKS` | A number on screen is a promise that something concrete is being delivered |
| **Google-Maps-style maps** | Dark map, bright green region, `WESTERN SOUTH ATLANTIC`, `BRAZIL` | Geography without new art. Free, credible, and instantly legible |
| **Tiny kill-strike icons on a map** | Dozens of tiny orca marks clustered in one rectangle | Makes a statistic *visible*: you can count the dots |
| **A UI screen with checkmarks** | List of paper/document items with green ticks and red crosses | "We checked the sources" is *shown*, not claimed |
| **Depth-meter / timeline bar** | A horizontal ruler under `115 ENCOUNTERS`, marked 1980 → 2016 | Time and depth become something you can see |

**The one thing that makes all of it feel expensive:** every diagram is *motion designed*,
not a still. The year counter animates. The strike icons appear one by one. The labels wipe
in. That is After Effects (or equivalent) work, not illustration work — and it costs far less
than 3D while looking more "produced" than 3D that is rushed.

## The numbers behind the picture

| Measurement | Value | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | **10:07** | Safely over YouTube's mid-roll threshold. Every video on the channel does this. |
| Resolution | **1280×720** | Not 1080p. Nobody watching on a phone noticed. |
| Bitrate | **416 kb/s total** (284 video + 127 audio) | Extraordinarily low. This is flat graphics — flat colour compresses to nothing. |
| Audio | AAC 44.1 kHz stereo, 127 kb/s | Ordinary. Nothing fancy. |
| Mean loudness | **−17.4 dB**, peak **−3.4 dB** | Normal speech-forward level. No loudness war. |
| Silence gaps ≥ 0.8 s | **0** | There is no dead air anywhere in ten minutes. |
| Hard cuts | **41** → **4.1 per minute** | Slow by Shorts standards, normal for documentary. |
| Median shot length | **7.0 s** | Long enough to read a label, short enough to hold. |
| Shortest / longest shot | 1.5 s / 71.5 s | The 71.5 s block is a talking-head-free diagram sequence. |
| First cut | at **15.0 s** | The first 15 seconds are one unbroken shot. |

**The last two rows matter most.** *First cut at 15 s* means the hook is not delivered by
editing — it is delivered by the **narration and the premise**, over a single moving image.
Compare that with the repo's own recent work, which cuts fast to compensate. For a 10-minute
story video, cutting fast is not the mechanism; **the claim is**.

And **zero silence gaps** over ten minutes is a hard, unglamorous production discipline:
no pauses, no "um" gaps, no breath room. It is also trivially checkable before upload, which
makes it the cheapest quality gate available.

## Why the two winners win — the pattern in the titles

All six titles are **questions or forbidden claims aimed at an animal the viewer already
knows**:

- "Why Orcas Are **Terrified** Of Humpback whales" — an animal famous for being the apex
  predator, shown afraid. The tension is inside the words.
- "The Only Animals That Make Orcas **Run Away**" — the *same* tension, restated. Hence 3.4M.
- "Nobody Knows About Megalodon's **Evil Twin**" — attaches a known monster (Megalodon) to an
  unknown one. 65K.
- "The Deadliest Man-Eaters In History" — a topic with no surprise in it. **15K.**
- "Why Do Squids Turn Into Nightmares The Deeper You Go?" — squids are not apex predators and
  the premise is not a fact. **7.5K.**

The rule this suggests, and it is the same rule the repo's own measured shorts sample
produced: **a known animal, in a state that contradicts what the audience believes about
it.** Not "the deadliest", not "the scariest" — those are claims the audience already
accepts, so there is nothing to resolve. The views come from *contradiction*.

## What this means for Extinct Life — the uncomfortable comparison

The repo currently produces 3D Blender renders: Cycles, 24 samples, 1080p, 1830 frames for
61 seconds, assembled from 30 shards. That is hours of GPU per minute. ApexMorph produces ten
minutes at **720p and 416 kb/s** in flat vector graphics, and one of those ten minutes
made 3.4 million views.

Neither approach is wrong. But they are not competing on the same axis:

| | Extinct Life (now) | ApexMorph |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes of finished video per render-hour | ~1 | ~10 |
| Looks like | 3D reconstruction, "real" | Diagrams, silhouettes, teaching |
| Sells on | authenticity of the creature | tension of the claim |
| Cost of a wrong shot | re-render, hours | move a shape, minutes |
| Weak point | expensive, slow, hard to iterate | flat look, needs a great script |

**The transferable finding is not "do 2D instead of 3D".** It is that ApexMorph's ten-minute
videos are carried by **the claim and the diagrams**, and both of those are *script-first*
work. The 3D is a cost this repo chose; the script is the thing that actually decides whether
it travels.

## What to do with this, concretely

1. **Rewrite the next Extinct Life title as a contradiction, not a superlative.** Extinct Life
   has exactly the raw material ApexMorph lacks: real specimens and real papers. *"This
   Saber-Toothed Cub Still Has Its Fur"* is already a contradiction title and is already
   researched in `NEXT-TOPIC-2026-11-05.md`. Ship that one.
2. **Split the 3D budget.** One 3D hero shot per act, diagrams for the rest. 480 Blender
   frames for 61 seconds is not a sustainable ratio against a channel doing 10 minutes for
   the same effort.
3. **Adopt the two free gates**: zero silence gaps ≥ 0.8 s, and every claim on screen as a
   legible text card. Both are measurable before upload; both are what the frames show.
4. **Do not chase 10:07.** The mid-roll threshold is a reason ApexMorph goes long. Extinct
   Life's Shorts pipeline is a different product with a different job. The lesson here is
   about **long-form**, and it is about the claim, not the length.

- **No publish dates.** "2w ago" is the only age information available.
- **No second video sampled.** The shot rhythm and the element inventory come from one video
