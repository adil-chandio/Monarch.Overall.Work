# Extinct Life — evidence-to-story workflow v1

An original production workflow informed by actual public-script observations, not a guaranteed viral formula. Its usefulness must be tested through fresh narration, owner comprehension review, finished-film inspection and eventual real audience data. It is not a claim that every successful channel uses these steps.

## A. Start with an evidence contract, not an opening sentence

Write four lines before drafting:

1. **What happened?** A 2022 study reported approximately two-million-year-old environmental DNA in North Greenland, including evidence for a mastodon lineage and a richer biological community.
2. **What is surprising?** An animal can enter a recoverable record through tiny traces in sediment, without its skeleton being the discovery.
3. **What can go wrong in telling it?** Confuse DNA with a body, short fragments with a genome, lineage with an exact portrait, an inferred community with a filmed simultaneous event, or a missing signal with a missing animal.
4. **What can the viewer truthfully leave knowing?** How traces enter sediment, why some may survive, how identification is tested, and why an incomplete ecological reconstruction is still important.

Create claim IDs and source anchors now. If the opening requires a claim that cannot be supported, change the opening rather than burying a correction later.

## B. Choose the narrative engine deliberately

A mystery engine asks a specific question, supplies prerequisites and earns an answer. A list engine accumulates examples that change a perception. A transformation engine tracks a state becoming another state. A decision engine makes the costs of a choice visible.

For this episode choose **trace → obstacle → test → community**, with the opening animal already revealed. We are not a creature-ranking list, a de-extinction proposal or a fake laboratory emergency. The physical identity anchor is one **illustrative** fragment and its surrounding sediment, not a full genome magically assembled on screen.

## C. Design the opening as three jobs, not one slogan

- **Concrete surprise:** name the animal and unexpected place.
- **Evidence correction:** immediately name the actual evidence, not the imagined skeleton.
- **Forward debt:** ask the two questions the rest of the episode really answers: survival and identification.

The rewrite's first three sentences contain 21 whitespace tokens. At a hypothetical 150 words/minute that is about 8.4 seconds of speech; it is **not measured timing**. The target is a fast, clear premise, not a mandatory ten-second template. Do not prepend the old 45-second intro or a brand speech.

Failure test: stop after the opening. Could the viewer wrongly believe researchers found a complete animal? Is the next question specific enough to answer? Is a study date/approximate age present before they become misleading?

## D. Keep a question-and-payoff ledger

For each beat, record the viewer's likely question, the fact that changes their understanding, what should remain uncertain, the next question, and the corresponding visible action. This is an editorial model of comprehension—not telepathy or a measured emotional state.

| Block | Question entering | New answer/reward | Necessary remaining boundary | Forward connection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B01 | What was found? | Mastodon evidence in old dirt, not a skeleton | Trace, approximate age, dated study | How does a life leave that trace? |
| B02 | How does a body reach sediment? | Shedding/decay/transport; mixed environmental DNA | Not a sealed single-animal record | Why was the trace not destroyed? |
| B03 | Why did any of it survive? | Cold and possible mineral protection; binding can also hinder extraction | Illustrative mechanism, surviving fraction | What can a short fragment identify? |
| B04 | How can a match mislead? | Reference ambiguity, contamination, characteristic damage as one authentication aid | No invented scores or perfect authentication | What does the supported collection reveal? |
| B05 | Why more than one exciting animal? | A plant/animal community, enriched beyond earlier evidence | No precise portrait or modern habitat copy | Can we treat our scene as one moment? |
| B06 | Did these animals stand together? | Transport/time mixing and missing-signal filters | No literal encounter or exhaustive census | What other evidence helps? |
| B07 | Why trust a partial reconstruction? | Multiple evidence routes with different blind spots; approximate geological age | Not an exact birthday for each molecule | What recovery did the title actually promise? |
| B08 | Did the past come back? | A vanished community became partly readable | Not a body, complete genome or resurrection | Close; do not open another debt |

If a paragraph answers no live question and supplies no necessary reward, cut or relocate it. Do not manufacture an emergency to rescue a dull explanation.

## E. Put the explanatory detail behind a felt need

The sequence is not simply fact → fact → fact. It is problem → physical mechanism → consequence.

- A missing skeleton creates a need for shedding and transport.
- Fragile information creates a need for cold and mineral binding.
- Mineral protection introduces a useful trade-off: strongly bound material may be difficult to recover.
- Ambiguous short reads create a need for reference comparison and cautious labels.
- Contamination creates a need for authentication; the paper's characteristic-damage filtering makes this concrete.
- A beautiful combined scene creates a need to distinguish accumulation from simultaneity.

A technical word earns its place only if it helps answer the current question. Reference DNA is necessary; naming every sequencing instrument and threshold is not necessary in this film. Put detailed methods in the source ledger rather than pretending that reciting jargon proves expertise.

## F. Use one coherent metaphor, with explicit limits

The archive idea was already in our baseline. The revised form is shredded identification guides mixed in a pile. It maps:

- several guides → several biological contributors;
- a scrap → short partial information;
- a readable scrap → possible identification;
- proximity in the pile → **not** proof of proximity between the organisms.

The later broken sentence stays inside the same information-loss family. Do not introduce memory palaces, time machines, resurrection blueprints, crime scenes and magic vaults as five more competing models. The ending explicitly states that the ground has no literal memory.

Metaphor test: list what the comparison does **not** imply. If a visual creates the false implication faster than narration corrects it, redesign the visual.

## G. Build local tension through a changing interpretation

Allowed sources of tension here: recognizable animal/unrecognizable evidence; protective binding/difficult extraction; damaged sequence/possible authentication aid; rich reconstruction/incomplete observation. Each comes from an actual methodological contrast.

Rejected sources: fake investigator dialogue, a last-second machine rescue, claims a result was suppressed, false dinosaur possibilities, an invented probability of identification, or a narrator repeatedly saying the next fact will change everything.

Do not equate frequent cuts with progress. A three-second shot may continue the same action while a longer rhetorical phase develops. Attention is not guaranteed by a countdown, a random zoom, a loud sound or an arbitrary mystery every thirty seconds.

## H. Write for a voice, then author the visual chain

Use short statements to land a contrast and longer connected sentences to explain it. Read aloud without music; remove clauses that cannot be followed on one hearing. Do not imitate a competitor's cadence or make speech faster to hide unclear writing.

For every voiced phrase, author these production fields **after** measured narration exists:

`phrase ID | measured start/end | visible subject | action verb | incoming spatial anchor | outgoing motion | evidence/illustration label | audio cue | shot duration`

Standing film rules still apply: each shot at most three seconds; meaningful moving 3D objects and rigs; no image slideshow; no repeated proof clip or generic forest filler. An animation instruction is not an implemented scene. The master currently supplies block-level action contracts, not a finished phrase-level shot list.

Transition tests:

- The outgoing action motivates the arrival: material falls into a sediment layer, the camera follows it rather than teleporting to an unrelated graphic.
- The viewer has one persistent orientation cue: fragment identity, light direction, physical location or motion vector.
- A label change, recolour or zoom alone does not count as a new world or action.
- Music and SFX support contact, scale or a resolved question; they do not cover missing logic. Never fabricate a scientific recording as recovered ancient sound.

## I. Translation is a semantic contract

English is canonical. Translate the same claim order, uncertainty, questions, contrast and ending into easy Urdu-English. Proper names and ordinary technical loan words may remain English. Two million years becomes بیس لاکھ سال, not two lakh.

Review each pair against: subject/taxon, number/date, mechanism, may/can qualifier, negation, contrast, and payoff. A translation may use a different natural sentence shape; it may not become a separate more sensational story. Run a numerical/ID check, then a human semantic read. Automated presence checks are not independent bilingual quality assurance.

Never reuse the old Urdu audio after changing the words. Generate fresh speech at a natural rate, measure duration, align actual phrases, and adjust the visual timeline. If Urdu runs longer than the intended English film, disclose that measured duration. Do not silently time-compress it.

## J. Release gates, not invented quality scores

1. **Evidence gate:** every material scientific claim resolves to a source and the visual restrictions are explicit.
2. **Comprehension gate:** a cold reader can explain what was found, how, and what was not found. No actual cold-reader test has been run on this rewrite yet.
3. **Promise gate:** the opening's two questions and title are answered; no unsupported current record or resurrection promise.
4. **Voice gate:** natural fresh recordings measured and listened to; not currently completed for v2.
5. **Picture gate:** actual encoded sequence inspected for subject/action/timing and spatial continuity. The full film is not implemented.
6. **Sound/delivery gate:** inspect full playback, music/SFX and native 1080p output; source tests do not substitute for this.
7. **Learning gate after publication:** compare actual retention/drop points and viewer comprehension, without claiming causation from one upload. No YouTube upload is authorised by this workflow.

The new script is a testable editorial improvement. It is not a guarantee of virality, zero boredom, hypnosis or universal appeal.
