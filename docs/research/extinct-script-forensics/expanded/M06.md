### Sequential microscope
1. A Triceratops beside a river makes a planetary event followable through one creature and one place.
2. Tuesday, weather, and a precise countdown create immediacy and dramatic irony: the audience knows something the animal cannot.
3. Physical clues—iridium, crater evidence, and fish-associated spherules—are introduced to make the reconstruction feel investigatory.
4. Pre-impact ecology and the impactor's orbital backstory enlarge the stage while the river remains the emotional anchor.
5. Seconds at atmospheric entry are narrated with short action units. Time is a structural motif, not just a number in the title.
6. The shallow sulfur-rich target turns geography into causal misfortune. Location changes the outcome instead of remaining map decoration.
7. Crater formation, ejecta, and seismic waves scale outward in sequence. Mechanisms connect the spectacle rather than appearing as unrelated disasters.
8. Tanis fish become the local evidence window on a distant global event. A security-camera analogy makes the promise of temporal precision memorable—and risky.

10. Radiant heat, fire, water, and burrows create contrasting refuges. The cast widens while the viewer retains a stable spatial question: where can life persist?
11. Tsunamis supply a water-based threat after water was offered as refuge. A solution is not permanent protection.
12. Atmospheric aerosols shift the killer from impact to lost sunlight. The food chain becomes the connecting system behind delayed deaths.
13. The move from hours to years changes the pace and the threat from sudden violence to starvation. The extinction's meaning broadens.
14. Ferns, marine food webs, and survivors show that consequences are ecological, not merely a montage of dead dinosaurs.
15. A much later recovery jump changes mood to inheritance and a mammal-centered future.
16. The closing section introduces planetary defense, nuclear-winter analogy, and resilience. These are three relevance extensions after the historical answer.
17. The original river/animal/evidence imagery returns. The ending answers emotionally through continuity of life and the surviving clue.

### Narrative DNA and small-detail functions
- **Engine:** expanding clock plus recurring local witness.
- **Sentence rhythm:** short statements and repeated negatives create grave certainty. This is visible in the text; it does not prove a particular voice cadence or soundtrack.
- **Open loop:** when will the animal's apparent refuge fail? The audience can follow this even when the physical scales are difficult.
- **Metaphors:** camera, oven, ceiling, and food-chain failure translate remote physics into familiar functions.
- **Ending risk:** multiple contemporary lessons after the main payoff can create an extended tail; no retention evidence shows whether this happened here.

### What not to copy
The weekday, exact local weather, individual animal actions, every-species two-year death schedule, 300,000-year atmospheric clearing, and every-modern-mammal-lineage-within-ten-million-years assertions should not be presented as established reconstructions. The script itself alternates aerosol effects lasting decades with a vastly longer clearing account. Claims of no surviving dinosaurs ignore birds unless explicitly restricted to non-avian dinosaurs. Fahrenheit temperature *changes* also require careful checking. These are serious scientific warning signs, not tiny editorial preferences.

### Original application to Greenland
A recurring real sample can anchor global/deep-time explanation as effectively as an invented individual animal. Use a labeled imagined reconstruction only after explaining its evidential limits. Keep one present-day implication, not three new conclusions after the core answer.
