# Eleven additional publisher-script microscopes

These original analyses use actual PBS public transcript prose. The API/caption collector independently preserved **4,129 cue derivatives** over **10,670 publisher-duration seconds** (2:57:50), not complete competitor scripts. Automated questions/keywords are not human semantic labels. Ordered beats below are analyst-defined narrative units; they are not exact caption or edit boundaries.

**Important selection distinction:** these are craft/science comparators, not eleven newly verified viral YouTube uploads. Some are shorter non-Short episodes; five are 25–26-minute programs. YouTube title matches may be related or differently edited editions. Public PBS runtime and YouTube runtime are deliberately not conflated. The 2026 long programs have no validated like-for-like million-view match in this packet. Their value is long-form construction, not a fabricated viral credential.

## P01 — We Were Wrong About The Dodo

Source: https://www.pbs.org/video/we-were-wrong-about-the-dodo-w98vvk/

Coverage: **complete main public transcript** (page chunks 2–3). Caption derivatives: key `dodo`, 130 cues; publisher 436 s. Related YouTube candidate `Juci-kAqjes`, 1,177,317 views, PT8M52S, has a different title and duration: do not attach its views to the exact PBS cut.

### Ordered script microscope
1. The familiar stupid-bird stereotype is established as the belief to overturn. Recognition makes an obscure historical correction immediately relevant.
2. The fact that extinction was not recognized promptly makes the human observers unreliable, shifting blame from the bird to the record.
3. The 2005 bone discovery is promised before the older history is unpacked. The audience knows why the historical detour is worth following.
4. A sequence of early sightings and later assessments exposes how little direct evidence supported the inherited picture.
5. The swamp bone deposit changes the investigation from caricatures and reports to many physical specimens.
6. Growth marks and environmental evidence make survival under difficult conditions an inference from remains, not an adjective attached to the bird.
7. Strong legs and brain evidence directly answer the opening stereotype. Details are selected because they can change the audience's judgment.
8. DNA-related pigeon affinity and island ecology explain why flight loss can be a sensible local tradeoff.
9. The script asks a new question after solving the first: if it was well adapted, why did it disappear?
10. Ground nesting, reproductive exposure, and introduced animals supply the mismatch. A successful adaptation becomes vulnerable under changed conditions.
11. The ending reframes extinction as ecological disruption rather than proof of stupidity, with de-extinction as a further chapter rather than the historical answer.

**Narrative DNA:** reputation trial → missing evidence → new material → trait reinterpretation → changed-environment explanation. A promised later discovery keeps the historical chronology from feeling aimless. The same body is first judged, then understood.

**Risks:** one discovery should not erase all uncertainty about behavior; future de-extinction is not demonstrated resurrection. Avoid making extinction sound inevitable from one reproductive trait alone.

**Greenland transfer:** use the misconception of DNA as an intact recoverable creature as the belief to revise. Resolve it with what a damaged fragment can actually do, not with a scolding disclaimer before the audience has a reason to care.

## P02 — The Second Time Sponges Took Over The World

Source: https://www.pbs.org/video/the-second-time-sponges-took-over-the-world-igqybm/

Coverage: **complete main public transcript** (page chunks 2–3). Key `sponges`, 128 cues; publisher 484 s. No confidently matched YouTube view total is assigned.

### Ordered script microscope
1. A fossil community from 2017 is introduced as something that should not have been thriving after a mass extinction. The contradiction precedes the explanation.
2. Sponges are an intentionally unimposing protagonist. Their apparent simplicity makes dominance surprising.
3. A 2015 discovery initially looks exceptional; later findings change it into a pattern. The sequence dramatizes replication, not just discovery.
4. Seven sites spread over a large distance expand the evidence from one lucky patch to a geographically broader question.
5. The question becomes why sponges, rather than merely whether they were there.
6. Environmental tolerance supplies one part of the answer. The winner fits the conditions rather than being universally superior.
7. Filtering food and reduced competition supply additional mechanisms. Success is assembled from several interacting advantages.
8. Other extinction episodes test whether the response is unique or recurring.
9. A modern El Niño example supplies a recognizable analogue without requiring the audience to imagine another deep-time world from scratch.
10. Sponge activity is reframed as ecosystem engineering. A survivor can help make recovery possible for other organisms.
11. The ending connects the unglamorous animal to a larger inherited ecosystem and returns value to the underestimated protagonist.

**Narrative DNA:** impossible-looking exception → replication → mechanism → ecological consequence. The paper/discovery dates are structural steps in changing confidence, not a decorative bibliography.

**Risks:** repeated fossil occurrences do not establish uniform global abundance or the same mechanism in every extinction. Modern analogy is not identity.

**Greenland transfer:** the tree/plant community must not become filler after the mastodon. Reframe plants as evidence-bearing and world-making actors: they change what sort of landscape the animal signal can belong to. Escalate explanatory power, not body size.

## P03 — Our Most Mysterious Extinct Cousins

Source: https://www.pbs.org/video/our-most-mysterious-extinct-cousins-jj1kmf/

Coverage: **complete main public transcript** (page chunks 2–3). Key `cousins`, 153 cues; publisher 581 s. Same-title YouTube candidate `HE2MmAGUgnw`: 1,220,528 views, PT12M21S. Identity is a strong episode lead, not proof that the cuts/timings match.

### Ordered script microscope
1. A counterfactual human evolutionary path creates personal relevance without immediately claiming these cousins were our direct ancestors.
2. The 1938 tooth discovery makes the mystery begin with an object, not a taxonomy lecture.
3. Familiar food/chocolate scale helps translate unusually large teeth into a bodily image.
4. Three Paranthropus species establish a successful, varied lineage rather than a single odd skull.
5. Powerful chewing anatomy supports the intuitive specialist interpretation. The audience is allowed to form a plausible explanation.
6. Microwear and isotope results then complicate that explanation. A strong-looking jaw does not tell the entire story of actual food use.
7. Tool associations introduce another attractive explanation, but the makers are not securely identified. The script resists turning proximity into authorship.
8. Bovid/habitat evidence from a newer study moves the problem from individual anatomy to environmental context.
9. Climate and specialization are offered as a possible extinction explanation, not an observed final act.
10. The ending keeps the vanished lineage meaningful without declaring our own survival inevitable or superior.

**Narrative DNA:** human counterfactual → object → intuitive model → independent evidence contradicts it → wider ecological model → honest unresolved ending. Competing evidence creates the suspense normally supplied by villains.

**Risks:** distinguish chewing capacity from habitual diet, associated tools from confirmed makers, and a plausible explanation from a settled extinction cause. These distinctions contrast usefully with the certainty in M08's inspected fight passages.

**Greenland transfer:** make one expected interpretation fail because another method adds information. Reference matching is not the same thing as reconstructing an animal's complete ecology. A well-placed correction can be a reveal rather than a momentum-killing footnote.

### Ordered script microscope
1. An old cave lion stalks a forest environment, inviting the audience to assume it is the hunter.
2. The predator/prey reversal makes the familiar human relative surprising before its anatomy is discussed.
3. Apex success is paired with a possible cost: the trait that made the lineage successful may have contributed to vulnerability.
4. Straight-tusked elephant cut evidence and older-male selection turn impressive hunting into a testable behavior argument.
5. Caloric reward and coordination give the prey size social significance. Large food packages imply problems of processing and sharing as well as killing.

8. The question switches from how they succeeded to why they vanished.

**Narrative DNA:** role reversal → demonstrated competence → success/vulnerability paradox → competing extinction explanations → evidence-object return. It refuses the simple equation of predator with invincibility.

**Risks:** extinctions have multiple proposed drivers; diet/technology contrasts can be oversimplified. A reconstructed ambush is not directly observed behavior. Do not convert the narrated scene into an eyewitness event.

**Greenland transfer:** show a magnificent animal, but let the more interesting question be how we know. Keep the physical clue as the final point of return. Handle caveats beside the claim that needs them.

## P05 — When the Rainforests Collapsed

Source: https://www.pbs.org/video/when-the-rainforests-collapsed-tholej/

Coverage: **complete main public transcript** (page chunks 4–5; earlier chunks were navigation, not script). Key `rainforests`, 212 cues; publisher 460 s. Same-title YouTube lead `sFHTA8dKceI`: 753,057 views, PT8M33S, **below the packet's million-view threshold**.

### Ordered script microscope
1. A small Hylonomus in a tree-stump setting creates an intimate scale for a habitat-level catastrophe.
2. The opening's local creature becomes a bridge to the rainforest-collapse problem, not a protagonist with an invented documented biography.
3. Coal thinning and plant/spore evidence make a disappearing habitat legible through the rock record.
4. Glacial and volcanic explanations introduce causal possibilities rather than reducing the change to one unexplained disaster word.
5. Plant turnover makes the new world materially different: changing moisture and reproduction alter which plants prosper.
6. The story then tests how habitat connectivity changed for animals.
7. One interpretation emphasizes fragmentation; another emphasizes more continuous dry habitats. The alternatives are explained as different routes, not silently blended.
8. Both predict an advantage for amniotes, supplying a robust conclusion even while part of the mechanism remains debated.
9. Eggs/reproductive independence and moisture-retaining bodies explain why the same shift hurts some lineages and helps others.
10. Permian body forms show the consequence of the opportunity rather than becoming a separate catalog.
11. The viewer is identified as an amniote. The tiny opening creature's world is connected to the audience without treating humans as the planned endpoint.

**Narrative DNA:** tiny local witness → environmental evidence → competing spatial models → shared outcome → personal inheritance. The most valuable device is agreement beneath uncertainty.

**Risks:** a common outcome under two hypotheses does not prove either hypothesis. Be precise about ancestry and broad physiological generalizations.

**Greenland transfer:** separate what is robust (mixed community evidence in sediments) from what depends on transport, time averaging, and local habitat assumptions. Uncertainty in exact placement need not erase the larger ecological discovery.

## P06 — That Time Oxygen Almost Killed Everything

Source: https://www.pbs.org/video/that-time-oxygen-almost-killed-everything-cnzweg/

Coverage: **complete main public transcript** (late page chunk 4 and chunk 5). Key `oxygen`, 84 cues; publisher 285 s. This is a shorter non-Short comparator, **not a 15–40-minute study**. Same-title YouTube lead `qERdL8uHSgI`: 2,017,737 views, PT5M44S.

### Ordered script microscope
1. Oxygen, normally associated with life, is recast as a threat. A familiar beneficial substance carries the paradox.
2. An older anaerobic world provides the baseline needed to understand that reversal.
3. Photosynthesis creates food while releasing a waste product. A normal biological process becomes the causal driver.
4. Oceanic iron reacts with the new oxygen. Banded iron formations supply a visible surviving trace of otherwise invisible chemistry.
5. The balance among microbes changes; the waste product harms organisms adapted to the previous conditions.
6. Oxygen accumulation shifts from water to atmosphere, changing the scale of the story.
7. Changes to greenhouse gases connect biology to climate. The crisis escalates through a causal chain, not through an unrelated new hazard.
8. Glaciation threatens even the organisms whose activity helped initiate the change: the winner's waste can rebound on the winner.
9. The script acknowledges that the full damage is unknown before making its broad extinction framing.
10. Surviving life, oxygen, and ozone change the interpretation again: a catastrophe for one biosphere helps open possibilities for another.
11. The ending returns to the opening paradox and the audience's existence.

**Narrative DNA:** familiar benefit → context reversal → mechanism → self-inflicted consequence → long-term reversal. The unusual villain is a relationship between organism and environment, not a malicious animal.

**Risks:** an older popular account should not be the final authority for extinction magnitude, methane/CO2 chronology, duration of glacial episodes, or the precise chemical description of ozone. Treat the account's explanatory compression as something to recheck, not to inherit verbatim.

**Greenland transfer:** minerals can initially seem like destructive burial, then become protection for information. Let that reversal emerge from the physical mechanism rather than simply calling the dirt miraculous.
