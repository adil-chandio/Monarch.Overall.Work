# Stickman-style experiment — research first, owner approval before production

## Decision and scope

- The owner retained V9 as an enjoyable existing film. Do not modify or replace it.
- New experiment: one minute of the approved English film as genuinely rendered moving stickman-style animation, not panned images or a slideshow.
- No new experimental movie, image-based proof or production render until research is presented and the owner explicitly approves the treatment.
- This directory initially records discovery and access feasibility, **not completed forensic viewing**.

## Cohort target and screening

Discover broadly (16 search seeds, up to 25 results each; deduplicate and use current public API statistics), then screen a target of at least 24–36 accessible actual videos across multiple creators. This is a target, not an assertion of completed access. Limit creator concentration. Include million-view successes and lower-reach contrasts so popularity is not mistaken for proof that any single technique caused success.

Keep rendered 3D stick figures, 2D line animation, 2.5D composites, gameplay, tutorials, compilations and unrelated results in separate bins. A title mentioning 3D/Blender or a million views is not proof of visual style. Use 2D comparators for acting/timing only; do not mislabel them as rendered 3D references. Public views are snapshots, not actual audience geography, retention curves or RPM.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. API metadata: ID, author, exact URL, timestamp, duration, views, query and selection rationale.
2. Acquisition: successful public footage access, file hash, decoded duration, dimensions and cadence; explicit failure reasons where inaccessible. Metadata APIs do not supply frame-level video.
3. Mechanical full decode: per-frame timestamps, changes, duplicate/hold candidates, shot-boundary candidates, sampled motion and audio envelope. Automated cut/difference scores are not human interpretation or evidence of the author's software.
4. Actual visual examination: full-timeline scene coverage, consecutive frames around actions, and native-resolution checks of anticipation, contact and aftermath. Record which frames/windows were actually examined; do not describe sampling as every-frame human inspection.
5. Sound examination: separate measured peaks/onsets from actually heard and identified foley/music. No listening claims based only on a waveform.
6. Synthesis: observed craft → precise example/timecode → alternative explanation → applicability to our science minute → production test and acceptance criterion.

## Forensic matrix

For each accessible eligible video document: opening promise; cause/action/consequence progression; actor intent; silhouette and screen-space readability; proportion and rig design; line thickness/3D materials; pose timing, anticipation, holds, spacing, acceleration, follow-through and settling; foot/hand contact, grasp pivots and prop identity; gravity-like trajectories and reaction; camera motivation, staging, depth, occlusion and continuity; light/shadow/color hierarchy; graphics/text interaction; match cuts, ellipses and transition logic; sound-source alignment and quiet space; narrative escalation, payoff and reset/loop. Record weaknesses as well as strengths.

No assertion of flawless exhaustive understanding, a guaranteed viral formula, the original rig/software, or unseen creator decisions. No automatic production from a generic technique list.

## Approval package (not yet issued)

- Audited source inventory with counts at each eligibility/access/review stage and exclusions.
- Broad comparative study plus deeper consecutive-frame action analyses from several different creators, not just two or three clips.
- An original 60-second treatment with selected approved narration boundaries, beat/action/prop/camera/sound plans, visual design direction, science safeguards, real rig/contact tests and explicit limitations.
- Ask for owner approval **after** the evidence-backed study and treatment; only then build the one-minute rendered experiment.

Public footage is reference material, not permission to copy characters, choreography, branding, score or scenes. Do not publish complete third-party movies or the owner's private references. Keep bulk media in ignored cache, not Git.
