## Correction 1 — my lead title was the wrong mechanism

I recommended `This Cub Still Has Its Fur After 35,000 Years`, built on a contradiction hook.

Measured effect on this topic (n=29, R²=0.045 — weak, treat as a prior):

| pattern | effect |
|---|---|
| a number in the title | **+45.3 %** |
| a question in the title | **−94.3 %** |
| a shouted word | −51.0 % |
| +10 characters of length | +1.3 % (nothing) |

Top-decile title composition: number present **43 %**, curiosity gap **14 %**, question form
**0 %**, you-form **0 %**.

So the evidence supports a **statement with a number**, and it *punishes* the question form —
which is the opposite of what I carried over from the longform mammoth package, where
"Who Killed…" was the whole argument. That transfer was wrong, and this run is why the
package needs a measured version rather than an inherited one.

**Also note the winner is not the one I would have picked.** 357,245 views and 0.85 %
like-rate for *"Sabretooth Kitten found in Permafrost"*; but *"A Sabertooth Mummy!"* takes
**12.36 %** like-rate at 78,634 views, and *"easily the coolest discovery of 2024"* takes
**12.27 %** at 1.39M. Low views with a very high like-rate is the profile of a video that
found a small, devoted audience — worth more to a new channel than a big number with 1 %.

## Correction 2 — the topic is more saturated than the longform niche was

The engine's verdict on this topic is **NO, 39.0, confidence 0.85**: *"the numbers do not
support a day of production."* Its two objections:

- winnability **13** — a few videos own it
- fresh-share-30d **0.07** — almost nobody uploaded on this in 30 days, *"could be satiated
  or dead"*

That is a **production** verdict on the topic, not on our film. Our Short already exists and
is already made, so the useful reading is: do not expect the topic to carry it, and do not
launch it as a cold topic play. Launch it as a *specificity* play against a crowded field.

## What survives, and what changes

  framings (12.36 %, 12.27 %), and the word signal ranks `tiger`, `ice`, `tooth`, `frozen`,
  `cat`, `found` at the top.
- "Real specimen, not a living animal" stays. The sample contains a video claiming *"Caught
  on Security Camera"* at 0.69 % like-rate — the audience punishes the fake-alive framing.
- The three posters survive. Poster A already leads with the number.

**Changes:**
- **Title should be a statement with a number, not a possessive hook, and never a question.**
  See `PASTE-THIS-SHORTS.md` §TITLE, now reordered.
- **#Shorts folklore** already corrected in that file; unchanged here.
- **The like-rate benchmark to beat is ~3 %**, because that is what the 13.5M winner holds.
  12 % exists in this niche at small scale, so 3 % is a floor, not a ceiling.

## What this does not prove

30 videos, one search, one order (relevance), R² 0.045. The title coefficients are a prior
for a decision, never proof of causation — the report says so itself. What it *does* fix is
using a longform mechanism in a Shorts niche without checking, which is the error this file
documents.
