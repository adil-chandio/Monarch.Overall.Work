# PASTE THIS — Saber-Toothed Cub Short (51 s, 1080×1920)

Every field here is ready to paste. Two things make Shorts different from the longform
package and both are load-bearing, so they are stated before the copy:

1. **A Short's thumbnail is not what the Shorts feed shows.** In the vertical feed the video
   plays immediately; the poster is only seen on shelves, search results, channel pages and
   the watch page. So the thumbnail is *not* the first thing a swipe-decision depends on —
   the **first frame** is, and this film's frame 0 is already a full-brightness card
   (measured: 41.2 luma at frame 0, no fade from black). The poster matters for the *second*
   audience: people who see the Short on a shelf rather than in the feed.
2. **Custom Shorts thumbnails are still gated.** Since 24 July 2026 YouTube lets Partner
   Program creators upload a custom image for a Short in Studio on desktop; access is
   rolling out and Help Centre wording has lagged the blog. If the upload control is missing,
   fall back to the frame picker in the **mobile app** — and pick a frame, not the motion-blur
   one YouTube auto-selects.

## HASHTAGS

Placed at the end of the description, five of them:

    #Shorts #Homotherium #SaberToothedCat #Paleontology #ExtinctAnimals

Reasoning, and where the common advice is wrong: several 2026 guides say to "always include
#Shorts to signal the format". That is folklore — YouTube classifies a Short by aspect ratio
and duration (3 minutes or less, square or taller), not by a hashtag. `#Shorts` is included
because it costs nothing and some surfaces still surface it, **not** because it does
anything for distribution. The four content hashtags are the ones doing work.

Three of these render above the title. Keep the count at five; past 15 hashtags in total,
YouTube ignores all of them.

## TAGS (the hidden field)

The budget is 500 characters **shared across every tag** — not 500 per tag. Keep it to
keyword phrases rather than a keyword dump.

`homotherium`, `sabertooth` and `scimitar cat` — do not add a dozen variants of them.

## PINNED COMMENT

    Three weeks old. Thirty-five thousand years dead. Still furry.

    The detail that unsettles me is not the fur — it is the ears. Smaller than a lion cub's,
    with a powerful neck and broad front paws. Those are things a skeleton physically cannot
    tell you, which is why this mummy mattered so much when it was described in 2024.

    But it is partial. A full living appearance still needs reconstruction, so what you are
    seeing is preservation, not an animal brought back.

    Source: Lopatin et al., Scientific Reports (2024), open access.
