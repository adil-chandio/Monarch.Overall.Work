# PACKAGE — "The Last Mammoths Recovered. Then They Vanished."

Research-stage packaging for the long-form script in `SCRIPT-V1.md`. Nothing is rendered, voiced, published, or scheduled. No CTR/view forecast is offered; no A/B test has been run.

---

## 1. Title — one dominant promise

**Primary (recommended):**

> **The Last Mammoths Recovered. Then They Vanished.**

Why this one and not the alternatives: it states a paradox, it is fully paid by the film (the 2024 recovery finding is the spine, and the ending resolves the paradox honestly), and it does not promise a killer the evidence cannot deliver. It also does not reuse the pyramid hook, which the earlier draft leaned on and which competitors already own.

**Alternates (if the primary tests flat, in order):**

1. *The Last Mammoths: A Cold Case With No Final Witness* — leads with the unsolved-mystery frame; slightly less concrete, stronger curiosity.
2. *We Have DNA From the Last Mammoths. We Still Don't Know How They Died.* — strongest honesty hook, longest title, weakest mobile truncation.
3. *The Mammoths That Recovered — and Vanished Anyway* — shortest; loses "last" (the island specificity).

**Explicitly rejected titles:** anything with "killed by", "the virus that killed the mammoths", "mammoths were brought back" (a cloned-mammoth hook is a different film), and anything pairing mammoths with pyramids (ubiquitous, and a second promise that competes with the main one).

## 3. Description (draft)

```
The last mammoths on Earth lived on an Arctic island for thousands of years. Their genomes say the population recovered. And then it was gone.

We took the four best explanations for how the last mammoths died — a 2017 genome study, a 2019 climate-and-water study, the human arrival evidence, and a 2024 analysis of twenty-one mammoth genomes — and tested what each one can actually support. The honest answer is better than a fake culprit: every witness told a true story, and none of them was there for the last three hundred years.

Sources:
• Dehasque et al., Cell (2024), DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2024.05.033
• Rogers & Slatkin, PLoS Genetics (2017), DOI 10.1371/journal.pgen.1006601
• Arppe et al., Quaternary Science Reviews (2019), DOI 10.1016/j.quascirev.2019.105884
• Guinet et al., Cell (2025), DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2025.08.003

Reconstructions in this video are original illustrations, not photographs or laboratory footage. Timings and population figures are scientific estimates; effective population size is a genetic estimate, not a head count.

#mammoth #extinctlife #paleontology #wolongmammoth #science
```

## 7. Kill criteria — what would make us abandon this package

1. **No final hero beat.** If the story cannot end on a concrete, earned image (the empty slot / the missing three hundred years) without a fake reveal, the premise is wrong and the package is dropped.
2. **The evidence cannot carry the middle.** If Chapter 5 cannot be explained without either a fake "twist" or a jargon wall, the chapter is rewritten or removed — the film does not survive on being dense.
3. **The competitor overlap becomes total.** If a frame-level comparison against the closest existing coverage shows the same sequence of beats with no independent demonstration, the package is dropped in favour of a different topic rather than defended.
4. **The microbe section cannot be kept honest in under 90 seconds.** Cut it. It is context, never the payoff.
5. **Thumbnail/title A/B does not clear the channel's own median.** If the package tests below the channel's recent median impressions, it is not re-cut indefinitely; it is shelved and a new topic is researched.
