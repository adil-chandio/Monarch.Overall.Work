## 1. The creative promise: a life, a clue, and an unanswered ending

We open with a **living reconstruction**, not a diagram. A mammoth feels weight, wind, ground and the herd around it. A footprint carries us into its island. A bone and other evidence later let us look back. Throughout the film each witness changes the audience’s understanding of that world:

1. **Emotional question:** How did a population last so long on an isolated island?
2. **Surprise:** The larger genetic study indicates a severe bottleneck **and** modelled recovery; neither removes genetic strain.
3. **Tension:** Can traces of genes, diet and people tell us why it vanished? Each clue has a limit.
4. **Payoff:** Distinguish supported findings, plausible hypotheses and unknowns. No invented killer, no invented death scene.

This is an editorial design for attention, not a claim that viewers will be hypnotised, that retention is guaranteed, or that every viewer reacts the same way.

### Recurring visual anchors—not repetitive endcards

- **Footprint:** first a living animal’s contact with snow, later a physical trace in sediment, finally marks on an open landscape. One different storytelling function each time; not a cutaway on every chapter.
- **Horizon / water boundary:** animal-scale shoreline → geographic isolation → open horizon near the ending. Does not imply the animal saw a historically exact coastline.
- **Evidence in human hands:** preparation, comparison, controls; research activity is reconstructed, never passed off as footage of the original studies.
- **The gap:** only introduced as an absence in the **2024 genomic dataset**, and contrasted with other evidence. Not “no data of any kind.”

### The viewer should always know

Where am I? What changed in this shot? Why does it matter now? Is this an observation, a reconstruction, or a hypothesis? If a shot cannot answer at least one of these, it does not get screen time.

## 2. The continuity system: no arbitrary jump from object to object

**A. Start and end poses.** Each transition is designed as a pair: outgoing subject/direction/brightness/focal distance, incoming matching element, and the next scene’s orientation. A new location gets a brief visual anchor. A match cut suggests a relationship; it does not pretend the two spaces are physically continuous.

**B. Sound leads picture.** In a J-cut, a new location’s wind, brush or room tone starts shortly *before* its image. In an L-cut the previous sound briefly continues *after* a picture change. Exact frame overlap is set by listening; no mandatory whoosh or sudden silence at every cut.

**C. Change what is happening, not just the lens.** A sample is handled or compared; an animal tests vegetation or transfers weight; a stream flows; a source-verified evidence mark changes or is interpreted. The camera should reveal an action, not manufacture activity by orbiting a static prop.

**D. Rhythm is editorial, not an automatic timer.** Typically reveal a new action/viewpoint within about 3–6 seconds; hold longer for a readable discovery if the image itself develops. Every new angle must expose fresh action, scale, evidence or emotion. A quiet sustained shot is allowed. No fixed “cut every two seconds,” speed-up, shake, excessive dissolves or synthetic transitions.

**E. Match visual weight:** No jump from large bright DNA on black to a tiny flat icon. Spatially relate the subject first; keep a dark-to-light path and similar motion across the edit. Captions and source labels must not obstruct the subject.

### Five concrete transition pairs

| Connection | Outgoing frame/action | Incoming frame/action | Why it flows / truth guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening: mammoth → island | Foot pushes snow diagonally screen-left; camera rises behind shoulder | Cut to a wider coast with herd still travelling screen-left; wind is already audible | Continues direction and sound, but the wider view is a different camera/shot, not an impossible single take. |
| Island → evidence | Camera follows a footprint downward; last frame holds an oval in snow | Modern excavation: brush reveals **a bone surface**, not the same footprint; brush sound enters early | Shape/composition echo carries the thought “trace remains”; does not claim one event turned into a sample. |
| Comparison → genetic mechanism | Scientist brings a sample into focus; camera follows instrument light into a short schematic | A labelled, simplified visual shows a missing section; match colour/light from instrument | Clearly an explanatory change of scale, not actual microscope footage or a real genome sequence. |
| Environment → humans | Stream runs toward frame edge and audio recedes | Surveyor’s brush moves in the same direction over sediment; dry brush texture replaces water | Same motion, different evidence; no implication that humans and mammoths met on Wrangel. |
| Genomic record → unknown | Source-verified timeline reaches its youngest genome point | Camera follows its line into dim empty space; another evidence type remains separately visible | A gap in one dataset, not a fictional erased history or an absence of all evidence. |

## 4. Visual, camera and animal craft: what “better” actually requires

### Character performance

- Build or properly license a detailed, rigged **woolly mammoth**; verify origin, permission, attribution and whether animation/adaptation are allowed. Do not relabel an elephant or a mastodon as a validated mammoth.
- Four limbs with stance contact and articulated joints; hip/shoulder load transfer, centre-of-mass momentum, deliberate acceleration/braking and foot-ground collision. Feet must not slide, penetrate terrain or pass through rocks.
- Flexible trunk with reach/lag/contact, restrained eyes and ears, head/tusk inertia, fur groom moving with body and wind; plausible vegetation/snow response. No fixed anatomical motion is presented as directly observed mammoth behaviour. Living elephants are imperfect references.
- Choreograph several readable actions—walk, stop, trunk exploration, forage, turn and regroup—rather than endlessly walking past the camera. Body scale, tusk shapes, age/sex and herd relationships are researched reconstructions, not claims about a sampled individual.

### Environments and camera

- Design separately readable mainland, Wrangel coast/tundra, St. Paul and modern research/survey settings; use licensed/reference-checked geology, climate, vegetation, maps and historical shoreline approximations. Label reconstruction and time compression. No invented exact route or prehistoric map as surveyed fact.
- A small palette system: cold blue/warm coat for life, restrained neutral research light for evidence, distinct but natural environmental hues; controlled contrast for phone viewing. No black void as a default science scene.
- Mix close, medium, wide and animal-level moving shots; screen direction, horizon, focal length, exposure and movement are checked across the edit. Avoid constant orbit, floating camera, random lens flare, fake zoom and motion blur masking poor animation.
- Research sequences feature hands, tools and process appropriate to a clearly labelled **reconstruction**, not an assertion that these were the authors’ exact lab procedures or specimens. 3D surfaces, textures and limited data overlays are fine; no image-backed slides, still plates or endlessly repeated cards.

### The visual-psychology test

Before any shot is accepted, answer: **(1)** What changes within the frame? **(2)** What question does it raise or answer? **(3)** Why is the next shot the next shot? **(4)** Is the intended meaning readable without the VO? **(5)** Does the image accidentally overclaim? If the answer is “it looks cool” only, redesign it. This is a creative review test, not a retention guarantee.

## 5. Narration, music and SFX: one continuous story

- **Voice:** Existing selected English voice remains at its generated speed. Listen for pronunciation, stress and pacing; retake corrected text rather than editing transcript alone. Captions need human correction and word-by-word sync; viewer controls/phone safe areas must not cover them.
- **Score concept:** A restrained acoustic/organic palette: a sparse low string/cello-like texture, subtle upper harmonics and occasional human-scale tonal movement. Reversal gets a warm harmonic lift; uncertainty returns to less resolved harmony. Instruments and exact arrangement must be actually sourced/created and reviewed—this is a proposal, not a completed score.
- **No rejected sound:** Avoid regular rotor-like throb, machine pulses, sub drones and constant rhythmic ticking. Do not reuse the earlier rejected mechanical bed. No arbitrary cinematic booms or whooshes at cuts.
- **SFX anchored to visible cause:** Contact on snow, wind moving grasses, water over stone, careful glove/tool handling, rain on ice only when relevant. Use quiet atmospheric layers with natural variation, not loud loops; no sound effect implying an unobserved death.
- **Mix/QC:** Voice intelligibility first; planned J/L overlaps must not obscure it. Check music licence and attribution, narration/score masking, full decode, levels and true peak. Human review on headphones and phone speakers; measurements alone cannot prove quality.

## 6. Scientific & editorial guardrails

1. **FOXQ1:** 2017 authors predicted a satin-like coat; it was not directly observed and does not prove loss of wool, insulation or freezing.
2. **Effective population size:** Model parameter, not a headcount. Do not animate precisely 8 or 300 animals to represent it.
3. **2024 study:** Data indicate a bottleneck and modelled recovery, with persistent genetic effects. No final ten generations in that genomic dataset does not mean no other evidence; stability in sampled history does not establish the exact final trajectory.
4. **Isotopes:** C/N stability applies to studied specimens. Bedrock weathering and potentially affected water are interpretations; no claim of directly measured poisonous ancient water.
5. **Icing, human involvement, disease:** Plausible hypotheses, not confirmed death scenes. Label hypothetical weather before it occurs; do not portray a Wrangel hunt as observed. Earliest known human evidence does not prove first arrival.
6. **2025 microbial work:** Includes specimens from much earlier than the island population; pathogen relatives are not an identified Wrangel killer. Health implications remain uncertain.
7. **Graphics:** Verify values, temporal axes, samples and wording against original papers. Illustrative molecular scenes are labelled; no invented letter sequence presented as a real mammoth genome, no tiny unreadable scientific decoration.

## 7. Approval and production gates — plan, not a guarantee

1. **Approve this story direction first.** No render or script replacement before approval.
2. **Script lock:** compare entire original research draft with adaptation; correct science, list substantive inclusions/omissions, remove repetition and approve revised text. Then retake only changed narration and re-align subtitles. Final runtime will change if wording changes.
3. **Assets and style:** secure mammoth rig/landscapes, licence/provenance and animal/scene references; establish art, motion and render tests without treating them as a finished film. Current mammoth rig is crude, kinematic and not validated; existing simplified lab/molecular models do not meet this quality target.
4. **Performance, environment and editorial:** animate all scenes, physics/contact and interactions; match the edit and sound to approved narration; build source-verified data visuals.
5. **Review:** continuous picture and audio viewing; frame-level inspection of transitions, foot contact, collision, captions and source labels; independent science and human audience feedback where possible. Test phone playback and revise based on observable problems.
6. **Technical delivery:** render native 3D frames for the full film, encode and decode check, assess true peak and listening experience, document sources/rights and provide the GitHub link. Preserve prior masters separately. No YouTube publication without separate authorisation.

**Feasibility:** The current software renderer needed several minutes for a 30-second 720p test. A full film with detailed, believable character animation and multiple 3D environments requires substantially more asset work, animation, render capacity and review. Shorter cuts or automated tests cannot turn the existing prototypes into a premium documentary. The desired quality and audience response are goals to review, not guarantees.
