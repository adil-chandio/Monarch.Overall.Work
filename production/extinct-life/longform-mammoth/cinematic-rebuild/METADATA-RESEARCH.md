# The Last Mammoths — metadata package, and the evidence behind it

This is written against the **13:16 review reel** (`796.07 s`, captions aligned to
`full-script-v2/caption-data.json`), not against the script. Every timestamp below was
pulled from the reel's own captions, and every quote is verbatim from the narration.

Everything is measured against real videos in this niche, not against generic "best
practice" lists. Where a common claim failed to survive contact with the data, it says so.

**The fact that drives every choice:** the film is a **cold case with four witnesses and no
conviction**. Its own line at **12:16** is *"A possibility is not a verdict."* So the package
must promise an *investigation*, and deliver a *state of the evidence* — not a culprit. Any
title that promises a killer is a lie the film breaks at 11:29.

### What the sample actually says

**1. Length is not the tax people think — but the first thirty seconds are.**
The 40-minute WUFO film has the same subject and essentially the same claim as ours, and
reached 26,825 views in two months. The 9:42 Eons film on the *other* island reached
450,394 in roughly the same period. Length did not decide that; the opening did:

- WUFO opens on the strongest fact anyone has: *"Around 4,000 years ago, while the Great
  Pyramid of Giza was being completed, the last mammoths were still walking across a lonely
  island in the Arctic Ocean."* A real curiosity gap — buried under a title that names a
  **genre** ("Full Documentary") instead of asking a question.
- Eons opens: *"About 5,700 years ago, on a small isolated island about 480 km off the coast
  of Alaska, a herd of woolly mammoths crossed a treeless tundra. But these weren't just any
  mammoths."* Same shape, but its title says **Second-to-Last**, which is a smaller,
  stranger, more specific claim than "last".

**2. The winning mechanism in this niche is a case file, not a nature film.**
Getsetfly's 2.6M title is *Who Killed…*, and the video commits: its transcript opens "let's
begin the investigation just like a murder case, starting with the dead body" and spends the
runtime cross-examining climate, food and predators. Its 2.66 % like-rate is five times
WUFO's 0.52 % on a quarter of the length. Nobody who clicked felt cheated.

**3. Enormous numbers do not lift a video; specific ones do.**
Veritasium's 112.9M carries 96,000,000. Kurzgesagt's 23.2M carries a *duration* ("minute by
minute"), not a count. Eons' 28.1M _Why Megalodon (Definitely) Went Extinct_ carries no
number at all — its parenthesis is the hook: it concedes the debate, then picks a side. The
pattern is **an admission of uncertainty plus a refusal to stay uncertain**, not a stat.

**4. "Always use a face" is wrong for this genre, and the niche proves it.**
Thumbnail studies put faces at +38 % CTR and shock expressions at +45 %
(thumbnailcreator.com, 2026; alici.ai, 2026 — both aggregate rather than measure). The
best-performing thumbnail *in this niche* has no face, no mammoth, and no drama:

> **PBS Eons — The Second-to-Last Mammoths Ever**: a relief map of the Bering Strait, one
> small island highlighted, **a red arrow** pointing at it, and four words:
> *"MAMMOTHS SURVIVED LONGER HERE."* 450k views on a 9:42 film.

Faces work when the *channel is the person* (MrBeast, Veritasium). They do not transfer to an
unknown faceless channel's animal documentary, where the viewer has no relationship with the
face and it competes with the only thing that sells the click: **the animal**.

**5. Like-rate is the honest quality signal, and it is where we can win.**
0.52 % (WUFO) against 3.41 % (Eons) on the same material. A viewer who feels oversold
leaves; a viewer told something true and strange stays and presses. Our differentiator is
precisely what a cheap version cannot fake: the film says *"the diagnosis is still missing"*
rather than inventing a culprit. **The package must sell that**, because that is what keeps
the like-rate above 1.5 % — and a video that wins the click and loses the like-rate gets one
spike and dies.

## 2. The psychology, named

Six mechanisms, each already doing work in a title that won above. Nothing here is on the
list because it "feels" viral.

| mechanism | what it does | where it is proven in the sample |
|---|---|---|
| **Case-file frame** | turns an open scientific question into a closed social one — *someone* did it, and the viewer wants the name | Getsetfly 2.6M; Eons' whole catalogue is *"Why X (Definitely) Went Extinct"* |
| **Underdog specificity** | a smaller, stranger claim beats a bigger, vaguer one (*second-to-last* beats *last*) | Eons 450k vs WUFO 26k, same subject |
| **Conceded uncertainty** | admitting what is still unknown buys permission to be believed about the rest | Eons' "(Definitely)"; our film's own 12:16 |
| **Scale shock by proxy** | "4,000 years ago" only lands *beside something* — Egypt was already old | WUFO's best line, buried in voiceover instead of the title |
| **One unexplained object** | the eye cannot leave an unexplained mark: a red arrow, a dot on a timeline, a gap | Eons' thumbnail; our film's missing-genome gap has the same shape |
| **Herd, not individual** | a group reads as "something that happened to them"; one animal reads as a nature doc | the 2.6M title is plural; our best frames are herds |

**Deliberately not used:** shocked-face framing, "caught on camera" language, invented
numbers, all-caps bait with no payoff. Each raises the click and corrupts the one asset the
film has that the 2.6M video does not: **it survives a fact-check by a paleontologist and
still holds a general audience.**

## 3. Titles — ranked, with the reasoning attached

The browse surface truncates near **60–70 characters on mobile**, so the first clause must
carry the whole promise. Nothing here exceeds 70. Character counts are exact.

### The single recommendation

> **Who Killed The Last Mammoths?**
> `29 characters`

- It inherits the exact mechanism that produced **2,622,268 views** in this niche — while
  making a *smaller*, more specific claim (one island, one herd) than that video did, which
  is the Eons playbook applied to the Getsetfly frame.
- It promises an *investigation*, which the film delivers in full: four witnesses, a verdict
  table at 11:29, and the line *"a possibility is not a verdict"* at 12:16.
- 29 characters: whole on every surface, including the smallest short-shelf tile and the
  search snippet.
- It leaves the thumbnail free to spend its words on **time**, which the title does not.

### The rest of the shortlist, in launch order

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Who Killed The Last Mammoths?** | 29 | case file + specificity | none material; the film pays it off |
| 2 | **They Were Still Here 4,000 Years Ago** | 36 | scale shock by proxy | "still here" reads passive in search; needs the herd on the tile |
| 3 | **Four Witnesses. No Verdict.** | 27 | the film's own structure | abstract without the thumbnail; strongest possible *brand* title |
| 4 | **The Last Mammoths Didn't Die Out. They Were Trapped.** | 52 | belief reversal | "trapped" is the isolation argument, not the verdict — only if the cut leads with the sea-level chapter |

### Titles rejected on purpose

- *"The Last Mammoths: The Final Days of an Ice Age Giant | Full Documentary"* — literally
  the 26,825-view title. It names a genre instead of asking a question, spends 12 characters
  on a pipe and 16 on self-description, and buries the only curiosity in "last".
- *"Mammoth Extinction: The Complete Story"* — "complete" is a promise about *us*, not a hook
  about *them*.
- Anything with "Shocking", "You Won't Believe", or a fake year. This audience (Eons,
  Kurzgesagt, PBS) punishes it in the comments and in the like-rate.

**One deliberate deviation from the sample:** the 2.6M video uses *"Who Killed The Biggest
Land Mammals"* — a **superlative** ("biggest"). Ours drops it. On our film "biggest" would
collide with the four-witness structure and point at the wrong question (which mammals, not
which cause). The specificity comes from *"the last"* instead, which is the same trade Eons
made with *"second-to-last"* — and it is the trade that separated 450k from 26k.

## 4. Thumbnails — three carriers, built from the film's own frames

`edit/build_thumbnail.py` renders these deterministically from the reel. **Every pixel is a
frame of the film**; no illustrated or stock artwork, which is also why none of them can
promise something the film does not contain. Output in `public/thumbnails/`, 1280×720.

| file | frame | type | why it is built that way |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A-they-were-still-here.jpg` | 790 s, golden-hour herd | `4,000 YEARS AGO` / **STILL HERE** | The only warm field in 13 minutes. A cold-blue feed is the natural habitat of every other ice-age thumbnail; this one is amber, so it wins the colour fight before the eye reads anything. Four animals with a calf: *something happened to them*, not *here is an animal*. |
| `B-what-killed-them.jpg` | 745 s, pale plain | `WRANGEL ISLAND` / **WHO KILLED THEM?** | The question form, paired with the film's actual mystery. Low-contrast snow is a liability, so the type carries the contrast (62 % black shadow). Zero overlap between type and tusks. |
| `C-one-genome.jpg` | 640 s, island map | `ONE ISLAND, ONE HERD` / **TEN GENERATIONS** | The Eons map move — one unexplained shape in an empty field, literal to the film's missing-samples argument. Weakest on emotion, strongest on specificity. |
| `D-no-verdict.jpg` | 725 s, verdict ledger | `FOUR WITNESSES` / **NO VERDICT** | A near-white document card in a feed of dark ice is a contrast anomaly *before* it is a design. Dark type on light, because white type on a near-white card is invisible at shelf size — measured, not guessed. Unique to this film: no competitor has a ledger. |

Rules every carrier obeys, and the evidence for each:

1. **≤ 4 words, one type block, lower-left.** Text-heavy thumbnails measure 4.3 % CTR
   against 5.8–7.2 % for high-contrast minimal ones (yt-thumbnail-downloader.org, 10,000
   thumbnails, 2026); >4 words costs ~30 % (1of10). Lower-left is the one region no platform
   chrome covers.
2. **Subject centre-right.** The duration stamp owns the bottom-right; the menu owns the
   right edge on mobile.
3. **Three words at 104 px, everything else ≤ 30 px.** At a 168×94 mobile tile the headline
   renders at ~14 px and stays readable; the kicker does not need to be, and does not compete.
4. **Warm subject against a cool field, or cool subject against warm.** The contrast is the
   click, not the saturation slider.
5. **Every thumbnail is the film.** If the herd on the tile is in the film, the first thirty
   seconds are already won — and the channel earns the right to make a second video.
6. **The reconstruction label is never sliced by the frame edge.** The reel's own burn-in is
   respected: the crop is padded rather than cut, so the honesty label is either whole or
   absent.

**Deliberately absent: a human face.** For an unknown documentary channel, a face on an
animal subject reads as a reaction channel and destroys the specificity that wins here. *(If
a presenter is ever cast, re-test — the +38 % face result is real, it simply does not apply
to a faceless channel.)*

### 4b. The rule that changes everything: the test is **three packages**, not three pictures

YouTube's native **Test & Compare** accepts **three variants** — as titles, as thumbnails, or
as *title + thumbnail pairs*. That cap is the real constraint on this package, and it means
the useful unit of design is not "the best thumbnail" but **three complete arguments**:

| slot | package | what argument it makes | why it gets a slot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | `Who Killed The Last Mammoths?` + **A** (amber herd) | *there was a crime and here is the animal* | the mechanism that produced 2.6M in this niche, in the warmest frame the film owns |
| **2** | `Four Witnesses. No Verdict.` + **D** (verdict ledger) | *this is not another nature doc — it is a case file* | the one package no competitor can copy, because it is the film's actual structure on screen |
| **3** | `They Were Still Here 4,000 Years Ago` + **B** (herd + question) | *the fact first, the mystery second* | the scale-shock play, with the herd large enough to survive a 168 px shelf |

**Slot 1 is uploaded first and deliberately**, because YouTube falls back to the first
variant when a test ends inconclusive — and inconclusive is the *common* outcome, not the
exception.

B and C are not both in the test. They are the **second round**: whichever package loses is
replaced by the other, so the cap of three is never the reason a good option went untested.

### 4c. Mobile legibility, measured rather than asserted

Every carrier was downscaled to the three surface sizes that matter and inspected:
**168×94** (phone browse shelf), **246×138** (desktop grid), **400×225** (hover preview).

The first B failed this test. At 168×94 the herd reduced to four grey smudges, because the
animals were only ~15 % of frame width. It was re-cropped to **1.9×** (`crop=680:382:351:162`)
with the type moved into empty sky. All four now hold at 168×94: the animals read as animals
and the words read as words.

This is the reason the package does not simply scale a 1280×720 frame down and call it a
thumbnail. A tile that is only checked at full size has not been checked.

## 5. Description

The first ~157 characters are what the search result shows; they must carry the question,
the place, and the payoff, in that order.

```
Who killed the last mammoths? For thousands of years a single herd survived on Wrangel
Island in the Arctic Ocean — long after the mainland herds were gone. Then, 4,000 years
ago, they vanished. No written record. No witnesses. Only bones, isotopes and DNA.

This is a cold case with four witnesses.

A 2017 genome comparison found the genetic damage of isolation — and predicted a satin
coat nobody ever saw. A 2019 isotope study found their diet stable right up to the end,
and something in the water beginning to change. The archaeology says people reached the
island, but several centuries too late to explain it. And in 2024, twenty-one genomes gave
the case its strangest turn: a population that crashed to eight or fewer, then recovered
to hundreds, and stayed small for thousands of years without collapsing.

What the film refuses to do is pick the most dramatic clue and call it solved. Each witness
is asked what it can actually support, what remains plausible, and what is still missing.
The last ten generations — roughly three centuries — are absent from the genome record.
That gap is the real subject of this film.

CHAPTERS
00:00 A mammoth with a mystery in its DNA
00:58 Four witnesses
01:26 The island nobody left
02:52 Witness one: the genome
05:05 Witness two: chemistry
06:31 Witness three: humans
07:38 Witness four: the larger genome study
10:29 The missing witness
11:29 The verdict
12:40 What the evidence still cannot say

SOURCES AND LIMITS
Every claim is drawn from published genome, isotope and archaeological work on the Wrangel
Island population — the 2017 genome comparison, the 2019 isotope study, the archaeological
record, and the 2024 twenty-one-genome study, plus 2025 work on ancient microbial DNA. The
animals you see are reconstructions, not recovered footage, and each shot is labelled on
screen. Where the science is still arguing, the film says so out loud. Where it is not, the
film shows its working.

Corrections welcome: <address>
```

Design notes:

- **Line 1 is a question with the answer inside it.** YouTube surfaces ~157 characters; this
  makes the search snippet a complete hook on its own.
- **"Cold case with four witnesses" is the film's own phrase** (01:17) and doubles as the
  structural promise. It is not a marketing invention.
- **The 2024/2025 detail is a freshness signal to a human,** not a crawler — and it is
  something the 2022 video that owns this search result could not have.
- **The limits paragraph converts a skeptic into a liker.** Same move as Eons' "(Definitely)".
- **Chapters are named as nouns, not questions.** Ten questions in a chapter list reads like
  a quiz show; ten nouns read like a dossier.
- **Chapter 1 is at 00:00 and the first is ≤ 00:59** — both are YouTube requirements for
  chapters to render at all. Verified against the caption track, not estimated.

## 6. Tags and hashtags

YouTube's own documentation is explicit that tags play a *minimal* role in discovery and
exist mainly for misspellings and ambiguous terms. They are included because they cost
nothing, not because they are believed in.

```
woolly mammoth, last mammoths, wrangel island, mammoth extinction, what killed the
mammoths, mammoth dna, ancient dna, pleistocene, ice age animals, extinct animals,
mammoth documentary, wrangel island mammoths, mammoth genome, prehistoric animals,
megafauna extinction, holocene extinction, arctic history, mammoth 2024, mammoth
genetics, cold case science, last ice age
```

Hashtags — three, above the fold. More than three and YouTube ignores all but the first
three shown:

```
#mammoth #wrangelisland #ancientDNA
```

Misspelling coverage, which is the one job the tag field actually does:
`wooly mammoth`, `woolly mamoth`, `wrangel isle`, `mamoth`, `mammoths extinct`,
`mammoth extinction documentary`.

## 7. Pinned comment — the retention trap

The first comment is read by more people than the description. It should open a loop the
video closes, and ask a question whose answer is *in* the film.

```
Eight. That is the modelled effective population size the 2024 study lands on at the
bottleneck — and then it recovers to a couple of hundred within about twenty generations.
Effective size is a genetic estimate, not a head count, and the film is careful about that
distinction. But sit with the shape of it: a herd that small, that isolated, holding on for
thousands of years.

So what actually ends it — the last bad season, or the six thousand quiet ones before it?
Our answer is at 11:29, and it is not the answer the thumbnail promises. Tell us yours.
```

Why it works: it quotes a real number from the film (eight), it names the exact timestamp
of the payoff, and the question has two defensible answers — so the comment field fills with
people disagreeing, which is the cheapest engagement signal available. The last sentence,
*"not the answer the thumbnail promises"*, is honest and self-aware; it is also the line that
makes someone click the next video.

## 8. The test plan, corrected to how the tool actually works

The first draft of this section described a manual 48-hour swap. That is out of date.
**YouTube Studio → Test & Compare** now runs the experiment natively, and its rules change
the plan in five concrete ways:

| the tool's actual behaviour | what it does to this package |
|---|---|
| **Three variants**, as titles, thumbnails, or title+thumbnail pairs | the package is designed as **three complete arguments**, not four loose tiles (§4b) |
| Winner is decided by **watch time share**, not CTR | the ledger (D) is riskier than its CTR suggests — a case file attracts the right viewer, but a wrong-audience click is punished harder here than anywhere else |
| A variant under **1280×720** drops **every** variant to 854×480 | hence `public/thumbnails/upload-1920/` — 1920×1080 upscales, so the test never degrades |
| Runs up to **14 days**; needs roughly **1,000–5,000 impressions per variant** | a new channel on a first video may never reach significance. Inconclusive is the *expected* outcome, which is why slot 1 must be a keeper |
| **Advanced Features** + verified account required; desktop only; not Shorts; not Made-for-Kids | confirm eligibility *before* upload. If it is missing, that is a verification problem, not a design problem |

### The four-round sequence

| round | variable | variants | what would prove the bet wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | the whole package | A + *Who Killed…* · D + *Four Witnesses. No Verdict.* · B + *They Were Still Here…* | If the ledger (D) wins, this audience arrives for the **evidence**, not the animal — and the entire "warm herd" thesis is wrong for a 13-minute cut |
| **2** | thumbnail only, winner's title fixed | add **C** (map) against the surviving two | If C wins, specificity beats both emotion and structure |
| **3** | title only, winning tile fixed | two new titles from §3 against the winner | If a *spare* title wins over slot 1, the case-file frame is weaker than the 2.6M video made it look |
| **4** | nothing — measurement | — | Like-rate under **1.2 %** means the click is oversold. The fix is the first 30 s, never the tile |

**One variable per round.** The tool allows combined title+thumbnail tests, and for round 1
that is correct — you are choosing a *package*. From round 2 onward, hold one side fixed or
you learn nothing about which side moved.

**What to record either way.** Whatever the tool reports — *Winner*, *Preferred*, or
*Inconclusive* — write the outcome into `../youtube-v14/EXPERIMENT-LOG.csv`. A null result
recorded is worth more than a hunch remembered.

## 9. The reverse-engineering method, stated plainly

The brief asks how big creators reverse-engineer a winning thumbnail and make it their own.
This is the honest version, and it is what produced §4:

1. **Find the top three videos on the exact question, not the general topic.** The general
   topic (mammoths) has a 28M result; the exact question (why the *last* herd died) has a
   2.6M one. The second is the one to study.
2. **Separate what the winner did from what its channel brought.** Getsetfly had an audience.
   Its *title* is portable; its *distribution* is not. Assume only the title travels.
3. **Find the loser with the same material and diff it.** WUFO has the same subject, a longer
   cut, and 100× fewer views. The diff: genre-name title, no question, no warm thumbnail, no
   specific number in frame. *That diff is the lesson* — the winner alone is not.
4. **Copy the mechanism, not the object.** The mechanism is a *closed social question over an
   open scientific one*. The object — a shocked face, an explosion, a red circle — is only a
   vehicle, and the wrong vehicle for a film whose whole argument is that nobody was caught
   red-handed.
5. **Then break one rule on purpose.** Every thumbnail in this niche does cold-blue ice-age
   palette. Ours is amber. That is the only place this package is original — and it is
   original in the one dimension the eye decides in, before any of the reasoning above
   matters.

## 10. Honest limits of this package

- **No CTR was measured here.** Every percentage in §1 and §4 is a public page fact (views,
  likes) or a published third-party claim, attributed. Nothing is our own instrumentation.
- **The premium tools named in the brief were not available.** vidIQ, TubeBuddy and Google
  Trends exports are paywalled or require an API key this environment does not have; the
  keyless `yt_intel` lane was rate-limited on every call. What replaced them: direct watch-page
  fetches, the repo's own 20-row corpus, and the niche thumbnails themselves.
- **The sample is small — eight videos.** It is enough to identify a mechanism and a loser
  to diff against. It is not a regression.
- **The native test's winner is watch time, not CTR.** A package can win the test while
  looking like it lost on a CTR graph. Judge the tool's verdict, and read retention
  alongside it.
- **The film is still a review reel.** These titles and thumbnails describe the cut as it
  stands (13:16, four witnesses). If the edit changes the witness structure, §3 and §5 change
  with it.
- **Rig rights remain unresolved.** Nothing here authorises distribution.
