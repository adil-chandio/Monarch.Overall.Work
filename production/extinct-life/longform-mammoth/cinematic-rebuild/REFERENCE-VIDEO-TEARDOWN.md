## The transferable lesson

The reference is **method, not content**. What it demonstrates, and what this package
copies from it:

1. **Ship several tiles for one video, not one hero image.** He has three candidates for a
   single upload and treats them as interchangeable by design. Our package does the same
   thing with `A`, `B`, `C` — three different *arguments* for the same 13 minutes, not three
   colour grades of one picture.
2. **The tile and the title are a matched pair.** He swaps the picture and the words
   together. §3 and §4 of `METADATA-RESEARCH.md` are therefore paired 1:1 — title 1 goes
   with tile A and B, title 2 with tile A only, and so on.
3. **The face is a variable, not a rule.** He tests his face as *one of three* options, not
   as the default. That is the correct status for the face result: a testable input. Our
   package omits the face because this channel has none — and says so, instead of pretending
   the +38 % figure doesn't exist.

## What it does not give us

- **No data.** There is no CTR readout, no view count, no test result on screen. It is a
  creator asserting a method, not a case study measuring one.
- **No niche transfer.** His subject is not an animal documentary and his channel is
  personality-led, so his face is an asset ours can never be.
- **Nothing about titles, descriptions, tags or chapters.** It is exclusively about the
  thumbnail swap. Everything in §5–§7 of the metadata document comes from the niche sample
  and the platform's own documented behaviour, not from this file.
