# Mammoth asset and experimental rig — work in progress

This is **not the finished film or a production-approved performance**. It is an attempt to turn a static third-party mammoth model into a usable animated asset. The source has one mesh (29,818 vertices, 56,994 triangles), a colour texture, no armature, and no animations. Its Sketchfab title says “Baby”, but its large tusks and proportions do not reliably establish an age; do not present it as a documented calf or an actual Wrangel specimen. Its anatomical and archaeological accuracy has not been reviewed.

## Limitations and required work

This revision fixes an additional deformation bug discovered during the trunk-performance test: coordinate-only skin weights also assigned part of a tusk to the trunk, visibly bending it. The builder now uses the source mesh’s disconnected components to assign both tusks rigidly to a newly added head bone, the trunk weights only to the head component, and leg weights only to the body/sole components. The two trunk bones are parented to the head, allowing a small head inclination with the trunk and tusks following. The exported GLB validator checks the two tusk components’ actual weights; this does not establish correct anatomy or deformation elsewhere.

It also identifies four separate feet from the source geometry; the fifth low component is the trunk tip, which the earlier experimental rig incorrectly treated as a foot. It adds separate foot bones and a sampled, planar two-link kinematic solution. If a consuming scene translates the animal at the specified speed, the experimental flat-ground inspection measures selected sole vertices during stance and checks that they stay close to the plane without obvious fore-aft slip. That limited check does **not** establish believable motion: uneven terrain, variable footing, full body weight transfer, animal behaviour, collisions, fine trunk contact and physical fur are not solved. Bone placements and weights remain approximate; the trunk and legs need anatomical review, close animated inspection and manual correction. The clip itself is in-place and must be translated by its consumer; the inspection scene handles the exporter’s 1-frame leading timestamp. Camera/lighting/display are crude inspection setups only. A convincing film still needs reviewed performance, environments, storytelling, score/SFX, editorial integration and human review.

## Reproduction and checks

- `rig_asset.py` is the deterministic construction recipe using Blender 4.2 Python `bpy`; it disables scripts when loading the source file. It requires a functioning graphics library environment for that version of Blender, and exits immediately after export because the local headless wheel crashed in teardown. The separate `validate_glb.py` checks the emitted file after exit.
- `python3 validate_glb.py` checks the GLB structure, embedded texture, sixteen joints, two clips, normalized four-influence vertex weights and head-only tusk weights; it cannot check artistic quality or validity of rights.
- From repository root, `node production/extinct-life/longform-mammoth/cinematic-rebuild/mammoth-rig/inspect.mjs` renders six headless inspection frames into the ignored `.cache/mammoth-rig-review`, checks deterministic seek, frame differences and browser errors. Requires the repository's installed Three.js, Playwright, `@sparticuz/chromium` and supporting graphics libraries. These are stills and automated checks, not a continuous animation review or a finished scene.

## Independent geometric corroboration — not master verification

An independent [Prehistoric Animal Museum provenance record](https://github.com/s010s/prehistoric-animal-museum/blob/HEAD/src/content/animals/mammoth/provenance.ts) describes a Sketchfab-converted SDPM source GLB (reported SHA-256 `8f46db6e2ba44c109fdc115506e1048212a876b51060930d0e20a8bd9f472aea`), then a modified runtime derivative. Its accompanying [source note](https://github.com/s010s/prehistoric-animal-museum/blob/HEAD/src/content/animals/mammoth/provenance/LICENSES/model-source.txt) explicitly says the runtime model was built from the SDPM CC BY 4.0 source, **not** kenchoo's CC BY-NC-SA animated re-upload. We acquired only the museum's published modified derivative, not its reported source master: `reference/museum-rigged-derivative.glb`, Git blob `7c9b33bd40d38c53d214ca896a52c94dd5fb4744`, SHA-256 `623a62621f1c6f2955fd3fe6442be8dfd34cdc94064e1bbb0c5e43e8970a1ece`. Credit SDPM Esare and the Prehistoric Animal Museum project's stated conversion, normalization, rigging, weighting, animation and compression if using that derivative. It is retained solely as a reference; our film prototypes still use our own modified rig.

Both our rig and that derivative decode to 29,818 rest-position vertices and 56,994 triangles. After each point cloud is centered and uniformly scaled by its longest bounding-box dimension, two-way nearest-neighbor distance has a maximum of approximately `0.00001294` of that dimension. This strongly corroborates matching rest geometry despite vertex reorder and quantization, but **does not** establish byte identity to the original Sketchfab master, texture identity, rights ownership, anatomical accuracy or license clearance. The museum's own statement is third-party provenance, not direct proof. Reproduce from the repository root:

```
node production/extinct-life/longform-mammoth/cinematic-rebuild/mammoth-rig/compare_reference.mjs
.venv/bin/python production/extinct-life/longform-mammoth/cinematic-rebuild/mammoth-rig/compare_reference.py
```
