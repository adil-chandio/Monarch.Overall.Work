# Sample-transfer scene — unfinished experiment

A 14-second deterministic Three.js *illustrative* sample-transfer sketch; **not** a reconstruction of an actual mammoth research protocol, not scientifically or artistically approved, and not footage in a completed film. Run `node production/extinct-life/longform-mammoth/cinematic-rebuild/sample-workflow/inspect.mjs` from the repository root to generate seven cached stills and structural state checks. Its assertion checks determinism, basic motion and that the sample reaches the simplified vessel floor. It is not a simulation or a review of the animation.

The gloved hand is a recoloured, statically posed adaptation of `public/models/glove.glb` from [kingsxiao/valorant-hold-training](https://github.com/kingsxiao/valorant-hold-training); that repository's README attributes the gloved hand to J-Toastie and identifies CC BY 3.0. The likely original is [Gloved Hand by J-Toastie](https://poly.pizza/m/MTB4egc4Vh), listed as Creative Commons Attribution. The byte identity of the copy against the original download and exact licence version need independent verification before publication. Local copy SHA-256: `d0ece4483c2d122ce2775acb7ff0957d57ae433e51bad7ea43d2a342d9a4bfa7`.

Known defects: fragments, laboratory geometry and sleeve are crude, stylized approximations; hand grip and tool/sample contact have not been solved or validated; the forceps now open before release, but collisions with the vessel have not been validated; the fragment's pickup is not physically demonstrated, and its falling motion is a simple kinematic approximation. The camera, lighting, specimen and material require substantial work. Do not treat this scene as ready for inclusion.
