# Arctic shore and herd — work in progress

A 30-second native-3D concept for a herd near open water. The coast and landforms are invented, not a scientific reconstruction of Wrangel's ancient geography or coastline; the identifying label marks the landscape as illustrative. Three scaled copies of the experimental rig walk in the same direction along a flat ground strip. Sea movement is shader-driven, stones are instanced and vegetation/snow are procedural; the ground uses the existing generated tundra image as a texture, not a slide. The sun casts shadow-mapped silhouettes. The sea is an approximation, with no hydrodynamic interaction with shore or animals.

The gait, repeated animal model, terrain, snow marks, lighting, water, cuts, atmospheric effects and organism/environment interaction still require substantial creative, scientific and animation work; this is **not an approved sequence or finished film**. The source model's provenance and anatomical uncertainty remain unresolved; see `../mammoth-rig/README.md`. The current scene has no sound or final narration alignment.

From the repo root `node production/extinct-life/longform-mammoth/cinematic-rebuild/shoreline/inspect.mjs` checks deterministic seeks, browser errors, sampled deformed feet and a small analytic sea-height check; no physical or artistic certification. Stills are in ignored `.cache`. `node production/extinct-life/longform-mammoth/cinematic-rebuild/shoreline/render-preview.mjs 900` renders a 30-fps silent local motion review with input/output hashes, not a completed documentary. A 900-frame review render decoded fully; no continuous human review has been performed. The last view holds the same walking activity for about 17 seconds and is too repetitive for the approved storytelling brief; it should not be used as a finished scene.

From the repo root `.venv/bin/python production/extinct-life/longform-mammoth/cinematic-rebuild/shoreline/mix_preview.py` creates a four-second, approximate 19–23s comparison when a valid 900-frame render exists. It combines the unapproved narration/score with an unverified, processed Florida surf recording at low level. It is not historical location audio, not proof that the mechanical sound problem is solved, and not a final mix. It needs continuous listening; short-clip integrated loudness is not a film-loudness measurement.
