# Skeleton reference exhibit — work in progress

This short, silent, native-3D exhibit is an asset inspection, **not the film**. The static mount is not a living animal rig, nor evidence of a Wrangel specimen or either of the genomes in the 2017 study. The environment is an invented museum stage; the second framing does not isolate the head effectively. Neither the setting nor the specimen has been scientifically reviewed.

The OBJ was downloaded from [Jmeshesha/Woolly-Mammoth-Visualization](https://github.com/Jmeshesha/Woolly-Mammoth-Visualization/blob/main/Assets/3d%20Models/Skeleton/Low%20Poly%20Mammoth/woolly-mammoth-150k.obj), Git blob `1250874a8024651bb1340a37a98a81c3052f0fc0`, SHA-256 `be6dc1dddd27fa9f75a1fed1fa84b9d193b180ac51746fffc3acfafc984046e6`. The repository attributes its skeleton to the Smithsonian. A [Smithsonian 150k skeleton scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Woolly-mammoth-150k_(Smithsonian_Institution).stl) is identified as CC0, but this third-party OBJ has **not** been independently matched to that master and its provenance/rights must be confirmed before public distribution. Do not label it as a specific specimen solely on this basis. The project’s MIT code licence does not automatically clear included third-party assets.

From the repository root, `node production/extinct-life/longform-mammoth/cinematic-rebuild/skeleton-reference/inspect.mjs` checks six seeks and writes stills to the ignored `.cache/mammoth-skeleton-reference`. Requires Three.js, Playwright and `@sparticuz/chromium`. Such checks are structural only: they do not establish specimen identity, quality, science or rights clearance.

## Further comparison, not source authentication

A separately downloaded third-party glTF claims to be Smithsonian's CC0 [USNM V23792 mammoth from Alaska](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/mammuthus-primigenius-blumbach-229976b3db4646b39c44e57a7e3d8744), **not Wrangel**. It came from [TiagoRibeiro25/Jumper](https://github.com/TiagoRibeiro25/Jumper/tree/HEAD/assets/Desert/mammuthus-primigenius-blumbach-3d-model), Git blobs `543ddc3479625688d5aacba0b433a4cac4179126` (`scene.gltf`, SHA-256 `eb054c6bd289c86c1bb651f2a59abca82ffe6137402b1b127051379c4694bf5d`) and `56c88d2200e004eb1bd2f8173e49bac7ba5de023` (`scene.bin`, SHA-256 `9174930eb3995d8aa18c341bce13ac8f8be1d6894d522534b24a2eefb5c9c89c`). Third-party files were not matched to the Smithsonian master.

`compare_smithsonian.py` compares the 49,594 OBJ vertices to 105,215 glTF positions after dividing the latter by 1,000; both have similar bounds (longest extent ~5.082 OBJ units). Two-way nearest-vertex distances have means 0.00465/0.00642 and 95th percentiles 0.01135/0.01538 in OBJ units. Different sampling and vertex counts preclude direct identity; this is approximate shape corroboration, not surface equivalence, master verification, or rights clearance. Script requires numpy and scipy and the two separately downloaded, hash-pinned comparison files.

The OBJ has many UV seam duplicates. Welding while ignoring UVs/normals gives 49,594 vertices and three connected components (94,824, 2,794 and 2,382 faces), not 2,187 independent bones. These are disconnected mesh components, not verified individual anatomical specimens. The smallest component is adapted for the illustrative excavation prototype; its anatomy and any archaeological setting are unverified.
