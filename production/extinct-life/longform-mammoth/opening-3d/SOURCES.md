# Sources behind the on-screen claims in Opening 01

Source notes for the opening narration and graphics. Predictions are not direct observations; the graphs and animal are illustrations.

| On-screen element | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "GENOME 2017 · damage" | A 2017 study of two complete woolly mammoth genomes (one Siberian mainland, 45,000 years old; one Wrangel, ~4,300 years old) found more deletions, more deletions inside genes and more premature stop codons in the island animal, consistent with genomic meltdown, with effective population size more than 43-fold lower | Rogers & Slatkin 2017, *PLoS Genetics* 13(3):e1006601, DOI 10.1371/journal.pgen.1006601 |
| Predicted coat phenotype (narration) | The same study reports two independent loss-of-function mutations at *FOXQ1* in the island mammoth, predicted to confer a satin coat phenotype | Rogers & Slatkin 2017, DOI 10.1371/journal.pgen.1006601 |
| "CLIMATE 2019 · ice + water" | A 2019 isotopic study of Siberian, Alaskan, Yukon and Wrangel mammoth remains found Wrangel's collagen carbon and nitrogen values unchanged to the end, and reported increasing bedrock weathering that may have degraded drinking water; the authors propose a short-lived rain-on-snow icing event | Arppe et al. 2019, *Quaternary Science Reviews* 222:105884, DOI 10.1016/j.quascirev.2019.105884 |
| "HUMANS · earliest evidence" | The earliest human occurrence on Wrangel Island dates to ~3,600 cal BP, almost four centuries after the disappearance of the mammoths; the 2019 study states a human contribution cannot be ruled out | Dehasque et al. 2024, *Cell*, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2024.05.033; Arppe et al. 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.quascirev.2019.105884 |
| "BOTTLENECK / RECOVERY" | Heterozygosity dropped from 0.75 to 0.43 sites per 1,000 bp (>40%); runs of homozygosity rose from 10.0% to 41.6%; forward simulations support a bottleneck Ne of 8 or fewer followed by recovery to Ne 200–300 within ~20 generations, stable for the ~6,000 years on the island | Dehasque et al. 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2024.05.033 |
| "NO FINAL WITNESS · final ten generations: no sampled genomes" | The 2024 dataset contains no genomes from the final ten generations of the island population; the youngest genome is Wra4.3K at 4,333 cal BP | Dehasque et al. 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2024.05.033 |
| "21 GENOMES 2024 · recovered" | 21 woolly mammoth genomes: 14 post-bottleneck Wrangel individuals spanning 9.2–4.3 ka cal BP and 7 Late Pleistocene mainland specimens | Dehasque et al. 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2024.05.033 |

## Deliberate omissions in this minute

- **The 2025 microbial study** (Guinet et al., *Cell*, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2025.08.003) is not referenced in this opening. It is context for a later chapter, not the opening hook, and it does not identify a cause of the Wrangel extinction.
- **No single killer is named.** The empty slot is the point of the film.
- **Effective population size is not drawn as a head count.** The schematic curve shows a modelled genetic quantity, not a count of individual mammoths and not a genetic-diversity curve.
- **No calendar conversion.** Calibrated radiocarbon ages are not rendered as a confident year of extinction.
- **No pyramid framing.** That hook belongs to other videos; this film leads with the recovery paradox.
