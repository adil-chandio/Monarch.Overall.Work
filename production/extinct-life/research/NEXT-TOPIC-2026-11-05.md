## Recommendation

**The frozen Homotherium cub: a saber-toothed cat preserved with fur, more than 35,000 years old.**

Recommended working title: **This Saber-Toothed Cub Still Has Its Fur**

Opening line: **“This saber-toothed cub is over 35,000 years old.”**

Immediately clarify that these are preserved remains, not a living cub. The editorial angle is **what a real mummy reveals that bones alone cannot show**, not resurrection, a kitten waking up, or “everything scientists knew was wrong.”

Why I prefer it for our next instalment: the small cub provides emotional contrast with the previous giant; identifiable fur, ears and paws provide concrete visual reveals; a CT-to-anatomy sequence is different from repeated DNA/lab scenes; and the initial anatomical paper has a later habitat study that can support a deeper continuation. ## Verified fact bank and exact limits

### 2024 primary study

Lopatin et al., *Scientific Reports*, published 14 November 2024: **Mummy of a juvenile sabre-toothed cat Homotherium latidens from the Upper Pleistocene of Siberia**. DOI: `10.1038/s41598-024-79546-1`. Read the fetched abstract, introduction, specimen context and initial description/comparison sections; not every supplement or raw CT dataset. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-79546-1)

- Found in **2020**, in permafrost at the Badyarikha River in northeast Yakutia, Russia; described in the **2024** paper. Do not call it a discovery made this week or newly unearthed in 2026. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-79546-1)
- Identification: **Homotherium latidens**, a saber-/scimitar-toothed cat. Do not substitute Smilodon anatomy or call it a true tiger. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-79546-1)
- Reported calibrated radiocarbon interval: **35,471–37,019 cal BP**, at 95.4% probability, from fur. The uncalibrated measurement is **31,808 ± 367 radiocarbon years BP**. Do not accidentally present these as conflicting calendar ages. For the main hook, “over 35,000 years old” is appropriately broad; the detail card can show the calibrated range. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-79546-1)
- Tooth-eruption stage puts it in an age group comparable to a **three-week-old lion cub**. Say **about three weeks old**, not an exactly known birth/death date. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-79546-1)
- The mummy preserves the **head and anterior body** approximately to the rear edge of the chest; incomplete pelvic/hindlimb bones were also found with it. Do not show an intact full-body mummy as the actual specimen. A full-body living reconstruction must be explicitly labelled and must not appear to be the specimen reviving. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-79546-1)
- The described specimen has preserved dark-brown fur, small ears, a robust neck, long forelimbs and broad paws; comparison was with particular lion specimens, not a statistically broad test of all lion cubs. Whiskers are described as mostly broken near their roots, and eyelashes were not preserved. Avoid “every hair perfectly intact.” [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-79546-1)
- The study used **CT imaging and anatomical comparison**, including cranial/dental features, to identify the animal. This reveal must not be presented as a result of a DNA sequencing experiment reported by this paper. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-79546-1)

### 2025 follow-up: an optional deeper-story angle

*Habitat reconstruction for the Late Pleistocene Siberian saber-toothed cat Homotherium using microphytofossils*, published 28 November 2025. DOI: `10.1038/s41598-025-26872-7`. Read the abstract/introduction and a fetched discussion passage, not all supplements. The authors infer a mosaic including floodplain larch forest and sedge/grass/forb meadows from microfossils in a sediment sample at the find locality. [2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-26872-7)

This supports a labelled **habitat reconstruction**, not a surveyed view of the exact moment of death. Do not turn the locality sample into proof of every habitat used by the genus, an individual hunting scene, or the cub's cause of death. The follow-up supplies a more substantive long-form continuation than simply saying “we might clone it.” [2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-26872-7)

## Proposed Short structure — approximately 45–55 seconds

A creative outline, not a final timed/recorded script:

1. **0–4s — Face/fur reveal:** non-graphic specimen detail or clearly labelled illustration. Hook: “This saber-toothed cub is over 35,000 years old.”
2. **4–10s — Location and time:** riverbank/permafrost cutaway and compact Siberia map. Clarify preserved remains, not a living animal in an ice cube.
3. **10–17s — Scan identification:** a newly designed CT-style skull sequence, accurately labelled illustrative unless we obtain rights and suitable real scan data. “Scientists studied its skull and teeth.”
4. **17–25s — Identify the animal:** Homotherium name reveal and genus-appropriate outline, not a stock Smilodon tiger reskin.
5. **25–34s — Two visible clues:** ears and paw shape compared with a labelled modern-lion comparator, without inventing exact ratios.
6. **34–42s — Why the mummy matters:** fur and soft tissues supply information a bare skeleton cannot show. Separate observed specimen details from reconstructed living appearance.
7. **42–50s — Honest close:** “A preserved cub—not a resurrected cat. But what did its world look like?” Only promise a related full film after that film and its actual link exist. Otherwise end on the question without claiming a linked video.

One-time sets and actions: fur macro, geographical/deposit reveal, scan chamber, identity card, anatomical comparator, evidence/reconstruction boundary, and habitat/question close. No repeated mastodon forest, reference-panel endcard, generic DNA tunnel or looped snarling cat.

## Accuracy and production gates

- No waking mummy, blinking preserved body, thaw-to-life effect or cloning claim.
- No huge adult saber canines on a roughly three-week-old cub.
- Do not claim a specific cause of death, mother abandonment, hunting event or last breath; not established by the material reviewed.
- Do not reuse a generic Smilodon model as Homotherium. Any adult/living cub reconstruction needs paper-based anatomical references and clear uncertainty labels.
- Avoid a sensational “neck twice as thick” graphic: the paper's prose and listed paired dimensions are not a clean basis for a universal ratio. Keep the wording qualitative and specimen-specific until independently checked.
- Do not call the mummy complete, undamaged or perfectly preserved; postmortem deformation and missing tissue are described.
- Actual mummy photographs need a non-graphic crop and a figure-specific licence/credit check before use. Open-access availability alone is not the completed asset-rights audit.
- Snow/ice, living environments and CT-like CGI are illustrations, not new footage of the original event or actual scan data.
- Primary publication dates are 2024 and 2025. Recycled 2026 news-page timestamps are not evidence of a new 2026 discovery.
- Before final script approval, independently verify each quantitative detail against the complete relevant passage and inspect the source figures. Expert anatomical certification has not been obtained.

## Decision

**Develop the Homotherium cub next; keep Vasuki as the next reserve.** This is a researched editorial recommendation, not permission to render/publish a new film or a promise of guaranteed virality. Existing videos and metadata remain unchanged.
