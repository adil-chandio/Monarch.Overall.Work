## Decision

**Title:** How Do You Find a Mastodon Without Its Body?

**Concept:** an animal-identification mystery, not a generic ancient-animal fact list. Deliver the answer—environmental DNA—inside the Short. Then invite viewers into the harder identification/checking problem that the existing full film actually explains.

**Audience:** English-language science, ancient life and evidence-driven mysteries. Success means qualified full-film viewing, not unrelated giveaway/comedy reach.

**Format:** original 1080×1920 / 30 fps, approximately 44–48 seconds, final timing determined by natural narration. No full-film speed change. No forced duration through rushed speech. Full moving vertical composition, not a center-cropped landscape montage or slideshow. This is a creative proposal, not a claim that this duration is algorithmically optimal.

## Complete proposed English narration

```text
How do you find an extinct giant without finding its body?

In northern Greenland, scientists found traces of mastodons in sediment about two million years old.

The clue wasn’t a skeleton. It was broken DNA.

Living things leave genetic traces behind. Some fragments can bind to minerals, helping them survive.

Scientists compared the damaged pieces with known DNA.

Together with plant DNA, they revealed evidence of a vanished forest ecosystem.

Not a resurrected animal. Not a complete genome.

But here’s the harder mystery: how do you know those tiny pieces belong to a mastodon?

Follow the full investigation in the linked video.
```

No competitor wording or footage is to be reused. “Broken DNA” is a plain-language introduction to short damaged fragments, not a recovered intact double helix/genome. “About two million years old” modifies sediment; not each molecule independently. The North Greenland locality must remain explicit. The mastodon represents a lineage signal, not a precisely known individual or appearance. “Forest ecosystem” does not mean all Greenland was identical, nor that forest evidence was previously wholly unknown.

## Why this one, not other concepts

1. **“They brought a mastodon back”**: stronger spectacle, false science and wrong expectation for the film. Reject.
2. **“Five facts about ancient Greenland”**: covers more, but no single task or necessary next step. Reject for this first acquisition test.
3. **“Greenland used to be green”**: broad and generic; loses the molecular detective story. Reject.
4. **Body absent / evidence present**: understandable objective, concrete animal, surprising clue, satisfying local answer and an honest second mystery. Select this one.

The opening asks a solvable question. The answer is given at ~11–15 s, so viewers are not held hostage to an ad. The mechanism delivers further value. The final question concerns checking identity, which the existing full film addresses around 02:39; the full-film beginning also establishes the same animal/evidence puzzle. This is continuity, not an unrelated promised sequel.

## Measurement and decisions

No performance guarantees or invented competitor retention. Record at 24 h, 72 h, 7 d and 14 d; early observations are diagnostics, not automatic verdicts. Compare against channel baselines of similar length only if enough history exists. New channel: build a baseline; do not borrow giant-channel numbers as quotas.

1. **Entry:** feed exposure (if shown) and stayed-to-watch vs swiped away. If weak, inspect the opening image/question together before changing everything.
2. **Value delivery:** engaged views, average view duration, average percentage viewed and retention curve. Inspect drops at technical explanation, qualifier and CTA. APV over 100% may reflect replay, not universal completion.
3. **Acquisition:** full-film traffic attributable to Shorts/related surfaces where Studio exposes it; related-video clicks if available. Log exact UI/export metric name. Do not equate broad Shorts traffic with proven unique clicks from this particular Short.
4. **Quality:** full-film watch time/AVD/early retention for that traffic segment where available. A million starts with negligible qualified film watch time is not success for this brief.
5. **Derived diagnostics:** attributable full-film starts per 1,000 engaged Short views, and attributable full-film watch minutes per 1,000 engaged Short views. Only compute when numerator scope is actually identifiable; otherwise mark unavailable/proxy. Record denominators and date windows.

High Short retention but low film traffic → bridge/link/intent problem; high film starts but immediate exits → expectation mismatch or film opening; low initial hold but good survivor retention → opening problem. These are diagnostic hypotheses, not automatic causal proof. Do not delete/reupload immediately or change hook, length, captions and CTA together. Any future revision needs a fresh decision after data, not a claim of a controlled A/B experiment from sequential uploads.
