## B. Scientific source study — what matters for wording

Read selected primary-paper sections: abstract/introduction; dating and preservation; mineral adsorption and metagenomic processing; plant/animal comparison; and the animal-DNA classification passage. This is targeted reading, **not** a claim to have audited every method, supplement or raw dataset.

The paper reports environmental DNA from the Kap København Formation, an approximately two-million-year-old ecosystem, evidence including mastodons, and possible mineral-surface protection. That supports the age/eDNA/animal/ecosystem package. It does not support revival or a living animal found in ice. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05453-y?error=server_error&error=server_error)

Two less obvious guardrails from the reading:

1. **Age is approximate, not a stopwatch.** The dating discussion considers approximately 1.9 and 2.1 million-year scenarios. The public description therefore says “about two million years old”; the compact title uses the paper's two-million-year framing. Do not say “exactly 2,000,000 years.” [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05453-y?error=server_error&error=server_error)
2. **Identification is inference with limitations.** The animal section discusses incomplete reference representation and says the specific placement within mastodons depends on two transition SNPs with limited read support. This is not equivalent to saying the whole study consists of two DNA letters. It is a reason to avoid “100% proven,” exact-species certainty, or complete-genome wording. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05453-y?error=server_error&error=server_error)

“World's oldest DNA” was part of the original 2022 coverage, but I have not established a comprehensive current world-record audit. The recommended metadata avoids that time-sensitive superlative and also avoids presenting a 2022 publication as breaking news. The Nature summary independently supports the mastodon/forested-Greenland framing. [2](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-022-03626-3)

## D. Official YouTube guidance versus SEO folklore

- Shorts recommendations use viewer personalisation and performance, including choosing to view, average view duration, average percentage viewed and enjoyment signals. Search also considers metadata/query fit and whether people click and watch. Metadata is therefore not a substitute for a satisfying video. [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts)
- YouTube advises accurate titles; misleading promises can cause viewers to leave. Our title excludes resurrection and does not promise the entire identification investigation within 43.5 seconds. [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)
- Tags play a minimal discovery role except in cases such as misspellings; title, thumbnail and description matter more. No time is spent claiming the tags box is a viral switch. [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402?hl=en)
- Up to three description hashtags can display by the title. Official guidance says **more than 60** hashtags causes all hashtags to be ignored—not the “15 maximum” repeated by some secondary SEO pages. This package uses only three relevant labels; it does not claim three is optimal. [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658?hl=en)
- URLs in Shorts descriptions/comments are non-clickable. Related Video is a separate clickable player feature. It requires advanced feature access, and the target must be a public or unlisted video from the channel. [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639?hl=en) [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)

Secondary “2026 SEO” pages with unsupported fixed character sweet spots, guaranteed percentage lifts, mandatory hashtag myths or conflicting policy claims were not used as evidence for performance predictions.

## F. Measurement plan — recommendations, not invented results

Use available Studio analytics after a meaningful sample accumulates. A 24-hour check and a later multi-day review are organisational checkpoints, not algorithm deadlines or guaranteed distribution windows.

1. Separate **Shorts-feed** traffic from **YouTube Search** traffic; don't treat one overall CTR as a complete Shorts diagnosis.
2. For feed distribution, examine chose-to-view/stayed-to-watch, view duration and retention. If people swipe before the promise lands, hashtags alone will not resolve that; any later video change would be a separate task. These are relevant signals, not universal pass/fail thresholds. [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts)
3. For search, inspect actual queries where available. If the packaging is attracting resurrection/dinosaur intent rather than environmental-DNA interest, keep the evidence/sediment wording clear; do not add those unrelated terms as tags.
4. Inspect long-form traffic attributable to Shorts where the account exposes it. Do not invent a universally available related-link CTR metric or promise conversion.
5. If a title change is warranted, log the previous title, new title, time and traffic mix. A sequential edit is not a controlled A/B test, and Shorts title-test availability has not been verified for this account. Do not repeatedly delete/reupload to manufacture a test.
6. No channel geography, audience-active-hours data or validated keyword volume was available. Consequently, no “best posting time,” demand score, predicted views, CTR lift or viral probability is claimed.
