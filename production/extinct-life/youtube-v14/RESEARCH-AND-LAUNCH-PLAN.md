## 1. Research scope — what “studied” means here

**20 successful public-video metadata/package audits, including 15 with fetched counts above one million.** Four are directly about Greenland’s ancient DNA; the remainder cover adjacent natural history, scientific animation and a small number of farther packaging analogies. A failed NOVA fetch is excluded, not filled with an invented count.

Method: web search for discovery, then `fetch_page` for public video titles, channels, dates, lengths, views, likes, descriptions and opening transcript excerpts. This is **not authenticated YouTube Data API or private Studio Analytics access**. Public-page extraction can be cached; counts below are fetched snapshots, not guaranteed live counters. No credentials were requested.

There was no uninterrupted viewing of twenty complete videos. Whole-transcript review is also not claimed: only fetched opening chunks were used. Six usable indexed visual references were inspected; two additional returned images were tiny/unusable. Visual references from PBS episode pages and Reddit embeds may differ from current YouTube assets or the thumbnails that originally earned the historical views. The audit does **not** pretend to have visually inspected twenty original upload thumbnails.

There is no competitor impression count, CTR, retention, traffic mix, historical packaging timeline, channel-median baseline, spend or A/B-test result. Consequently:

- A large lifetime view count is not evidence of a high CTR, an overnight viral spike or thumbnail causation.
- Larger/older channels and years of accumulated viewing confound comparisons.
- Shorts/promos and 53-minute documentaries are not controlled comparisons to a 7-minute animation.
- Farther science analogies illustrate mechanisms, not demand for this specific Greenland film.
- The sample is purposive, not a statistical census. Including below-million cases reduces—but does not eliminate—survivorship bias.

## 4. Forensic explanation of likely click mechanisms

These are falsifiable design hypotheses, not findings from eye-tracking or private analytics.

### 4.1 A specific information gap beats an empty tease

“Something incredible was discovered” withholds the subject itself. “A mastodon in Greenland?” provides an animal and a place but leaves the explanation unresolved. A viewer knows what kind of answer they are buying with their time. Our search-led A challenger supplies another concrete clue—2-million-year-old DNA—while the selected B pairing puts the age on the image instead of turning the title into a technical abstract.

### 4.2 Familiarity gives the surprise an anchor

A viewer need not know *Mammut* or Kap København. They can recognise an elephant-like extinct mammal and associate Greenland with cold. The gap between those familiar ideas is accessible. The film must then correct the implied modern-ice setting: the evidence concerns a different ancient landscape, not a living animal discovered on ice today.

### 4.3 Time is a meaningful number; arbitrary numbers are decoration

“2 million years” comes from the studied deposits, rather than a made-up ranking or a claimed number of complete recovered genomes. Use the age once in the leading pair. Repeating “2 million years” in both title and thumbnail consumes scarce space unless testing deliberate reinforcement.

### 4.4 Title and image should split the work

- A title challenger: age, DNA mechanism, Greenland, lost-world payoff.
- Proposed A image (declined): large illustrative animal plus cold-place cues.
- Proposed A type: **HERE?** would ask about the relationship rather than repeating the title; it is not a delivered thumbnail.
- Selected B title: dirt-to-world mystery.
- B image/type: sediment gives the object, **2 MILLION YEARS** supplies deep time.

This division is a design choice, not a universal algorithm rule. A thumbnail is an argument at small size: one subject, one relationship, one unanswered question.

### 4.5 Contrast should clarify—not invent evidence

Warm brown subject against cool blue-grey landscape is a proposed separation system. A rim light may help silhouette readability. Neither makes the reconstruction exact. Do not add a giant live mastodon trapped in ice, a visible microscopic DNA strand inside an ordinary dirt clod, a false laboratory measurement, or a real scientist apparently reacting to our invented scene.

### 4.6 A high click count with a broken promise is not success

The film starts with the mastodon DNA puzzle, then follows sediment transport, survival, reference matching, uncertainty and an illustrative world. A dinosaur attack, resurrection or “we went to Greenland” thumbnail would attract a different promise. Even honest packaging cannot remove V14’s documented long holds or stiff animation; retention remains a product constraint, not a metadata field.

## 6. Thumbnail production specification

Two distinct concepts were explored; only B was selected and exported. A remains an unapproved concept, not a delivered alternative. Artwork should be original and maintain the film’s recognisably stylised 3D identity; no photoreal wildlife promise for a simplified animation.

**A — animal/place contradiction:** one mastodon dominates the right side, silhouette and tusks clear, body kept within frame; a minimal cold Arctic landscape supplies context; restrained vegetation can hint at the ancient-world puzzle. Left/top-left text zone: **HERE?**. No crowded deer/goose/rodent supporting cast. No fake recovery/excavation of a body.

**B — dirt/deep-time contrast:** a gloved hand holds a conspicuous irregular clod of sediment in foreground; a modest illustrated ancient forest/animal context provides story, not a magical portal or an animal emerging alive from soil. Type: **2 MILLION / YEARS**. The clod should read as soil, not a fossil tooth, rock crystal or embryo.

**Proposed production constraints, not competitor-derived measurements:**

- Compose at 16:9; deliver 3840×2160 JPG masters plus 1280×720 compatibility copies, all under 2 MB as a conservative cross-device choice.
- YouTube’s fetched current help recommends 3840×2160; do not repeat the older claim that 1280×720 is the only recommended size. [6](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431?hl=en)
- Two-word or short-question text where possible; exact typography added in post rather than trusting generated letters.
- Use a generous type margin and keep vital information away from the bottom-right duration badge.
- Test 320×180 and 160×90 previews, not only a full-size desktop image. A reduced preview is a layout check, not audience eye-tracking evidence.
- No logo copied from a benchmark channel; do not use “4K,” “V14” or release/run IDs as thumbnail clutter.
- A small illustration label can reinforce transparency, but it does not replace an accurate title, clearly animated style and description.
- Inspect for malformed trunk/tusks, extra limbs, illegible crop, texture artefacts and a misleadingly photographic rendering after selection and export.

## 8. Measurement and testing — how to find a winner honestly

### Phase 0 — verify the promise

Upload unlisted first; allow HD processing; check the actual opening, captions, chapters, music checks, thumbnail render and owner comfort with the remaining V14 limitations. The package is not permission to mark the film flawless. Nothing in this task publishes to a YouTube channel.

### Phase 1 — test the main hypotheses

Where eligible in desktop Studio, use **native concurrent testing**, not rapid manual daily swaps. Current YouTube guidance supports up to three titles/thumbnails/combinations, requires advanced-feature eligibility and excludes certain formats/states. It optimises tests for **watch time**, not CTR alone, and can take days to two weeks. [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)

Choose the question before testing:

- **Which complete package works better?** A versus B is a future experiment only after a different A artwork is approved. At present only B is delivered; do not pretend there are two ready image variants. Any future combined-title/image result is a **bundle** result, not title-only attribution.
- **Which title works better?** Keep the selected B thumbnail fixed; test B, A and C. This is the immediately actionable native test. A repeats the age already in the image; record that deliberate trade-off.
- **Which image works better?** Requires another approved artwork. Do not count a resolution export or a rejected concept as an independent thumbnail hypothesis.

Do not run all three experiments simultaneously, declare a winner from a handful of clicks or repeatedly stop/restart based on early noise. If the native result is “Performed Same” or “Inconclusive,” report exactly that, retain the first/default or make an editorial choice, and do not manufacture a statistically significant winner. If the account lacks native testing, sequential swaps are only exploratory and are confounded by time/audience changes.

### Phase 2 — watch the right diagnostics

Record native test result and watch-time share, impressions, CTR **by traffic source**, average view duration, first-30-second retention, meaningful comments and whether the content meets the package promise. YouTube’s impressions/watch-time funnel is the appropriate place to examine the relationship. [5](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314486?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)

A rough conceptual diagnostic is `watch time per impression ≈ CTR × average view duration` for a consistently defined comparable traffic slice; it is **not** YouTube’s internal ranking formula or a replacement for the native test. Example only: 8% × 2 minutes = 0.16 minutes/impression; 5% × 4 minutes = 0.20. These invented example numbers are not this film’s analytics.

Interpretation:

- Low source-matched CTR, acceptable retention: packaging is a plausible bottleneck.
- Higher CTR but poor early retention: promise mismatch or weak opening may be the bottleneck; more sensational copy can worsen it.
- Both weak: revisit audience fit and the film experience, not just tag lists.
- Good retention, low impressions: cannot infer a thumbnail failure without enough suitable audience exposure.
- Falling blended CTR while impressions expand: can reflect a broader audience mix, not necessarily deterioration.

No universal “good CTR,” guaranteed release time, magic tag count or arbitrary 24-hour winner threshold is asserted. Use the channel’s real audience data where available. No channel-specific Analytics was supplied here.

## 9. Scientific truth ledger

Primary source: Kjær and colleagues, *A 2-million-year-old ecosystem in Greenland uncovered by environmental DNA*, Nature (2022), DOI `10.1038/s41586-022-05453-y`. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05453-y)

The fetched abstract supports approximately two-million-year-old Kap København deposits, environmental DNA evidence including mastodons/reindeer/rodents/geese, a mixed open boreal forest community, no modern analogue and probable mineral-surface preservation. Its discussion includes pre-existing macrofossil/pollen evidence, so packaging must not erase previous knowledge of a greener landscape. We do not copy a competitor’s more dramatic summary when it conflicts with the approved script’s caution.

| Public-facing claim | Allowed framing | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 2-million-year-old DNA | Shorthand supported by the study; explain deposit dating in description | Not an exact direct date for each molecule. |
| Mastodon in Greenland | Evidence of a mastodon lineage from sediment DNA | Not a complete skeleton, live animal, exact species portrait or exact coat. |
| Lost world revealed | DNA added evidence about a vanished community | Not proof every organism was unknown, every species identified or all animals stood together. |
| Frozen dirt helped preserve fragments | Conditions and mineral association can support survival | Not a perfectly intact instruction book or demonstrated resurrection. |
| Ancient community unlike today | Broad ecological transformation and no modern analogue | Not a tropical rainforest, actual recovered footage or absence of earlier palaeobotanical knowledge. |
