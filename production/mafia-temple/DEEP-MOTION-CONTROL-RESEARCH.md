## The newly identified route

**One-to-All Animation 1.3B is the best new research candidate for the running shot**, not because it is proven to produce this film, but because it directly combines a character reference and a driving motion, addresses reference/pose mismatch and has a documented author-reported Kaggle T4 route. This is a genuinely different architecture from both the rejected StableAnimator and the previously tried Wan2.2-TI2V-5B. It uses a Wan2.1 backbone plus a learned reference extractor and pose-control pathway. [Project and source](https://github.com/ssj9596/One-to-All-Animation), [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22940v1).

### What the T4 evidence really says

The authors' [T4 recipe](https://ncn0ojsozocg.feishu.cn/wiki/J9Ohwmtudin0vtkuyPccIZkhnZz) links their [Kaggle notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/ssj180123/onetoall1-3b-comfyui-kaggle) and describes a driving video read as **161 frames at 16 fps**, rendered in segments, with the first chunk **81 frames**, 480×832, `dpm++_sde`, 8 steps, CFG 2.0 and pose CFG 1.0. The guide reports ~7.28 GB reserved at sampling and ~9.63 GB reserved at VAE decode, and separately shows an 81-frame 576×1024 example at 11m44s. The repository says ~11 minutes for a 10-second 832×480 example. These are **author reports**, on their inputs/workflow, not measurements by us. They are not evidence of native 24 fps, identity fidelity to our user, realistic running or a moving camera. Two chunks with temporal overlap need an explicit seam check. The guide acknowledges first-run OOM and suggests a restart.

The visible notebook logs independently show a Tesla T4, ~15,095 MB GPU and ~32,103 MB host RAM, model downloads, ComfyUI startup and dependency conflicts; **the visible saved notebook does not itself contain the finished generation**, and its displayed 10.7-second run time is not the render time. The guide's output media was not available for frame-by-frame inspection through the research interface. So the stronger conclusion is *documented author T4 recipe*, not independently verified end-to-end replication.

At the pinned official source revision `b3c9d886c93c43e767b995e93968708ee2d410cb`, `video-generation/inference_1.3b.py` defaults to **BF16** and `pipe.to(device)`; it is **not** the same as the author's FP16 ComfyUI recipe. The paper reports ~29 GB H100 memory and 1m58s for 81 frames without compression/acceleration at its stated benchmark configuration. Do not assume that running the raw inference script unchanged fits a T4. The notebook instead downloads `Wan21-OneToAllAnimation_1_3B_v2_fp16.safetensors` (~4.12 GiB), a BF16-named UMT5 encoder (~10.58 GiB), a Wan 2.1 VAE (~0.24 GiB), YOLOv10m (~0.06 GiB) and ViTPose-L (~1.15 GiB), plus runtime dependencies. Filename precision is storage, not proof of the dtype used for every operation; actual text-encoder, VAE and transformer dtypes need recording on T4. The guide's workflow JSON is a Feishu attachment we have not yet retrieved; a corresponding 14B example graph in the wrapper is not proof of the exact 1.3B T4 graph. Exact node versions, headless graph conversion and input wiring therefore remain open engineering tasks.

**Security issue in the example:** its interactive setup publicly exposes a ComfyUI server through a tunnel and changes manager security settings. We must **not** reuse that public tunnel, its credentials, or blindly execute the unpinned notebook. A future private Kaggle batch notebook should use an internal loopback-only API, pin audited code and model revisions, minimize external access, and avoid secret/data logging. The notebook's exposed example credential is not ours and must not be used.

### What the model may and may not solve

- The authors say the **1.3B_v2** checkpoint was further trained for larger camera movement and more image input; **v1** performed best in their video benchmark. Choose v2 as an initial hypothesis, not an assumed winner; test reference and pose guidance against a fixed seed and driver.
- The official preprocessing estimates pose from **ordinary video frames**, estimates the reference pose, and constructs reference image, reference pose, mask and driving pose tensor. Modes include aligning to the reference or the driving pose; the Kaggle guide suggests `align_to pose` and weak face/head skeleton options. Cropping, normalization, orientation, pose color and masks matter.
- Our previous CMU 09_01 experiment projected a 3D skeleton into 2D, removed root translation for a tracking-camera hypothesis, and approximated the character's hand/face landmarks. It is *not* a real tracked performance with real fingers or reliable 3D foot contact. Feeding those colored skeleton pictures to the normal human-video detector would be the wrong interface; a direct pose-embedding path must implement and validate this model's exact convention or use a rights-cleared real driving video and the intended preprocessor. A mismatched driver can recreate the same failure as before.
- I inspected contact sheets of two author 1.3B GIF demonstrations and two longer GIFs from the source checkout. The examples are small, selected demonstrations, often stylized/animated; they show pose and approximate appearance continuity, but are not a side-running black-suited man, do not establish realistic hands or ground contact, and cannot verify our user's likeness or frame-to-frame camera parallax.
- 16 fps output is not 24 fps motion. Encoding it as 24 fps makes it faster; interpolation synthesizes estimates, not correct missing hand/foot physics. A native 24 fps driver/render at this exact model/workflow requires a specific experiment, and may generalize less well than its demonstrated 16 fps route.

### License / availability boundary

The repository has Apache-2.0 source code, and the raw [1.3B v2 model card](https://huggingface.co/MochunniaN1/One-to-All-1.3b_2/raw/main/README.md) declares Apache-2.0 in its metadata. That is promising, but the separately converted Kijai FP16 file, its base and pose/detection models also require their own provenance and terms/checksums to be checked. Do not infer that the entire pipeline is cleared merely from the code or one card. CMU motion can be used subject to its published FAQ and redistribution restrictions; the particular converted source and any new driver need provenance. Research-only output is not cleared for publication.

## GPU/CPU reality check

1. **Existing route:** previous research has run private Kaggle T4 kernels via an existing GitHub Actions workflow. This is not a new paid GPU provider, but quota/availability are unknown until allocated; two T4 cards do not automatically act as one 32 GB card. In this workspace the local machine has no usable GPU and ~3.8 GiB RAM. It cannot perform full diffusion locally in practical time/memory.
2. **GitHub's standard public-repo runners** are CPU runners; GitHub offers T4 as a **larger runner**, available only for eligible organization/enterprise plans and billed, including on public repositories. No entitlement or payment authorization has been checked. It is not a free higher-end GPU. [GitHub runner specifications](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/runners/larger-runners), [pricing](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/reference/actions-runner-pricing).
3. **Colab free GPU** is subject to dynamic availability and restrictions and requires an account/user interaction; no entitlement here is established. [Google FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html). Alibaba's model card mentions an application-based free GPU offer; its region, eligibility, access and terms were not verified. Do not create accounts, bind billing or use someone else's machine without permission.
4. GitHub Actions can orchestrate work but does not conjure GPU capacity. A paid or stronger GPU, if genuinely needed, would require separate approval and a clear budget. We cannot promise perfect results by naming more compute.

## Proposed next execution plan — not yet run

**Gate A — prepare trustworthy motion:** choose or prepare a single-person side-view running driver with visible feet and natural alternating steps, fixed or known moving camera, no occlusion by background people, and documented rights. The old CMU pose/control is a useful starting diagnostic but its synthetic hands/face and unknown 2D ground contact must not be treated as ground truth. Confirm whether direct pose input can be mapped to One-to-All's expected representation; otherwise use a correctly preprocessed driving video.

**Gate B — first private T4 experiment:** reference only `keyframes/shot-04-identity-study-b.jpg` (approved generated reference), never the original. Reproduce the author-compatible 1.3B-v2 FP16 ComfyUI path using private, non-public batch execution, not a public tunnel. Initial 16 fps pilot is to determine pose/appearance viability; record input frame count, GPU/host peaks, timings, actual compute dtypes, checkpoint hashes, versions, seed, offload settings and exact failure. Compare observed results to prior StableAnimator v7 rather than labeling any output final. If motion or hands remain fundamentally wrong, stop and diagnose driver/pose before trying random seeds.

**Gate C — actual brief:** only after B passes, test a full 3-second shot with adequate resolution and either verified native 24 fps generation or a separately inspected 16→24 fps interpolation, with no foot teleportation or unrealistic velocity. Evaluate at final viewing size, all frames, and compare identity locally to the original without publishing it. Then address camera parallax/background independently; a locked character image does not magically create a tracked environment. Cross-check with the higher-cost Fun-5B-Control path if the T4 pose route does not hit the quality bar.

**Other four scenes:** rear walking requires comparable gait/contact and background motion; gate and aerial need architectural stability and coherent camera path; the close-up requires identity, dynamic facial expression and plausible red-eye emission/reflection, not flat red circles. Finish with edit timing, native 24 fps playback, sound-source rights, loudness plus listening QA and an entire-film inspection. No upscaling or compositing claim substitutes for human visual review. Each stronger provider, original-reference upload, paid compute or public release has its own permissions boundary.

**No approval is implied by this analysis.** The user rejected the earlier limited plan and requested deeper research; do not launch a new generation until the user approves the new motion-controlled experiment.
