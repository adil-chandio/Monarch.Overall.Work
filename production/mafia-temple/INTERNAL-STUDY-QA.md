# Internal assembly check — not a deliverable

A local 20-second study was assembled solely to expose remaining problems. It has 480 frames at 24 fps and 1280×720; scene boundaries at frames 96, 216, 312, and 384 were sampled. The audio stream is present; automated volume analysis found -14.3 dBFS sample peak and -30.2 dBFS mean, but no listening/creative review was possible and sound sync is unverified.

**Fails final review:** walk is a 2D segmented figure over a flat, digitally scaled plate with unverified foot contact; gate and aerial footage are low-resolution upscales, and aerial motion is a digital push rather than a physical descending camera; the running figure has distorted fingers, an intermittent shoe artifact and floating contact, and the face patch is frozen; the close-up likeness remains approximate, the mouth deforms and the tracked red tint resembles contacts rather than convincing emitted light. The five scenes also need continuity, animation, sound, and licensing review. Keep this study local and do not present it as the requested finished film.

## Later local comparison
A further internal assembly used pose-segmented running, depth-guided street and aerial camera tests, and the alternate face turn. Cut timings and 480-frame duration were verified. The running matte now retains the raised leg better, and the aerial is sharper, but the pose still floats/changes sleeves, the aerial is a nearly static 2.5D image without real fog movement, and the close-up likeness and eye effect remain unverified and synthetic. The gate is still a low-resolution generated shot. Audio and legal/creative review remain outstanding. This is still not a final film.

A local-only face-descriptor sanity check compared the provided reference portraits with sampled generated close-ups and running frames. It suggested the running face diverges more than the close-up samples, but viewpoint, lighting and low resolution affect the score; it is neither identity proof nor a substitute for user review. Reference images and descriptors were not committed or uploaded. The research check used the face-api.js model family: https://github.com/justadudewhohacks/face-api.js. Likeness therefore remains a blocker.

## Local v5 assembly and grounding test
A 480-frame local-only v5 assembly combined the v4 face turn, a texture-preserving red-eye test, the v6 run with a local generated-reference face blend, inferred-depth plates and provisional normalized audio. A 30-frame contact sheet across all five scenes and cuts was reviewed. The close-up looks closer to the reference but has static body/background and synthetic mouth motion; the red tint reads more like contact lenses than emitted light. The running figure still has malformed hands/shoes, inconsistent foot contact and approximate face motion, while walk, gate and aerial retain the earlier limitations. Audio has not been listened to or checked for creative synchronization. Third-party weight and driver rights are not cleared. This is not a deliverable.

An additional local pose-landmark experiment shifted the entire running cutout toward an assumed fixed ground line. The change was visually small; it does not establish stance phases, shoe locking, realistic shadow or reflections and is not suitable as a fix. It was not added to the compositor.
