# Video-generation forensic research and proposed pilot

**Research cutoff:** 2 November 2026. **Status:** research and plan only; no new model run in this research phase. The existing 20-second studies have not passed the user's quality bar and are not final deliverables.

## Executive decision

The most defensible next step is a **bounded, no-paid-service comparison**, not a promise of a finished film:

1. Test **Wan2.2-TI2V-5B** using a newer, separately reported T4 memory-management approach. This is **the same model family already tried here**, not a new magic model. The difference is the runtime/offload approach, target resolution and a stronger generated source frame. We previously rendered it at 512×288 and the quality was rejected; our 640×352 attempts ran out of memory. A higher-resolution result could be better, but character motion and consistency might still fail.
2. Compare against Z.ai **CogVideoX-5B-I2V**, a genuinely different image-to-video model. Its conservative standard model produces 720×480 at 8 fps, so it is a feasibility and motion comparison, **not automatically acceptable 24 fps final footage**. CogVideoX 1.5 is a higher-resolution candidate but its T4 fit, speed and temporal quality require a separate proof.
3. First benchmark a short running-shot slice, the hardest scene. Only if it passes continue to a full 3-second running shot and the close-up. If neither passes, stop instead of stretching or calling it finished.

No new GPU work or private-image upload is authorized by this report. Seek the user's explicit approval for the pilot.

## Project facts, not vendor claims

- Scene plan: rear walk 0–4 s; temple gate 4–9 s; aerial descent 9–13 s; side-view running 13–16 s; face turn, smile and red eyes 16–20 s.
- Current local sandbox has no NVIDIA GPU and about 3.8 GiB RAM. Previous private Kaggle runs used T4 GPUs reporting about 14.56 GiB usable each. Two cards do **not** automatically pool their memory; model parallelism is a distinct implementation. Availability, queue time, disk, host RAM and quota are not guaranteed.
- The latest StableAnimator v7 research generated 24 frames but its face became stylized; fingers, shoes and first-frame motion still failed. Repeating a successful process exit is not a quality pass. The later v4 portrait turn improved likeness but body/background remain static, mouth movement is imperfect and eye glow remains synthetic.
- Previous model pilot artifacts and informal face-embedding comparisons are **not proof of recognizable likeness**. Video and audio still need real human review.

## Proposed bounded experiment (requires approval)

1. **Privacy / resources preflight:** use the existing **AI-generated keyframe**, not the privately supplied original or face descriptors. Run on a private Kaggle notebook, which is still a third-party cloud environment, not local storage. Do not write image payloads, biometric data or tokens to logs. Keep test outputs out of the public GitHub repository; download them to the private workspace if supported. Do not create or upload recognizable 3D assets. No paid external provider or GPU rental without separate approval.
2. **Wan memory test:** pin an audited implementation/version with T4-capable FP16 and group/model offload. First use a small 25-frame diagnostic at an appropriate supported canvas. Record GPU, host RAM, disk, dtype, seed, timing, and failures. This only proves a one-second path; a full 72/73-frame test is a separate scale-up and may OOM. Avoid claiming the outside T2V/B200 numbers are our I2V results.
3. **Cog comparison:** if account quota and runtime permit, try the 5B-I2V standard checkpoint with FP16, sequential CPU offload and VAE slicing/tiling on a comparable generated running keyframe. Preserve its native 8 fps for assessment; frame interpolation is not a cure for pose errors. Keep it explicitly research-only pending weight license review.
4. **Acceptance gate:** inspect the complete candidate clip, sampled full frames and every hand/foot transition; check recognizable face/wardrobe against the private reference locally; assess continuous stride, contact, camera stability, artifacts and reasonable lighting. A descriptor can assist but cannot establish identity. The actual 24 fps film must have usable true motion; merely upscaling, slowing or interpolating a defective clip is not a pass.
5. Only if a running candidate passes, apply the winning approach to the remaining scene lengths, then perform continuity/color/motion review, verify final VFX and retime/listen to audio. Clear each model, driver and audio license for the intended distribution. Do not publish a full film until it actually meets the brief. If both tests fail, report failure and reassess larger compatible GPU or consented provider; no unapproved purchase or upload.

**Expected time/cost:** no paid service is planned. T4 allocation, kernel setup, model downloads and queue may take a long time or be unavailable. One cited comparison used 25 frames, not a full shot; another T4 notebook reports more than an hour per Cog render. There is no reliable project-specific completion time or guarantee until our own pilot runs.
