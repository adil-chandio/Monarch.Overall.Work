# One-to-All running pilot — frame-by-frame QA

**Verdict: research result only; not acceptable as a film shot.** The approved private T4 experiment technically completed. All 49 decoded frames were inspected in seven sequential contact sheets, with enlarged foot crops at frames 0, 1, 4, 5, 8, 12, 13, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26, 29, 33, 37, 40, 44 and 48. These are visual checks of decoded frames, not a claim of having heard the film or reviewed real-time playback. The file has no audio.

## What succeeded

- `onetoall-running-pilot.mp4`: 49 frames, 16 fps, 480×832 portrait; encoded duration 3.0625 s. It shows a full-body man running from left to right in a black outfit. Body motion and some lateral background movement are present.
- At the human-review scale the gross silhouette follows a repeating run; it is not merely a static still or the prior 2.5D depth push. Relative to the previous v6 driver there is a visibly moving ground/background and more coat motion. This does **not** mean gait physics or identity passed.
- Observed on a Tesla T4. Sampling ran for about 4m52s for eight steps; the ComfyUI prompt took 448.10 s, and the whole submitted prompt 452.6 s. The wrapper logged **4.458 GB allocated and 5.312 GB reserved at the sampling stage**. These are its PyTorch sampling counters, not total GPU peak across ONNX, VAE and other processes, and not proof of fit at other resolutions or settings. VAE peak and host RAM were not recorded. Torch was 2.11.0+cu130. Configuration and SHA256s are in `onetoall-receipt.json`.
- No original user reference image was uploaded to Kaggle. The input image was the previously generated `shot-04-identity-study-b.jpg`; the motion input was the previously generated and flawed StableAnimator v6 clip. Both were fetched from an existing pinned public repository commit. Output was recovered from the completed private kernel; no duplicate GPU render was launched during recovery.

## Why it fails the brief

1. **Feet and contact:** shoes are dark, soft and change shape; e.g. frames 13 and 37 have a detached-looking dark spot near a trailing foot, and multiple footfalls blur into the surface. The floor texture moves relative to the feet, but the apparent contact shadows do not establish convincing, stable contact. The original driver had documented foot problems and this model did not reliably repair them.
2. **Hands and anatomy:** hands are mostly tiny blurred fists with little defined anatomy; sleeve and coat silhouettes change. At this scale there is no evidence of consistently natural fingers or a stable garment.
3. **Likeness:** a black-haired, bearded, suited character is maintained in broad terms, but the face is very small and differs in detail from the generated reference. A local visual check against the private original is insufficient to confirm recognizable identity. It must not be claimed as likeness-matched.
4. **Environment and continuity:** the courtyard turns into a simplified blue/purple colonnade with smeared floor and little recognizable temple architecture. Background movement is present but not a verified coherent tracked camera in the same temple as the other shots. Missing environmental geometry, reflections and mist do not match the requested continuity.
5. **Temporal delivery:** this is a 16 fps portrait research clip, not a finished 24 fps, landscape cinematic shot. Interpolation or cropping could introduce new artifacts and would not fix anatomy, likeness or lighting. There is no sound and no completed five-scene edit.

## Diagnosed input limitations

`pose-audit.jpg` confirms that the intended detector produced nonempty controls, but nonempty pixels are not a pose-accuracy score. The driver is itself a flawed AI-generated mocap rendering, not a clean real running performance; its errors and occlusions can be inherited by the new result. `warped-ref-audit.jpg` shows that `align_to=pose` enlarged and cropped the reference: the lower legs and both shoes are outside the warped reference image. This is a concrete reason the shoe appearance was not preserved; it is not proof that changing alignment alone will solve contact.

A better next research direction is to acquire a rights-cleared, unobscured side-view runner as a driver, check the first detected pose against the reference, and test `align_to=ref` or otherwise constrain the reference warp **before** spending GPU time. The exact result and fit are still unproven. There is no justification for random-seed retries, upscaling this test, calling it final, or proceeding to full production until these input issues are resolved and the user approves the next scope.

One candidate source is [ASPset-510](https://github.com/anibali/aspset-510/tree/b4f6a70589ebd47abdf0e48d1ff90e8495bb0be5), a dataset of sports actions which its creators dedicate to CC0. Its [Internet Archive metadata](https://archive.org/metadata/aspset510) lists a 1.39 GB test-video archive and a 23.72 GB train/validation-video archive; the local sandbox cannot reach Internet Archive directly. We have **not** inspected the actual clips or established that a suitably framed run is present, or that the remote private runtime can retrieve one. Dataset CC0 does not by itself settle personality/privacy rights; use an unidentifiable pose guide, assess releases/terms, and don't place the original third-party athlete in the final mafia scene. Another [side-view stock candidate](https://www.pexels.com/video/side-view-of-a-man-running-at-a-track-8533913/) has not been downloaded or visually assessed; [Pexels rules](https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042332714-What-are-the-rules-for-using-Pexels-photos-or-videos) restrict portraying identifiable people in criminal or offensive contexts, so it is not a production-cleared source. A user-supplied performance with permission is another path.

## Receipt correction

The historical receipt says `native_bf16: true`, but the kernel called `torch.cuda.is_bf16_supported()` with its default `including_emulation=True`. PyTorch 2.11's implementation can return true via emulation on pre-Ampere GPUs; that is **not** evidence of native BF16 execution on the T4. The actual pilot requested FP16 for the text encoder, VAE and transformer. The kernel has been corrected to record compute capability and explicitly query `including_emulation=False` for future runs; the original receipt is retained unchanged as an audit record. [PyTorch 2.11 source](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/v2.11.0/torch/cuda/__init__.py).
