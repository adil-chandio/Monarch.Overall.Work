### 04. `2:10-3:30` — Why water becomes a sphere

**VO:** On Earth, gravity pulls a drop down and flattens it. In microgravity that pull is gone, so the surface of the water pulls on itself instead. NASA describes this as surface tension, the elastic-like force that gives droplets their shape. Left alone, the drop settles into the shape with the least surface: a sphere. So the first strange thing in this clip is not the face. It is that the water is round at all. Our own simple diagram here, drawn for this video, not stock footage.

**On-screen:** `ORIGINAL DIAGRAM: surface tension → sphere · label 'Diagram: Replay Lab'`

**Sources:** [3](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/astrophysics/what-is-the-fluidic-telescope/) [14](workspace://config/channels.json) · **~79 words**

### 05. `3:30-5:00` — The liquid lens

**VO:** Now the flip. A curved boundary between air and water bends light. Light crossing that boundary is refracted, and the curved surface sends the rays into a reversed arrangement, so the picture arrives upside down. The same bending makes rainbows in raindrops. So the sphere is behaving like a small optical element - a liquid lens. That is why the opening line said it is not a lens: there is no glass, no manufactured optic. The water is doing the job. Freeze here, at twenty-three seconds, and watch it at half speed.

**On-screen:** `REFRACTION · air → water → image inverts · replay 0.5x at 23.42s · further reading: NASA image article (on screen source line)`

**Sources:** [2](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/bubbly-chiao/) [3](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/astrophysics/what-is-the-fluidic-telescope/) [10](workspace://production/monarch-cam/caught_on_camera_commentary_v10/review/final/edit_ledger_v10.json) · **~92 words**

That gap is a pattern interrupt, and it is the reason people replay the moment. We are not going to turn that surprise into a claim about how the footage was made, or into a story about a broadcast error. A separate fact-check has documented how a different clip was misused for staged claims; we will not repeat that pattern here.

## 3. Hard guardrails

- One NASA clip only; credit 'Source: NASA / Artemis II' + 'no endorsement implied' [4](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/).
- No 'No CGI', '100% real', AI-free claim, invented date, injury, outcome or view-count claim.
- No 'official NASA', NASA insignia, co-branding, astronaut names used promotionally [5](https://www.nasa.gov/collaborating-with-nasa/strategic-partnerships/).
- VO must never say 'according to NASA' or imply NASA reviewed/approved this analysis - NASA SI guidance prohibits that form of attribution [4](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/). Factual source disclosure ('Source: NASA / Artemis II') is retained instead.
- NASA native audio retained; no music, SFX, procedural bed, audio substitution or time-stretch [9](workspace://production/monarch-cam/caught_on_camera_commentary_v10/review/final/qc_summary.json).
- No stock b-roll as hero, no reaction-only footage, no clickbait without payoff [14](workspace://config/channels.json).
- No unrelated/misleading tags, engagement coercion, guaranteed virality/retention/views/RPM.
- AI-use toggle, visibility, audience and license are human decisions [7](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en).
- technical_pass stays separate from human_creative_approval / human_legal_review (both false) [9](workspace://production/monarch-cam/caught_on_camera_commentary_v10/review/final/qc_summary.json).
