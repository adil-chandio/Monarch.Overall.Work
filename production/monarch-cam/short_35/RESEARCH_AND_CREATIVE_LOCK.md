## 1. Evidence used

### Monarch Cam demand evidence

- The channel's original bear encounter video was still growing in the live audit: **959,920 public views**, +6,945 over the previous supplied snapshot.
- A recent animal Short reached **5.8K views / 126 likes**, proving that recent Shorts distribution is not universally absent.
- The weakest recent uploads used generic rankings, delayed payoffs, mixed niches, generic “wait for it” language or unclear rights. This Short stays with one real bear/human-space incident and one behavioural answer.

### Public viral-pattern evidence

A bear casually opening a car and climbing inside was reported at roughly **25 million Instagram views** in November 2024. The useful pattern is the instantly readable, humanlike physical action—not the unrelated clip, unsupported “he has done this before” caption or casual key jokes. I use only the cleared NPS incident and do not copy that clip's unverified history claim.

Research references:

### Factual evidence

- NPS says black bears can identify food by smell and appearance and that bears fed human food can associate campsites, bags, garbage cans and **cars** with food: https://nps.gov/subjects/bears/black-bears.htm/index.htm?amp=1
- NPS says feeding a bear even once can lead to food conditioning and seeking people for food: https://www.nps.gov/grsm/learn/news/great-smoky-mountains-national-park-reminds-visitors-that-feeding-bears-is-illegal-and-dangerous.htm
- NPS describes a human food reward as capable of producing food conditioning: https://www.nps.gov/glac/learn/news/aggressive-food-conditioned-grizzly-euthanized-in-gnp.htm
- The previously checked CPW source supports repeated checking even when no food is currently inside. This script makes the weaker, directly supportable statement that the learned shortcut **can** remain when nothing is visible; it does not claim this particular bear's unrecorded history.

## 3. Retention architecture

The goal is coherent curiosity, not a literal hypnosis promise.

| Time | New information / visual | Narration function |
|---|---|---|
| **0.00–2.15** | Bear already halfway through the car window; no logo/fade-in | **“He didn't open the door.”** Immediate contradiction + visual proof |
| **2.15–4.35** | Upright bear places paws at the open window | **“The window was enough.”** First micro-payoff by 2.15 s |
| **4.35–7.25** | Bear inside the vehicle | **“But why climb into a stranger's car?”** Larger question opens after the small one resolves |
| **7.25–11.35** | Interior rummaging | **“Not for a ride. For whatever food he can find.”** Clear answer; light contrast without fake comedy |
| **11.35–16.25** | Close bear/vehicle action | **“If one search ends with food, the lesson can stick.”** Behaviour mechanism begins |
| **16.25–19.35** | Original animated **SEARCH → FOOD REWARD → CHECK AGAIN** diagram | **“Search. Reward. Repeat.”** Memorable compressed model; new visual texture |
| **19.35–25.20** | Entry and exit action | Names **food conditioning** and links the car to a place worth checking again |
| **25.20–30.35** | Bear approaching/investigating vehicles | Learned shortcut can remain even when nothing is visible; no empty-car claim |
| **30.35–35.00** | Return to opening exit angle; short closing hold | **“Don't teach the lesson. Secure food and lock up.”** Actionable payoff and loop back to the opening image |

### Deliberate pacing rules

- First meaningful frame is the action itself—no Monarch logo animation, establishing scenery or “wait for it.”
- Eleven source/edit beats in 35 seconds, but no mechanical zoom spam; each cut accompanies a new question, answer or model step.
- Phrase captions remain inside a central mobile-safe area and avoid the right-side Shorts UI.
- Music and SFX support reveals but never imply a handle pull, attack, bear vocalisation or unseen food reward.
- The diagram gives original explanatory value rather than simply cropping/replaying source footage.

## 4. Visual/source lock

All footage comes from the explicitly public-domain NPS **Bears in Cars** asset:

- Rights page: https://www.nps.gov/media/video/view.htm?id=07B1F1A5-E8DC-0BCE-DAFAE3F9B356AA73
- Declaration: “Stock footage of bears in cars in Yosemite National Park. All footage is in the public domain and may be used for any purpose.”
- Primary reviewed windows: approximately **14–23 s**, **65 s**, **316 s**, **338–342 s**, and **352–357 s** in the source.
- The first frame uses the later exit action around **338 s**, not the weaker empty-car opening at 0 s.

The source is low-resolution archival night footage. The vertical design uses a brightened, mild-sharpened 4:3 action window over a dark blurred extension instead of an extreme low-quality crop that would remove the vehicle context.
