# Fact check: *This ship is about to climb a mountain* (Gatun Locks, 47 s)

Every factual claim in the narration, mapped to a source. Sources were read on 2026-11-05.

## Sources

1. **Panama Canal Authority — *Design of the Locks***
   <https://pancanal.com/en/design-of-the-locks/> (official operator's own description).
2. Lock dimensions, the three-step Gatun flight, the 85 ft lift, the gravity-only fill and the
   miter-gate mechanism are all taken from source 1; nothing in the script comes from a
   secondary summary.

## Claim-by-claim

| # | Narration | Verdict | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "This ship is about to climb a mountain" | figurative | Gatun Locks raise ships 85 ft (25.9 m) from the Atlantic to Gatun Lake. Not a literal mountain; the flight steps up the hillside the canal was cut through. |
| 2 | "No crane. No elevator. Just water." | correct | Ships are not lifted. The chamber is filled and the ship floats up with the water. |
| 3 | "At Gatun three pairs of chambers climb eighty-five feet" | correct | Gatun is a three-step flight. Each step is a *pair* of parallel chambers (two lanes), i.e. six chambers in the flight; the lift of the whole flight is 85 ft. |
| 4 | "Steel gates seal the ship inside" | correct | Miter gates close behind the ship to seal the chamber. |
| 5 | "the water pours down through tunnels inside the walls" | correct | The main culverts run longitudinally inside the lock walls (18 ft diameter). Water flows by gravity from the higher chamber/lake level. |
| 6 | "rises through a hundred holes in the floor" | correct | Per chamber: 20 cross culverts under the floor, each with 5 openings = 100 floor openings. |
| 7 | "The ship floats up with it. Nothing lifts the ship. Buoyancy does." | correct | The ship rises because the water it displaces rises with the chamber level. |
| 8 | "When both sides reach the same level the gates swing open and it moves to the next step" | correct | The miter gates can only open when the water on both faces is level (that is why they are miter gates: water pressure holds them shut). |
| 9 | "Three steps. Eighty-five feet. All the way to Gatun Lake." | correct | The Gatun flight is the three-step ascent to the lake. |
| 10 | "Gravity moves every drop." | correct | No pumps anywhere in the lockage. Water moves only because of the head between levels. |
| 11 | "Electric motors only open gates and valves." | correct | The gate and valve machinery is electrically driven (the original 1914 installation was electric). The towing locomotives are also electric. |
| 12 | "No pumps. No engines." | correct for the lockage | No pumps lift water for the lockage, and no engines lift the ship. (Ships themselves, and the mules' electric motors, obviously exist; the line is about what lifts the *water/ship*.) |
| 13 | "Engineering hiding in plain sight." | rhetorical | — |

## Deliberate simplifications (disclosed, not hidden)

- The 3-D model is **stylised**: it is not to scale in every dimension, the chambers are
  shortened to fit a vertical frame, and the terrain is generalised.
- The **near lock wall is sectioned** (cut down to a 1.15 m kerb) so the camera can see into
  the chamber. This is the standard cutaway convention for engineering explainers; the real
  wall is a solid mass roughly 11 m above the chamber floor.
- Only **one lane** of the pair is shown. The flight is described as "three pairs", but
  modelling both lanes would halve the screen width available for the mechanism.
- The **ship is a generic container ship**, not a specific vessel; it is representative, and
  the container colours are illustrative.
- Water is a height-field shader, not a fluid simulation. Fill times are compressed to the
  47 s edit (a real lockage takes several minutes).

## Not claimed

- No claim that the shot list matches any specific real-world camera position.
- No claim about the current condition, maintenance state or traffic statistics of the locks.
- No claim about the 2016 neopanamax expansion locks; this piece is about the **original**
  Gatun Locks only.
