Start with the bears, because the bears were there first.
Thousands of bear skeletons have come out of single cave systems. Not because bears were killed there — because they went there to sleep, and some never woke up. 
Now put a human family on that landscape. It's winter. You need shelter, and in limestone country shelter means one thing.
 The same cave. The same warm, dry, dark cave that something else is currently asleep in.
At Chauvet, where we started, humans and bears both used it for thousands of years — but not at the same time. One, then the other. The room changed hands.
At other sites it's the opposite. In Italy, in two caves called Rio Secco and Fumane, Neanderthals left more than seventeen hundred bear bones, with cut marks and stone tools among them.  Same cave. Same layers. And they were not waiting for the bear to leave.
